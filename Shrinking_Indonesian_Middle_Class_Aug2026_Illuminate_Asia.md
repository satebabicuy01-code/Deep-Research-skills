## Indonesia's Shrinking Middle Class Demands a Pivot to 'Value with Dignity'

*Navigating the consumer retreat, the 'makan tabungan' economy, and the new rules of mass-market strategy.*

---

## Part 1: The Macro Landscape

### The Indonesian Middle Is Structurally Hollowing Out

Three forces define the consumer landscape brands must now navigate:

#### 01 — MACRO SHIFT: A Demographic Landslide
10.6M people fell out of the middle class since 2019. The share dropped from **21.45% to 16.6%** — the hourglass is real.

#### 02 — CONSUMER SQUEEZE: Broke but Bougie
Stagnant wages meet rising aspiration. Households eat savings and lean on Buy-Now-Pay-Later to defend their lifestyle.

#### 03 — STRATEGIC MANDATE: Value With Dignity
Winning means low entry price without shame — aspirational branding, high frequency and proximity at unbeatable unit economics.

> *Each force compounds the next — this is a one-way ratchet, not a temporary dip.*

---

## Part 2: The Consumer Psychology

### The "Broke but Bougie" Tension Map

*Shrinking capacity and rising aspiration pull the consumer in opposite directions*

|---|---|

| Social-media lifestyles · Premium cravings · Status signaling | Stagnant wages · "Makan Tabungan" (eating savings) · Thinning formal jobs |

> **The consumer refuses to trade down in identity — even as the budget forces a trade-down in spend.**

---

### 'Makan Tabungan': Consumers Are Burning Savings and Running on Digital Debt

*Lifestyles are increasingly sustained by structural leverage, creating a fragile consumption floor*

#### The Exhaustion of Savings
- Average balances plummeted **-40%** in sub-Rp100M accounts (2019–2024)
- Savings-to-income ratio fell from **16.7% to 14.7%** in a single year
- Food share of spending spiked to **41.3%**, destroying discretionary buffers

#### The Rise of Leverage
- **Rp37–44 Trillion** (Buy Now, Pay Later) balances outstanding
- Online loans surged to **~Rp65 Trillion** (+25% YoY)
- **85% of Millennials** lack credit cards — driving BNPL despite NPLs at 2.77%

> 🧠 **Behavioural Insight — Present Bias:** Because 'Buy Now, Pay Later' lets people delay payment, spending feels painless — so shoppers keep buying on credit even as their own savings drop by 40%. The fix: show the real, full price clearly at checkout, before people decide to buy.

## Part 3: Industry by Industry

*The winners, the losers, and the undecided — with the behavioural lens behind each.*

---

### FMCG: The Sachet Economy Returns

Families are buying sachets and small packs to stretch a tight weekly budget — choosing a smaller amount to pay now even though it costs more per gram.

#### The Sachet Resurgence
- Lifebuoy still heavily promotes shampoo sachets
- Sunlight dishwashing liquid sachets remain a staple in warungs

#### The Squeezed Middle
- Leading cooking-oil brands saw their share slip from ~50% to 42%, as mid-priced products lose the middle-income shoppers they used to rely on
- Consumers increasingly switch from mid-tier brands like **Sania** or **Filma** to **Minyakita** or cheaper alternatives

#### Private Label Rising
- Shop-owned 'private label' products grew **5.6% in value** in 2024, more than double the 2.2% for branded goods
- Alfamart and Indomaret continue expanding house-brand groceries and personal care products

> 🧠 **Behavioural Insight — Scarcity Mindset:** When money is tight, our attention narrows to immediate costs instead of long-term value. Buyers choose sachets (*karena yang tunai terbatas*) even though the price per gram is higher.

---

### Food & Beverage: The Mixue Model and the Death of the Middle Menu

#### Affordable Little Luxuries
Chains like **Mixue** (Rp16,000 sundaes) and **Kopi Kenangan** (Rp20–35k, 800+ outlets, over USD200M) sell small indulgences priced as an affordable treat, not a big commitment.

#### The Emptied Middle
Mid-priced casual dining (Rp60,000–120,000) and ageing food-court chains are losing customers to cheaper meals below them and more tempting treats above them.

#### The Value Floor
Warungs, street-cart (*gerobak*) vendors and cheap rice-based meals are winning back volume as squeezed middle-class families trade down to them.

> 🧠 **Behavioural Insight — Affect Regulation:** Small affordable snacks are a reliable way for people to lift their mood (*mengangkat mood*). Because the spending feels reasonable, there is no guilt — and the purchase is justified.

---

### Case Study: Mie Gacoan Universe

**Strategic Playbook: Pair very low prices with clean, modern stores.**

#### 1. The Strategy: Affordable Without Looking Cheap
- **The Tension:** Families need lower prices but do not want a low-status experience
- **The Playbook:** Keep core items at **IDR 10,000–15,000** and serve them in bright, modern outlets

#### 2. Execution: One Model, Two Categories
- **Mie Gacoan (Noodles):** Makes low-cost noodles feel social and shareable through large, lively stores
- **Dikichi (Fried Chicken):** Brings street-priced fried chicken into clean outlets with strong branding and packaging

#### 3. The Shift: A Better Trade-Down
- **From Global Chains:** As budgets tighten, families spend less at KFC and McDonald's
- **The Value:** Dikichi offers a familiar fast-food night out at a much lower price

---

### Retail: Malls Under Pressure, Value Formats Up

#### Smart-Value Retail
**Miniso** (Rp29,900–99,900, with popular character tie-ins) and **Daiso**'s single fixed-price format are growing fast by feeling 'smart' rather than 'cheap.'

#### Department Stores Exposed
Department stores such as **Matahari** and **Ramayana** are closing outlets and losing revenue, while mid-tier malls sit too empty to sustain themselves.

#### Proximity Wins
With over **40,000 outlets**, Alfamart and Indomaret have become the go-to neighbourhood shops, backed by their own house brands and digital payments.

> 🧠 **Behavioural Insight — Smart-Shopper Signaling:** Landing a good deal makes us feel clever, not cheap. Hunting for unique items and discounts gives buyers satisfaction and pride. To win: package low prices as an achievement, not a sacrifice.

---

### Case Study: Lala Bundle — The Treasure Hunter's Paradise

**Strategic Playbook: Turn surplus stock into a fun treasure hunt.**

#### 1. The Strategy: Make Bargain Hunting Fun
- **The Tension:** Shoppers need low prices but still want discovery and pride in a good deal
- **The Playbook:** Prices fall each day (*TURUN HARGA SETIAP HARI*), creating urgency and making thrift feel like a game

#### 2. Execution: Build a Destination
- **Curated Stores:** Clean, organized outlets replace messy bargain bins
- **Community:** Auctions, events, and the "Tigers" community make deal hunting social
- **Online + Offline:** Stores and live-stream sales keep excitement high

#### 3. The Shift: Win Shoppers Trading Down
- **The New Safety Net:** Tight budgets push shoppers away from mid-tier malls
- **The Value:** Lala Bundle makes low-cost shopping feel clever, social, and shareable

### Property: The Affordability Crisis and the 14-Square-Metre Conversation

#### New Ownership Models
New ways to buy — renting with an option to buy later, group co-operative financing, purpose-built rentals, and credit scoring based on everyday data — are opening the market to aspiring middle-class buyers.

> **BTN Rent to Own:** A model allows consumers to move into a home as tenants while paying monthly rent. Part of these payments may later be credited toward purchasing the property, reducing the upfront financial burden.

#### The Squeezed-Out Middle
Indonesia is short **9.8–12.7 million homes**, yet the Rp8M/month income cap for subsidies shuts out the middle class, and subsidised housing was cut **24%** to 166,000 units.

> *The debate around 24 m² houses illustrates how affordability can conflict with consumers' expectations of a "proper home."*

#### Landed Still Wins
Buyers still treat a house on titled land as 'real' ownership, so homes that feel genuinely owned outsell tiny high-rise apartment units.

> 🧠 **Behavioural Insight — Psychological Ownership:** The more something feels 'ours,' the more we value it. That is why tiny 14 m² units fail to meet buyers' image of a proper home. To win: offer formats that still feel like full ownership.

---

### Case Study: Rumah Flat Menteng — The Co-Housing Cooperative

**Strategic Playbook: Use co-housing to keep middle-income families close to the city.**

#### 1. The Vision: Affordable City Living
- **The Tension:** High prices push middle-income workers to distant suburbs and long commutes
- **The Solution:** *Rumah Flat* zoning (Pergub RDTR 31/2022) allows compact multi-family housing in Jakarta

#### 2. The Engine: Cooperative Housing
- **Lower Cost:** Seven families fund and manage the project together, avoiding developer margins
- **Compact Build:** A four-story building on 275 m² provides seven homes near transit, plus shared spaces
- **Shared Ownership:** Residents hold a long-term collective lease and manage costs together

#### 3. The Shift: A Model for the "Missing Middle"
- **The Proof:** Professionals accept compact, shared living when it is affordable and well located
- **The Takeaway:** Banks and developers can support small co-housing projects and cooperative finance

### E-commerce: Social Commerce Becomes the Default

#### Social Commerce Surges
**TikTok Shop** sold about **USD 6.2 billion** of goods here — its second-biggest market in the world — and is expected to reach **USD 22 billion by 2028**.

#### Platform & Margin Risk
Resellers competing on price drag brand prices down, and the **August 2025 livestream ban** exposed the risk of relying on a single sales channel.

#### Live Shopping Converts
In 2024, **60% of Indonesians** bought something through live-stream shopping, which turns viewers into buyers up to **3x more often** than ordinary product pages.

> 🧠 **Behavioural Insight — Scarcity Cues & Social Proof:** Countdown timers and live demonstrations make offers feel urgent (*mendesak*), while seeing others buy builds trust (*kepercayaan*).

---

### Financial Services: BNPL Booms While Insurance Stagnates

#### The BNPL Explosion
'Buy Now, Pay Later' balances reached **Rp37.44 trillion** across **32.47 million accounts**, filling the gap for the **85% of millennials** who have no credit card.

**Leading BNPL providers:**
- **Shopee SPayLater** — one of the most widely adopted BNPL services for e-commerce
- **GoPay Later** — used for food delivery, transport, and everyday purchases

#### Insurance Stagnates
Insurance spending stays below **3% of GDP**, the lowest in Southeast Asia, because a shrinking middle class has less spare income to pay premiums. Insurance remains concentrated among employer-provided benefits or higher-income households.

#### Small, Bundled & Islamic Cover
Low-cost cover is growing fast — tiny policies, insurance built into other purchases, and sharia-compliant 'takaful' (Islamic assets up **26.4%**). A **Rp5,000/month** add-on outsells a **Rp200,000** standalone plan.

- **Prudential Syariah** leads the syariah insurance market with **26% market share**
- **Traveloka** offers travel insurance as an optional add-on during booking

> 🧠 **Behavioural Insight — Present Bias:** People avoid spending today to prevent losses that feel far away and unlikely, so they tend to skip protection like insurance. To win: make protection affordable, automatic, and bundled into products they already buy.

### The Behavioural Lens: Why the Squeezed Consumer Spends This Way

*Six simple ideas from behavioural science explain the spending patterns — and each points to a clear action for marketers.*

#### 1. Mental Accounting
People keep small treats in a separate mental "pocket" from big bills like rent, so little luxuries survive even when money is tight.

> **Marketer move:** Protect that "treat" budget by giving people permission to enjoy it, instead of making them feel guilty.

#### 2. Scarcity Mindset
When cash is tight, people focus only on getting through this week, so they will pay more per gram if the amount they pay today is smaller.

> **Marketer move:** Sell the smallest amount people can pay today, such as a sachet, not the lowest price per unit.

#### 3. Present Bias
People give much more weight to today than to tomorrow, which drives spending down savings ("makan tabungan") and Buy Now, Pay Later.

> **Marketer move:** Reward small actions people take today, and make protection such as insurance automatic.

#### 4. The Pain of Paying
Paying by card, paying later, or paying with one tap hides the feeling of money leaving, so people spend more easily.

> **Marketer move:** Make paying quick and easy, but still show the real cost of using credit.

#### 5. The "Lipstick Effect"
Even under financial stress, people keep buying small affordable treats to lift their mood and feel like themselves.

> **Marketer move:** Present the affordable treat as a well-deserved emotional reward, not an unnecessary extra.

#### 6. Loss Aversion & Framing
Switching to a cheaper option feels like losing status, but the same choice framed as a smart buy feels like a win.

> **Marketer move:** Offer "value with dignity" by presenting the cheaper choice as the clever, savvy option.
