# Indonesia's Fiscal, Monetary and Institutional State Footprint under Prabowo (Oct 2024 – Oct 2026)

Notes compiled 30 Oct 2026. "A" means actual or realized. "B" means budget, target or forecast. Every figure carries its date. Where sources conflict, both are given.

## 1. Size of the state: spending, revenue, tax ratio, deficit vs 3% cap, debt, interest burden, peer comparison

### Takeaway
By spending, Indonesia's state is small. General government expenditure is about 16–17% of GDP (IMF, 2024–25), against roughly 22–23% in Malaysia, Thailand and Vietnam and 43% on average in the OECD. The tax ratio is among the lowest of the large EMs, around 9–10% of GDP. The deficit has run close to the 3% cap: 2.92% in 2025 (provisional; 2.81% audited), 2.85% for 2026 (outlook) and 2.40% for 2027 (draft budget). Interest payments now absorb nearly the whole deficit. On aggregate size, the data do not support a "socialist big state" thesis. What has changed is how money moves: quasi-fiscal channels (Danantara, state banks, BI bond purchases) have grown.

### Cited Findings

An older list gives Malaysia 22.7%, Philippines 21.8%, Indonesia 16.6% — [Economics Help](https://www.economicshelp.org/blog/158853/economics/list-of-government-spending-as-a-of-gdp/)

- **2027 RAPBN (B, Nota Keuangan, Aug 2026):** spending Rp4,097.2tn, revenue Rp3,426.0tn, deficit Rp671.2tn = 2.40% of GDP. The DPR approved further deliberation of the RAPBN 2027 — [ANTARA](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5699691/dpr-ri-menyetujui-pembahasan-lanjutan-rapbn-2027)
- **Interest burden:** Universitas Andalas estimates 2027 interest at about 2.33% of GDP, so the 2.40% deficit is "arithmetically almost equal to interest payments" and the primary balance is near zero — [Unand analysis](https://www.unand.ac.id/berita/kepakaran/2042-analisis-rapbn-defisit-anggaran-beban-utang-dan-ketahanan-fiskal.html)
- Moody's (Feb 2026) cited the "narrow revenue base" as a constraint on fiscal flexibility — [Jakarta Post, 6 Feb 2026](https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2026/02/06/indonesian-markets-slide-again-after-moodys-cuts-outlook)

### Inferences
- With expenditure around 16% of GDP and tax around 9–10%, the Indonesian state is fiscally small. It is smaller than Malaysia, Thailand and Vietnam, and well under half the OECD average. The fiscal aggregates contradict a "socialist big-state" reading. Any "state control" story has to rest on SOEs, Danantara, regulation and directed credit rather than on budget size.
- Revenue is the binding constraint. If interest takes about 2.3% of GDP against revenue of about 12–14% of GDP, interest/revenue is roughly 17–20% (my derivation, not sourced). That leaves little room for an expanding state inside the 3% cap.
- The deficit has hugged the cap: 2.92% (2025, provisional), 2.85% (2026 outlook), 2.40% (2027 plan). The rule is binding and has so far held.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a sourced, consistent annual series for 2014–2024 (expenditure, revenue, tax ratio, deficit, debt/GDP). The IMF WEO database (Oct 2026 vintage) and Kemenkeu LKPP should be pulled directly. Training-data recollection, unverified: the tax ratio was about 10–11% in the mid-2010s, fell to about 8.3% in 2020, and was about 10.1–10.3% in 2022–23. Debt/GDP was about 24–30% in 2014–19, peaked near 40–41% in 2021, and was about 39% in 2023.
- I found no same-definition IMF EM-average figure. IMF Fiscal Monitor EM average expenditure, widely cited at about 30%+ of GDP, needs verification.
- I found no official interest/revenue ratio for 2025–2027. The Nota Keuangan 2027 gives interest payments (about Rp600tn+ is often cited), but this was not verified here.

## 2. Flagship programs: MBG, Koperasi Merah Putih, 3 million houses, efficiency cuts, transfers to regions

### Takeaway
The flagship programs are large in rupiah but modest against GDP. MBG is the single largest agency budget: Rp268tn in APBN 2026, cut to Rp240.2tn in RAPBN 2027. Koperasi Merah Putih is financed mainly off-budget through state-bank (Himbara) loans, repaid out of village funds, which makes it a quasi-fiscal channel. The programs were funded largely by reallocation: Inpres 1/2025 freed about Rp306tn, and transfers to regions (TKD) were cut about 25% in 2026. This is redistribution within a small budget, not expansion of the state's overall size.

### Cited Findings
- **MBG 2027 (B):** BGN budget Rp240,201.5bn (Rp240.2tn) for 72.1 million beneficiaries. That is 10.4% below the Rp268tn in APBN 2026, and BGN remains the largest ministry or agency budget — [CNN Indonesia, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.cnnindonesia.com/ekonomi/20260818220905-532-1393785/anggaran-mbg-rp2402-t-di-rapbn-2027-sasar-721-juta-penerima); [GoodStats](https://data.goodstats.id/statistic/rapbn-2027-belanja-bgn-turun-jadi-rp240-t-ImAH7). Sources conflict: PDIP cited Rp242tn; one outlet gives a 2026 baseline revised to Rp229tn and beneficiary targets of 63m→60.6m; a BPKP audit flagged suspected waste of Rp10.5tn per year — [Media Indonesia](https://mediaindonesia.com/politik-dan-hukum/923076/dpr-ri-setujui-pembahasan-lanjutan-rapbn-2027-soroti-program-mbg)
- Fitch's March 2026 outlook cut explicitly flagged MBG and debt issues — [IDNFinancials](https://www.idnfinancials.com/news/61891)
- **Koperasi Desa/Kelurahan Merah Putih:** the target is about 80,000 cooperatives under Inpres 9/2025, which assigns funding to "APBN, APBD, APBDes and other legitimate sources."   - Loan size per cooperative: up to Rp3bn per a later report (Himbara total ceiling Rp240tn), or up to Rp5bn with tenors of up to 10 years per an earlier one — [Metro TV](https://www.metrotvnews.com/play/N6GCVrx8-kopdes-merah-putih-dapat-pinjaman-modal-rp3-miliar-sumbernya-dari-himbara); [Kompas.id](https://www.kompas.id/artikel/en-pendanaan-koperasi-desakelurahan-merah-putih-akan-memakai-pinjaman-himbara)

  - The first repayment installments are due from September 2026 — [Liputan6](https://www.liputan6.com/bisnis/read/8254447/cicilan-koperasi-merah-putih-ke-himbara-dibayar-mulai-september-2026)
- **Inpres 1/2025 efficiency cuts (A, early 2025):** ministry and agency budgets cut by Rp256.1tn and TKD by Rp50.59tn. The Kemenkeu Jan–Mar 2025 exercise created about Rp306.6tn of fiscal space "without touching mandatory spending and social protection" — [Wikipedia: Inpres 1/2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Instruction_Number_1_of_2025); [Media Keuangan (Kemenkeu)](https://mediakeuangan.kemenkeu.go.id/article/show/menata-belanja-apbn-2026). Efficiency continues into 2026 via PMK 56/2025 — [ANTARA](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/4846161/sri-mulyani-beri-sinyal-efisiensi-anggaran-berlanjut-di-2026)
- **Transfers to regions (B, RAPBN 2026):** TKD Rp650tn, down 24.8% from the 2025 projection of Rp864.1tn (another source gives a 2025 allocation of Rp848.52tn). KPPOD warns regions may raise local taxes in response — [KPPOD](https://www.kppod.org/berita/view?id=1441)
- **Purbaya's Rp200tn placement (Sept 2025):** Rp200tn of government cash (about US$12bn) was moved from the government account at BI, which held about Rp425tn, into state banks to spur lending — [Wikipedia: August 2025 protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2025_Indonesian_protests); [business-indonesia.org](https://business-indonesia.org/news/indonesia-s-new-finance-minister-moves-idr-200-trillion-to-banks-signals-budget-continuity); [Taipei Times, 15 Sep 2025](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/world/archives/2025/09/15/2003843827)

### Inferences
- Centralization is happening through reallocation: from regions (TKD −25%) and line ministries toward presidential programs (MBG, cooperatives). It is not happening through a larger aggregate budget.
- Koperasi Merah Putih and the Rp200tn placement both run through state banks. That points to directed credit and quasi-fiscal activity, which the IMF explicitly asked to be limited (section 4). For the "state control" thesis, this off-budget channel matters more than the APBN headline.

### Gaps
- MBG 2025 (original about Rp71tn per training knowledge, later raised; realization unknown) was not verified with a 2026-dated source.
- 3 million houses program: I found no sourced budget or progress figures (FLPP/BP Tapera allocations, realization). Unresolved.
- I did not verify the final APBN 2026 TKD figure; the DPR may have raised it above Rp650tn during deliberation (training knowledge suggests about Rp693tn).
- I found no sourced data on Koperasi Merah Putih loan disbursement to date or on non-performing loans.

## 3. Finance Ministry turnover and the 3% / 60% fiscal rules

### Takeaway
Prabowo has had three finance ministers in under two years. Sri Mulyani was removed on 8 Sep 2025 after the August protests. Purbaya (pro-growth, liquidity-focused) followed, and was himself removed on 14 Sep 2026 in favor of technocrat Suahasil Nazara, widely read as a bid to restore investor confidence. Every minister has publicly held to the 3% cap. In September 2026, however, DPR Commission XI chair Misbakhun proposed loosening it in the State Finance Law revision (UU 17/2003). As of 30 Sep 2026 that proposal was still "under study," and no amendment had passed.

Purbaya Yudhi Sadewa, chairman of the Deposit Insurance Corporation (LPS), became finance minister — [Al Jazeera, 9 Sep 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/9/9/indonesian-president-prabowo-replaces-five-ministers-after-deadly-protests); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/indonesia/indonesian-leader-fires-ministers-finance-security-deadly-protests-rcna229996)

### Inferences
- The executive has repeatedly chosen credibility over expansion: it kept the cap, appointed a technocrat in September 2026, and planned a 2.40% deficit for 2027. Pressure to loosen comes from the governing coalition in the DPR (Gerindra's Misbakhun), not from the finance ministry. Investors should treat the State Finance Law revision as the key binary risk; it was unresolved as of October 2026.
- Ministerial churn (three ministers in about 24 months) is itself a predictability risk, and rating agencies cite it.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced October 2026 update on the State Finance Law revision or on whether the 60% debt cap is also on the table.
- No official statement explains Purbaya's dismissal.

## 4. Bank Indonesia: burden sharing, P2SK mandate change, independence, rates, rupiah, reserves

### Takeaway
Since 2025, BI's independence has eroded on several fronts. It resumed "burden sharing" with Rp200tn of SBN purchases in September 2025, outside any declared crisis, and bought Rp188.68tn of SBN in 2026 through 21 July. The June 2026 P2SK amendment added growth and job-creation mandates and gave the DPR power to evaluate BI. Prabowo's nephew, Thomas Djiwandono, was installed as deputy governor in February 2026. Market discipline has pushed back: the rupiah fell to record lows near Rp18,000/USD, and BI reversed from easing to tightening, with +100bp in 2026 to 5.75% plus a Deposit Facility hike to 5.00% in October 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Burden sharing:** BI resumed burden sharing in September 2025, buying Rp200tn of SBN with the finance ministry. Critics say this undermines independence, and the scheme is normally reserved for crises — [Tempo](https://en.tempo.co/amp/2045523/bank-indonesia-resumes-burden-sharing-scheme-buys-rp200tn-in-bonds); [Tempo: BI defends scheme](https://en.tempo.co/read/2045869/bank-indonesia-defends-rp200-trillion-burden-sharing-scheme-with-finance-ministry); [Indoneo](https://www.indoneo.com/capital/indonesia-central-bank-burden-sharing-deficit-breach/)
- **BI SBN purchases in 2026 (A, BI's own releases):** Rp86.16tn by 16 Mar (secondary Rp46.72tn); Rp140.57tn by 19 May (secondary Rp73.28tn); Rp188.68tn by 21 Jul (secondary Rp76.62tn, implying about Rp112tn in the primary market by derivation) — [BI release, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.bi.go.id/en/publikasi/ruang-media/news-release/Pages/sp_2814226.aspx). An unverified video source cites about Rp135tn by October.
- The July 2026 BI release says the KSSK helps finance the "Asta Cita" program and frames BI policy as "synergy" with government growth programs — [BI, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.bi.go.id/en/publikasi/ruang-media/news-release/Pages/sp_2814226.aspx)
- **IMF (Jan 2026)** recommended "gradually lowering Bank Indonesia's presence in the government debt market over the medium term" — [IMF PR 26/010](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/01/21/pr-26010-indnonesia-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consultation)
- **P2SK amendment:** the DPR passed the revision of Law 4/2023 on 4 Jun 2026. BI gains a mandate to support real-sector growth and job creation, and the DPR gains authority to evaluate BI and other financial regulators. The law also covers crypto and demutualization of the IDX. Misbakhun says it "does not disturb BI's independence." A Samuel Sekuritas analyst warns of inflation risk — [Beritasatu](https://www.beritasatu.com/multimedia/2999922/dpr-resmi-sahkan-revisi-uu-p2sk-jadi-undang-undang); [Katadata](https://katadata.co.id/finansial/keuangan/6a224f19939d5/revisi-uu-p2sk-sah-risiko-atas-independensi-bi-dan-kepercayaan-pasar-disorot); [Tempo: new BI mandate on jobs](https://www.tempo.co/ekonomi/uu-p2sk-baru-mandat-bi-lapangan-kerja-2253313). ## 5. Sovereign ratings and IMF assessment

### Takeaway
All agencies keep Indonesia at investment grade, but two of the three majors moved the outlook to negative in early 2026. Both cited governance, policy predictability and credibility, Danantara contingent liabilities, and BI's mandate change. Neither cited the size of the state. S&P (July 2026) stayed stable, citing fiscal discipline.

### Cited Findings
- **Moody's, 5 Feb 2026:** Baa2 affirmed, outlook stable→negative, citing "reduced predictability in policymaking," "signs of weakening governance," and uncertainty over Danantara's financing, governance and investment priorities as a source of contingent liabilities, plus a narrow revenue base. The action came a week after MSCI flagged transparency issues that triggered an equity rout — [Bisnis](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20260206/192/1950649/moodys-nilai-pembentukan-danantara-picu-ketidakpastian-pendanaan-tata-kelola); [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2026/02/06/indonesian-markets-slide-again-after-moodys-cuts-outlook); [BI note on Moody's action](https://www.bi.go.id/en/publikasi/ruang-media/news-release/Documents/SP_282726_rating-action-moodys.pdf). Danantara called it "a constructive reminder" — [Tiger/Reuters](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/2609957568)
- **Fitch, 3–4 Mar 2026:** BBB affirmed, outlook negative, citing "increasing policy uncertainty and erosion of Indonesia's policy mix consistency and credibility" and increasingly centralized decision-making. It warned that relaxing the 3% ceiling would weaken credibility and the ability to finance deficits "without support from the central bank," flagged risk from expanding BI's mandate to growth and jobs, and noted Danantara contingent liabilities and MBG — [BI-hosted Fitch release](https://www.bi.go.id/en/iru/economic-market-data/Documents/Fitch%20Revises%20Indonesia%27s%20Outlook%20to%20Negative%3b%20Affirms%20at%20%27BBB%27%20-%2003%20Mar%202026.pdf); [Indonesia Business Post](https://indonesiabusinesspost.com/6247/markets-and-finance/fitch-revises-indonesia-s-outlook-to-negative-amid-policy-uncertainty-and-fiscal-risks); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-04/fitch-lowers-indonesia-s-rating-outlook-to-negative)
- **S&P, 13 Jul 2026:** BBB affirmed, outlook stable, citing fiscal discipline and resource-revenue policies — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-13/idr-usd-s-p-affirms-indonesia-s-credit-rating-keeps-stable-outlook)
- **IMF 2025 Article IV (Board 14 Jan 2026, published 21 Jan 2026):** growth 5.0% in 2025 and 5.1% in 2026 (B). Directors "welcomed the authorities' commitment to prudent fiscal policies, anchored by credible fiscal rules." On Danantara, they "called for robust governance and accountability frameworks to prevent the buildup of contingent liabilities and quasi-fiscal activities." They urged the authorities to "limit below-the-line and quasi-fiscal activities to clarify the overall fiscal footprint," to target industrial policies at market failures "while minimizing trade and investment distortions," and to "accelerate deregulation" — [IMF PR 26/010](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/01/21/pr-26010-indnonesia-imf-executive-board-concludes-2025-article-iv-consultation); [IMF Country Report 26/11](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1idnea2026002-source-pdf.pdf)

### Inferences
- The rating agencies' concern is institutional (predictability, centralization, quasi-fiscal vehicles, central bank independence), not that the state is getting fiscally large. That matches the "state capitalism / centralized discretion" framing more than "socialism."

### Gaps
- No actions after July 2026 by Moody's or Fitch (including any reaction to the Suahasil appointment or the deficit-cap debate) were found. JCR and R&I actions in 2025–26 were not found. No 2026 Article IV had been published as of these searches.

## 6. Institutional checks: TNI law, DPR coalition, August 2025 protests, governance and freedom indices

### Takeaway

Heritage's economic-freedom score is flat at about 65, "moderately free," which points to no collapse in market freedom.

## 7. Ideological signals vs pro-market commitments

### Takeaway
Prabowo's rhetoric is explicitly anti-"neoliberal" and Article 33-centric. He coined "serakahnomics" ("greed-onomics") and invokes state control over "important branches of production" such as staple foods. At the same time he insists this is "not anti-capitalism." On the other side of the ledger are concrete pro-integration moves: OECD accession (roadmap May 2024, technical review from July 2026), the CPTPP accession process (launched June 2026), and EU-CEPA progress, alongside BRICS membership (Jan 2025). The mix is closer to nationalist developmental state-capitalism than to socialism.

### Cited Findings
- On 23 Jul 2025 (PKB anniversary), Prabowo said Article 33's family principle ("asas kekeluargaan, bukan konglomerasi") contradicts neoliberal economics and its acceptance of a few getting rich in the hope of trickle-down. He coined "serakahnomics," saying it is "not neoliberalism, not free markets, not capitalism" but greed. He called Article 33 his "senjata pamungkas" (ultimate weapon), citing Art. 33(2) on state control of important branches of production, and said staples such as rice, corn and cooking oil should not be left to market mechanisms — [Kompas, 24 Jul 2025](https://nasional.kompas.com/read/2025/07/24/07222321/prabowo-pasal-33-perekonomian-disusun-berdasar-asas-kekeluargaan-bukan); [Gerindra](https://gerindra.id/2025/07/24/prabowo-kritik-praktik-serakahnomics-perintahkan-tegakkan-kedaulatan-ekonomi-rakyat/); [ANTARA](https://www.antaranews.com/video/4987837/prabowo-sebut-praktik-serakahnomics-ancam-pasal-33-uud-1945)

### Inferences
- The rhetoric (Article 33, anti-"greed," food sovereignty) supports a thesis of state direction in staples and resources. The two tracks are in tension, and CPTPP's SOE chapter will be a concrete test.
