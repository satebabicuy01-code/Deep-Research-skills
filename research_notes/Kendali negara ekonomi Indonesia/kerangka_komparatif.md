# Comparative Framework: Classifying Indonesia's Political Economy (socialism vs state capitalism vs developmental state vs market economy)

Notes compiled 30 Oct 2026. Thesis to be graded: "Indonesia is moving toward a socialist / state-controlled economy where the state controls most strategic sectors." Every sourced claim is linked. Claims from standard literature that I could not re-verify this session are flagged as inferences or listed under Gaps.

---

## Q1. Definitions and measurable indicators: socialism vs state capitalism vs developmental state vs economic/resource nationalism

### Takeaway
The useful dividing line is not how big the state is. It is (a) who owns the means of production and whether private property is protected, (b) whether prices and allocation are set by plan or by markets, and (c) whether the state acts as owner/investor inside a market system. On every source found, Indonesia fits "state capitalism / developmentalism with resource nationalism" inside a market economy. It does not fit "socialism" (there is no abolition of private property, no central plan setting prices or quantities, and the private sector is still the majority of investment and output).

### Cited Findings
- **State capitalism (Bremmer, *The End of the Free Market*, 2010):** "a system in which governments use markets to create wealth and advance their political interests". The state is the principal actor and "state capitalists control the referees as well as the main players". Bremmer identifies state-capitalist countries by four policy tools: national oil companies, other SOEs, privately owned national champions, and sovereign wealth funds. — [Carnegie Council review](https://www.carnegiecouncil.org/publications/articles_papers_reports/0050); [NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/114663691). (The list of four tools is from my knowledge of the book; the search snippet only confirmed that "four specific policy tools" exist.)
- **State capitalism (Musacchio & Lazzarini, *Reinventing State Capitalism: Leviathan in Business, Brazil and Beyond*, 2014; HBS WP 12-108):** the defining feature is "governmental provision of capital to corporations – either equity or debt". There are three modes: "Leviathan as entrepreneur" (fully state-owned and state-managed SOEs), "Leviathan as a majority investor" (state-controlled listed firms), and "Leviathan as a minority investor" (minority stakes through development banks, pension funds and SWFs, plus subsidized lending). — [Harvard DASH working paper](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/handle/1/9056789/12-108.pdf?sequence=1); [SIOE 2013 paper](https://www.sioe.org/conference/2013/program/paper/1141-leviathan-evolving-varieties-of-state-capitalism-in-brazil-and-beyond)
- **Lardy's empirical test for China:** in *Markets over Mao* (2014), Lardy argued China "should not be regarded as state capitalist" because private firms got 52% of new lending in 2010 and 2012 against 32% for SOEs, and because SOEs' share of industrial output fell from about three-quarters in 1978. This shows that the metrics used to classify (share of credit, investment and output) can move the label even for China. — [Cato Journal review](https://cato.org/cato-journal/fall-2015/markets-over-mao-rise-private-business-china)
- **Kim's "varieties of state capitalism" applied to Indonesia:** Indonesia's current state capitalism differs from the Suharto-era version because it "is emerging alongside market liberalisation and in a democratic setting". — [Journal of ASEAN Studies (Kim, 2022)](https://journal.binus.ac.id/index.php/jas/article/download/9075/4647)
- **The OECD's measurement approach (Kowalski et al. 2013, Trade Policy Paper 147):** the "Country SOE Share (CSS)" is an equally weighted average of SOE shares of sales, assets and market value among a country's 10 largest firms, scored 0 to 100. The OECD says explicitly that "The CSS does not reflect the share of the state in the whole economy but among a country's top firms". This matters for the thesis: a high top-firm SOE share does not equal state control of the economy. — [OECD TPP 147 PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2013/04/state-owned-enterprises_g17a2290/5k4869ckqk7l-en.pdf)
- **IMF metric:** the IMF Fiscal Monitor (April 2020, Ch. 3 "State-Owned Enterprises: The Other Government") measures SOE weight as the SOE share of the world's 2,000 largest firms by a composite of revenue and assets (Capital IQ). It also finds SOEs about one-third less productive than private firms on average (sample of about 1 million firms in 109 countries). — [IMF blog, May 2020](https://www.imf.org/en/blogs/articles/2020/05/07/blog-state-owned-enterprises-in-the-time-of-covid-19); [FM Ch.3 exec summary](https://imf.org/-/media/Files/Publications/fiscal-monitor/2020/April/English/ch3execsum.ashx)
- **Prabowo explicitly rejects existing labels:** in his 15 Aug 2025 State of the Nation address he listed "liberal, neoliberal, free-market, socialist, or command economies" and said "This is different. I call it Serakahnomics" (the target is "greedy" cartels and rent-seekers, not private ownership as such). — [Setkab](https://setkab.go.id/en/serakahnomics-as-new-enemy-president-prabowo-calls-for-people-centered-economy/); [Yahoo/Reuters-type wire](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/indonesias-president-vows-tough-stance-100829994.html)

### Inferences
Below is a proposed scoring rubric for the report writer. The thresholds are my construction, based on the definitions above and standard comparative literature (Kornai's classical-socialism criteria; Johnson 1982 and Wade 1990 on the developmental state).

| Indicator | Socialism (classical) | State capitalism | Developmental state | Market economy w/ resource nationalism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership of means of production | Predominantly state/collective (SOE share of output >60-70%); private property abolished or marginal | Mixed. SOEs dominate commanding heights (top-10 firm CSS above ~50) but private firms are the majority of GDP | Private firms dominant; state steers through credit, licensing and pilot agencies (MITI/EPB) | Private dominant; state ownership concentrated in resources/utilities |
| Price formation | Administratively set for most goods | Market prices with selective controls (fuel, food, utilities) | Market prices, plus targeted subsidies/credit | Market prices, a few administered staples |
| Planning | Binding central plan with output quotas | Indicative plans; SOEs/SWFs as policy tools | Indicative plans plus export/performance discipline | Indicative or none |
| Capital allocation | State bank monopoly | State banks/SWF large but coexist with private banks and capital markets | Directed credit tied to performance | Market-based |
| Private property and FDI | Expropriation; FDI restricted | Property protected but politicized; FDI welcome with local-content/JV conditions | FDI often restricted; domestic champions | FDI welcome; resource-sector conditions |
| Political goal of the state | Abolish capitalism | Use markets for political/state ends (Bremmer) | Growth and catch-up via industrial upgrading | Capture of rents for the nation |

- On the evidence below, Indonesia scores "state capitalism / resource nationalism" on ownership of top firms and resources. It scores "market economy" on prices outside fuel/food/power, on private-majority investment (domestic private plus FDI) and on property rights. It scores "socialist" on none of the defining criteria. Prabowo's rhetoric (Article 33, "ekonomi kerakyatan", anti-"serakahnomics") borrows communitarian language, but it targets rent-seeking and leakages rather than private ownership per se.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the primary texts of Johnson (*MITI and the Japanese Miracle*, 1982), Wade (*Governing the Market*, 1990), Kornai (*The Socialist System*, 1992) or Kurlantzick (*State Capitalism*, 2016) this session, so their definitions above come from background knowledge and should be checked before quoting.
- I found no single comprehensive index that scores countries on "socialism vs state capitalism". The rubric thresholds are analytical judgment, not a published standard.

## Q2. Benchmarks with numbers: SOE weight in Indonesia vs peers

### Takeaway
Indonesia has long been one of the most SOE-heavy economies among large firms. In 2013 the OECD ranked it 4th of 38 countries on the Country SOE Share (69.2, behind China 95.9, UAE 88.4 and Russia 81.1, just ahead of Malaysia 68). This is a structural feature dating back decades, not a new Prabowo-era shift. But Indonesia's SOE footprint relative to GNI was small among the BRIICS (SOE sales of about 3% of GNI and assets of 19%, against China's 26% and 145%). On the whole-economy measure, Indonesia is far below China or Vietnam.

### Cited Findings
**Kowalski, Büge, Sztajerowska & Egeland (OECD Trade Policy Paper No. 147, April 2013), Forbes Global 2000, business year 2010-11** — [OECD PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2013/04/state-owned-enterprises_g17a2290/5k4869ckqk7l-en.pdf)
- Country SOE Share (top-10 firms, 0-100): **China 95.9; UAE 88.4; Russia 81.1; Indonesia 69.2; Malaysia 68.0; Saudi Arabia 66.8; India 58.9; Brazil 49.9; Norway 47.7; Thailand 37.3**. The only non-zero OECD members were Norway, France 16.7, Ireland 15.9, Greece 15.2, Finland 13.1, Korea 9.7, Belgium 8.1, Sweden 8, Austria 7 and Turkey 2.8. South Africa scored 2.8. 21 of 38 countries had a CSS above zero.
- Of the Forbes Global 2000, 204 were majority SOEs: China 70, India 30, Russia 9, UAE 9, Malaysia 8. SOEs accounted for more than 10% of aggregate sales of the 2000 and 11% of global market cap (USD 4.9tn).
- Annex Table A1.2, SOE share of each country's Forbes 2000 firms: **Indonesia 6 of 10 = 60%**; China 70/117 = 60%; India 30/57 = 53%; Russia 9/23 = 39%; Brazil 7/37 = 19%; South Africa 1/16 = 6%; OECD total 41/1500 = 3%; Turkey 1/10; US 3/543; UK 1/93.
- Table 4, SOE (Forbes 2000) sales / assets / market value as % of GNI (2010-11): **Indonesia 3% / 19% / 12%**; China 26% / 145% / 44%; India 16% / 75% / 22%; Russia 16% / 64% / 28%; Brazil 12% / 51% / 18%; South Africa 2% / 3% / 1%.
- On Indonesia specifically, the paper says privatisation and commercialisation "rendered most SOEs profitable, which together with constitutional guarantees for state involvement in some sectors and public demands for price administration of basic goods has reduced incentives for further privatisation". It also notes that in both Brazil and Indonesia "state control of strategic sectors and natural monopolies are constitutionally protected" (the Indonesian 1945 Constitution "has been used as an argument for state control of companies").
- The same paper says privatisation momentum in the BRIICS "slowed, if not halted or ... reversed" in the 2000s, with China ceasing privatisation in the mid-2000s and Russia reversing it in 2004-08.

**Global trend data**
- IMF Fiscal Monitor April 2020: the SOE share of the world's 2,000 largest firms "doubled to 20 percent", with assets of about USD 45 trillion (about 50% of global GDP), driven by emerging-market SOEs. The blog says the doubling took two decades; the chapter executive summary says a decade. — [IMF blog](https://www.imf.org/en/blogs/articles/2020/05/07/blog-state-owned-enterprises-in-the-time-of-covid-19); [FM ch3 summary](https://imf.org/-/media/Files/Publications/fiscal-monitor/2020/April/English/ch3execsum.ashx)
- OECD (2016) *State-Owned Enterprises as Global Competitors: A Challenge or an Opportunity?*: "an estimated 22% of the world's largest firms are now effectively under state control, this is the highest percentage in decades". OECD presentations describe this as 22% of the world's largest 100 firms. A related OECD calculation shows SOEs among the largest world companies rising from 9.8% to 22.8% between 2005 and 2014. — [OECD publication page](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/state-owned-enterprises-as-global-competitors_9789264262096-en.html); [Capobianco presentation](https://kt.gov.lt/uploads/documents/files/Vilnius%20-%20CAPOBIANCO%20-%20Competitive%20Neutrality%20-%20FINAL.pdf)
- A 2018 OECD review reportedly said the presence of Indonesian SOEs across the economy is more extensive than in any other country it monitors except China. — [New Straits Times via PressReader, 11 Oct 2018](https://www.pressreader.com/malaysia/new-straits-times/20181011/281981788545238) (secondary report; primary OECD text not retrieved)
- The OECD Product Market Regulation working paper on Indonesia (WP No. 1741, Dec 2022) says Indonesia's PMR "is less conducive to competition than in most OECD countries". It lists SOE governance among the areas with the most room for improvement, alongside network-sector barriers, command-and-control regulation and public procurement. — [OECD PMR Indonesia](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/product-market-regulation-in-indonesia_016eea51-en.html)

**Whole-economy SOE shares (peers)**
- **China:** the World Bank estimates SOEs at **23-28% of GDP** and **5-16% of employment** (2017). — [World Bank working paper](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/449701565248091726/how-much-do-state-owned-enterprises-contribute-to-china-s-gdp-and-employment)
- **Vietnam:** the GSO-based state-owned sector share of GDP was about **21% in 2023, down from 24.2% in 2010**. Government-linked reporting gives "more than 29%" on a broader definition. Politburo Resolution 79 (Jan 2026) reaffirms that the "state economic sector plays leading role". Resolution 68-NQ/TW (May 2025) targets a private-sector share of **50-58% of GDP by 2030**. — [Baochinhphu, Resolution 79](https://en.baochinhphu.vn/state-economic-sector-plays-leading-role-in-viet-nams-economy-resolution-no-79-111260108210650597.htm); [Vietnam News draft policy](https://vietnamnews.vn/economy/1725115/viet-nam-drafts-policy-to-boost-state-owned-economy.html); [VietnamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/double-digit-gdp-growth-hinges-on-state-owned-enterprises-2492298.html)
- **Malaysia:** GLICs were said to control about **42% of Bursa Malaysia market cap** (Gomez, 2017). An older Khazanah figure put 50 listed GLCs at 36% of Bursa (41% of benchmark-index market cap). The latest figure: the 24 GEAR-uP listed GLCs were **26.9% of Bursa's RM2.06tn market cap at end-2025**. The figures use different definitions (GLIC vs GLC). — [FMT 2017](https://fmtv5.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2017/07/25/glics-control-68000-companies-and-42-of-bursa-malaysia); [Khazanah GLC fast facts](https://www.khazanah.com.my/?p=584); [The Edge, 2026](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/763549)
- **Russia:** the MOEX State-owned Companies Index (separate sector index). See Q5 for returns. — [MOEX](https://moex.com/en/index/publicsector)

**Indonesia today (scale of SOE estate)**
- The SOEs Ministry reported consolidated SOE assets of **Rp 10,950 trillion in 2024**, up 5.3% from Rp 10,402 trillion in 2023. Danantara cites about **USD 900bn+ (about Rp 14,700 trillion)** of assets under its umbrella. Prabowo at Davos 2026 claimed "one trillion US dollars". The scopes differ. — [PwC Indonesia](https://www.pwc.com/id/en/media-centre/infrastructure-news/april-2025/anniversary-of-the-state-owned-enterprises-ministry-awaiting-mutualism-between-soe-ministry-and-danantara.html); [ASEAN Briefing](https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/danantara-indonesias-new-sovereign-wealth-fund/); [Setkab WEF 2026](https://setkab.go.id/di-wef-2026-presiden-prabowo-paparkan-peta-besar-transformasi-ekonomi-dan-pembangunan-manusia-indonesia/)
- Seven SOEs (including MIND ID) hold about Rp 8.9 quadrillion (about USD 570bn), **over 85% of all SOE assets**. This is a highly concentrated estate: the large banks (BRI, Mandiri, BNI), Pertamina, PLN, Telkom and MIND ID. — [search summary of Jakarta Post/Danantara coverage](https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2024/11/28/danantara-an-soe-superholding-la-temasek.html) (figure as reported in search results; verify)
- PwC notes the Rp 14,000tn headline is mostly fixed assets, working capital, third-party funds of SOE banks and goodwill that "cannot be invested". Net SOE dividends to the state were only about Rp 26.7tn over 2020-2024 (net of capital injections). — [PwC Indonesia](https://www.pwc.com/id/en/media-centre/infrastructure-news/april-2025/anniversary-of-the-state-owned-enterprises-ministry-awaiting-mutualism-between-soe-ministry-and-danantara.html)
- Indonesia had **138 SOEs as of May 2014, 20 of them listed**. Danantara now oversees roughly "1,000 state-owned firms" including subsidiaries. — [OECD Competition Committee, Indonesia submission](https://one.oecd.org/document/DAF/COMP/WD(2015)53/en/pdf); [Indonesia at Melbourne via APSN, 3 Oct 2025](https://www.asia-pacific-solidarity.net/news/2025-10-03/prabowos-first-year-unconsolidated-state-led-capitalism.html)
- **Private investment still dominates aggregate investment flows.** BKPM 2025 full-year realized investment was Rp 1,931.2tn (+12.7% y/y): domestic (PMDN) Rp 1,030.3tn (53.4%, +26.6%) and foreign (PMA) Rp 900.9tn (46.6%, **+0.1%**). Note that PMDN includes SOE investment, so it is not purely private. — [Infobank](https://infobanknews.com/lampaui-target-realisasi-investasi-2025-tembus-rp1-9312-triliun/); [Jawapos](https://www.jawapos.com/ekonomi/2601160018/realisasi-investasi-ri-pada-2025-capai-rp-1931-triliun-serap-27-juta-tenaga-kerja-pmdn-mendominasi)
- The Jakarta Post (20 Nov 2025) reported that FDI had long been "half or slightly more than half" of total investment but "the balance flipped this year". — [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2025/11/21/foreign-investors-still-weighing-prabowos-state-led-approach)

### Inferences
- Using BPS 2024 nominal GDP of about Rp 22,100tn (my figure, not verified this session), consolidated SOE assets of Rp 10,950tn equal roughly **50% of GDP**. That is high, but asset-to-GDP ratios are inflated by state bank balance sheets. SOE *value added* as a share of GDP is the better comparator with China's 23-28% and Vietnam's about 21%, and no current figure for it was found (see Gaps).
- Ranking on the "top-firm" metric: Indonesia ≈ Malaysia ≈ Saudi Arabia > India > Brazil ≈ Norway >> OECD average. On whole-economy metrics, Indonesia is likely well below China and Vietnam. Being "SOE-heavy at the top" has been true since the 1960s-70s, so it is not evidence of a *move toward* socialism.
- Benchmark for "the state controls most strategic sectors": Indonesia's state already controlled most of oil and gas upstream (Pertamina), power transmission and distribution (PLN), a large share of banking (the Himbara banks), much of the toll roads, ports and airports, and since 2018 majority stakes in Freeport Indonesia (via MIND ID) and Vale Indonesia. That is the status quo, not a change. The marginal question for investors is whether control is spreading into *non-strategic* sectors (food logistics, plantations via Agrinas, cooperatives via Koperasi Desa Merah Putih, commodity exports via "Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia"). That spread is the real signal of drift.

### Gaps
- No current (2024-2026) SOE value-added share of Indonesian GDP or SOE employment share was found. The IMF Fiscal Monitor 2020 country-level SOE asset figures were not extracted. No updated OECD CSS-style ranking post-2013 was found.
- The SOE share of IDX market capitalisation (often cited around 20-25% for listed BUMN) was **not verified** this session.
- No Singapore, Saudi or Norway whole-economy SOE share (e.g., Temasek-linked companies at about one-third of SGX market cap, or the Norwegian state at about 30-35% of Oslo Børs) was verified this session. These are commonly cited figures but unconfirmed here.

## Q3. Indonesian history: Guided Economy → New Order → post-1998 → Jokowi

### Takeaway
Indonesia has had one quasi-socialist episode: Sukarno's Guided Economy after the 1957-59 nationalization of Dutch firms. It ended in hyperinflation of about 600% in 1965 and about 1,500% in 1966. Since 1966, every regime has been a market economy with a large, constitutionally protected (Article 33) state sector. The degree of statism has oscillated: the New Order oil-boom era and the Pertamina crisis; liberalization in the 1980s and after 1998 under the IMF; and a "new developmentalism"/state capitalism under Jokowi built on SOE-led infrastructure that ended with SOE debt distress.

### Cited Findings
- **1957-59 nationalization:** takeovers of Dutch enterprises began in December 1957 (initially through worker/union seizures). The state ended up controlling major industries, plantations and banks, effectively taking "the bulk of the economy" from Dutch hands. — [Wikipedia: Hyperinflation in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation_in_Indonesia); [Leiden scholarly publication](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%253A2964245/view); [Library of Congress country study, "Role of Government"](https://countrystudies.us/indonesia/62.htm)
- **Guided Economy macro outcome:** central bank credit to government reached about half of government expenditure by the mid-1960s. Inflation was already near 100% a year in Jakarta in 1962-64, rose above 600% in 1965 (594% in one series) and about 1,500% in 1966 (635% in another series). — [Wikipedia: Hyperinflation in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation_in_Indonesia); [Keio paper](https://www.ic.keio.ac.jp/en/download/jjwbgsp/1998/1998_1Indonesia.pdf)
- **Pertamina crisis (1975-76):** under Ibnu Sutowo, Pertamina built up debts of **USD 10.5 billion, about 30% of Indonesia's GDP**, and could not repay. Sutowo was removed ("dismissed with honour") and later cleared in 1978. — [Wikipedia: Ibnu Sutowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibnu_Sutowo)
- **Constitutional anchor:** the 1945 Constitution (Article 33) "granted the government rights to intervene in certain sectors and has been used as an argument for state control of companies". Indonesia "has pursued privatisation with varying impetus since at least the 1980s". — [OECD TPP 147](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2013/04/state-owned-enterprises_g17a2290/5k4869ckqk7l-en.pdf)
- **Jokowi era (2014-2024):**
  - Warburton, "Jokowi and the New Developmentalism" (*BIES* 52(3), 2016, pp. 297-320): a "new developmentalism" emerged in 2016, narrowly focused on infrastructure and deregulation, "neither unique nor coherent" and shaped by "ad hocery". Indonesian economic planning had "become increasingly developmentalist" over the decade. — [ANU research portal](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/jokowi-and-the-new-developmentalism/); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:bindes:v:52:y:2016:i:3:p:297-320)
  - Kyunghoon Kim: Jokowi turned SOEs into "strong buffaloes" to drive development, which provoked objections from the private sector ("Jokowi wakes Leviathan"). The government framed SOEs as "agents of development", while institutional coordination mechanisms were thin. — [New Mandala](https://www.newmandala.org/jokowi-wakes-leviathan/); [East Asia Forum, Dec 2016](https://eastasiaforum.org/2016/12/06/institutional-challenges-in-indonesias-state-driven-development/); [Policy Forum](https://www.policyforum.net/indonesias-state-owned-predicament/)
  - Jokowi's second term partly shifted toward attracting private investment (the Omnibus Job Creation Law), while SOE corruption scandals were common. — [Developing Economics, Jan 2020](https://developingeconomics.org/2020/01/29/indonesias-state-led-development-custodian-of-the-national-interest-or-boondoggle/)
  - **SOE debt outcome:** Waskita Karya's debt was about Rp 84.3tn (2023). It missed bond payments in Aug 2023, and bank debt was extended to 10 years. Wijaya Karya signed an MRA with 11 lenders for Rp 20.6-24.2tn (Jan 2024). Equity injections (PMN) for FY2024 were Rp 6tn for WIKA and Rp 12.5tn for Waskita (via Hutama Karya). Reuters describes the builders' liabilities as having "ballooned amid an aggressive push to build infrastructure under the presidency of Joko Widodo". — [PwC Indonesia Nov 2023](https://pwc.com/id/en/media-centre/infrastructure-news/november-2023/debt-restructuring-race-between-wijaya-karya-and-waskita.html); [Jakarta Post Jan 2024](https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2024/01/24/wika-strikes-1-54-billion-debt-restructuring-deal-with-11-lenders.html); [Jakarta Post, Oct 2023](https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2023/10/11/analysis-waskita-karya-defaults-on-bond-payments-investors-dump-holdings)

### Inferences
- History gives a clear test. Indonesia's only real experiment with socialist-style allocation (1959-65) produced hyperinflation, and every subsequent regime has defined itself against it. Article 33's "dikuasai oleh negara" (controlled by the state) clause has been read as *state control of strategic sectors within a market economy*, never as abolition of private ownership. The post-1998 Constitutional Court jurisprudence on Article 33 is not covered in this session.
- The Jokowi-era pattern (SOE-led capex leading to SOE leverage, then restructuring and state bailout) is the closest precedent for Danantara-era risks: the state crowds into infrastructure and resources, and the fiscal/quasi-fiscal bill arrives 5-8 years later.

### Gaps
- Mubyarto's "Ekonomi Pancasila" (1980s) and Sumitro Djojohadikusumo's 1950s Benteng/"Sumitro Plan" ideas were not sourced this session.
- No aggregate SOE debt series (e.g., total BUMN liabilities 2014 vs 2024) was found, only firm-level cases.
- Post-1998 IMF-era privatisation data (number of SOEs sold, proceeds) was not retrieved.

## Q4. Prabowo's own ideas and how analysts label Prabowonomics (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Prabowo's intellectual frame (*Paradoks Indonesia*, Article 33, Sumitro's legacy, anti-"serakahnomics") is statist and nationalist. It admires China's and Singapore's state-led models and wants the state to control natural resources and key industries. Credible analysts label it "state-led capitalism", "economic nationalism", "state activism" or "top-down/military-backed" development. None of the sources found calls it socialism. Prabowo himself explicitly positioned his approach against "socialist or command economies" as well as "neoliberal" ones.

### Cited Findings
- *Paradoks Indonesia dan Solusinya* (book, 2022/23 editions): it admires China's state capitalism under Deng Xiaoping and Singapore's rapid growth, arguing these succeed because the state controls natural resources and key industries, and it links this to Article 33. — [Indonesia at Melbourne via APSN (Adisti Sukma Sawitri, 3 Oct 2025)](https://www.asia-pacific-solidarity.net/news/2025-10-03/prabowos-first-year-unconsolidated-state-led-capitalism.html); [Gebrak.id](https://www.gebrak.id/2026/07/paradoks-prabowo.html)
- His October 2024 inauguration speech tracked the book and argued the economic system had strayed from Article 33. — [Media Indonesia/Medcom column](https://www.medcom.id/pilar/kolom/RkjrjAQk-melawan-paradoks)
- In a DPR plenary speech (2026), Prabowo called Article 33 "the blueprint of the national economy", built on kinship (*kekeluargaan*) and mutual cooperation. He tied consistent implementation to curbing under-invoicing, false export reporting, illegal mining and illegal logging, and reportedly said officials who do not understand Article 33 should resign. — [Setkab](https://setkab.go.id/en/president-prabowo-affirms-commitment-to-1945-constitution/); [Antara](https://en.antaranews.com/news/416344/prabowo-stresses-article-33-of-constitution-to-protect-state-wealth); [IDX Channel](https://www.idxchannel.com/news/prabowo-minta-pejabat-yang-tak-paham-pasal-33-uud-1945-mundur/all)
- **"Serakahnomics":** first used 21 Jul 2025 at the launch of the Koperasi Desa Merah Putih (Klaten). In the 15 Aug 2025 State of the Nation address it targeted food cartels and corruption, with losses he put at up to Rp 100tn (about USD 6.1bn) a year. He said it is distinct from "liberal, neoliberal, free-market, socialist, or command economies". ### Inferences
- The best-supported label is **"populist / nationalist state capitalism with resource nationalism"**, in Musacchio-Lazzarini terms "Leviathan as majority investor" consolidated into a Temasek-style holding (Danantara). It is not socialism, since private property, capital markets and FDI remain, and Prabowo rejects "socialist/command" models by name.
- The thesis's "state controls most strategic sectors" is **largely true as a description of the long-standing status quo**. "Moving toward a socialist economy" is **false** on definitional criteria. "Moving toward a more state-controlled economy" is **partially true** and directionally supported by: asset seizures from "illegal" plantations and mines transferred to state entities (Agrinas Palma); the planned state commodity-export agency; Danantara centralization; cooperative/state food logistics; and reduced anti-corruption oversight. This is a drift toward *dirigisme / state capitalism*, not toward social ownership.

## Q5. Analogues and lessons for investors (Malaysia, Vietnam, Turkey, Russia, China)

### Takeaway
SOE- and SWF-centric state capitalism is compatible with investable markets (Malaysia, Norway, Singapore). But where it is combined with weak institutions, a personalized SWF and politicized capital allocation (Turkey's TVF, Russia, China post-2012), it has coincided with persistent equity valuation discounts, stagnant or falling FDI, and lower productivity. Vietnam is the counter-example of moving *away* from socialism and toward markets, with strong growth and FDI.

### Cited Findings
- **Turkey (Türkiye Varlık Fonu, the closest Danantara analogue):** chaired by President Erdoğan, with about USD 443bn in AUM (2025). It is capitalized mainly by transfers of SOE stakes rather than resource revenues, and its operations are described as "secretive and opaque". It holds 80.6% of Borsa Istanbul. — [Wikipedia: Turkey Wealth Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_Wealth_Fund); [Asia Times, "Erdogan takes wheel of Turkish wealth fund", 2018](https://asiatimes.com/2018/09/erdogan-takes-wheel-of-turkish-wealth-fund/); [Duvar English](https://www.duvarenglish.com/amp/turkey-wealth-fund-buys-ebrds-stake-in-borsa-istanbul-news-8448)
- Turkey's FDI fell from a peak of about USD 22bn in 2007 to about USD 12.5bn in 2021, attributed to "increasingly opaque means of doing business". High rates and inflation continued to deter foreign investment in 2026. — [Wikipedia: Turkey Wealth Fund (citing FDI data)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_Wealth_Fund); [AGBI, Aug 2026](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/economy/2026/08/high-rates-and-inflation-deter-foreign-investment-in-turkey/)
- **Russia:** MSCI Russia ADR/GDR annualized net return since May 2008 was about **-1.6%** (to Dec 2021), against about **+2.5%** for MSCI EM. Russian equities historically traded at a median **~38% discount** to MSCI EM (about 64% at one snapshot), attributed partly to "rampant corruption and state interference". — [MSCI factsheet](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/3b75a8ba-8690-41c4-9e0e-fe34f80ef808); [WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/us/insights/blog/are-there-valuation-improvements-in-russia/pdf); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.de/de/news/113200/analyse-ishares-msci-russia-adrgdr.aspx). Russia reversed privatisation in 2004-08 through strategic-sector rules and takeovers. — [OECD TPP 147](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2013/04/state-owned-enterprises_g17a2290/5k4869ckqk7l-en.pdf)
- **China, "the state strikes back" (guo jin min tui):** from 2012 private investment growth slowed, and by 2015 the state regained ground. The share of bank lending to state firms more than doubled from 2013 to 2016. Lardy estimates that declining SOE productivity plus weaker private investment cut China's growth by **1.6-2.0 percentage points a year**. — [PIIE chart](https://www.piie.com/research/piie-charts/chinese-investment-has-tilted-back-towards-state-owned-firms); [Andrew Batson preview of *The State Strikes Back*](https://andrewbatson.com/2019/01/24/a-preview-of-nick-lardys-new-book-the-state-strikes-back/); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/print/news/china/diplomacy/article/2185905/china-puts-its-economy-risk-spurning-market-oriented-reforms-it)
- **Malaysia (GLICs/GLCs):** GLICs at about 42% of Bursa market cap (2017 claim), against 26.9% for the 24 listed GLCs at end-2025. GLIC funds are heavily invested domestically (15.8% to 62.7% in local listed equities). Ongoing "GEAR-uP" and value-up programmes aim to improve GLC returns. — [FMT 2017](https://fmtv5.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2017/07/25/glics-control-68000-companies-and-42-of-bursa-malaysia); [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/690571); [The Edge 2026](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/763549); [IDEAS Malaysia policy paper on GLCs](https://www.ideas.org.my/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/PI45-Government-Linked-comapnies-and-its-Impacts-on-the-Malaysian-Economy-V5.pdf); [East Asia Forum, "Political preference crowding out enterprise", 2015](https://www.eastasiaforum.org/2015/01/14/political-preference-crowding-out-enterprise/)
- **Vietnam:** the state-sector share of GDP fell from 24.2% (2010) to about 21% (2023). The government targets a private share of 50-58% of GDP by 2030 (Res. 68, 2025), yet still affirms the "leading role" of the state sector (Res. 79, 2026). — [Baochinhphu](https://en.baochinhphu.vn/state-economic-sector-plays-leading-role-in-viet-nams-economy-resolution-no-79-111260108210650597.htm); [Vietnam News](https://vietnamnews.vn/economy/1725115/viet-nam-drafts-policy-to-boost-state-owned-economy.html)

### Inferences
- For investors, a regime's label matters less than **institutional quality around state ownership**: SWF transparency, independent boards, minority-shareholder protection, and whether state entities crowd out or co-invest with private capital. Malaysia, Singapore and Norway show that high state ownership can coexist with investable markets when governance is rules-based. Turkey and Russia show the downside when the SWF is presidentially controlled and opaque.
- On the governance dimension, Danantara currently sits closer to the TVF model than to Temasek: a president-appointed and supervised structure, the removal of SOE officials from Anti-Corruption Law coverage, off-budget financing, the Dalio exit, and the 2025 equity sell-offs. It sits closer to the Temasek/Khazanah model on intent (profit/ROA targets, co-investment with conglomerates via the Patriot Bond, and Prabowo's WTO commitments).
- **Leading indicators to monitor (for scoring the thesis over time):**
  1. FDI realization growth (2025: +0.1% y/y against +26.6% for domestic).
  2. The SOE share of new bank credit (the China 2013-16 test).
  3. New state takeovers of private assets beyond strategic sectors (plantations, food logistics, commodity exports).
  4. Administered-price coverage (fuel, rice HET, fertilizer, cooking oil DMO).
  5. Danantara disclosure and audit regime.
  6. Dividend payout versus capital-injection flows to SOEs.
  7. Listed-SOE governance (minority-shareholder outcomes, forced mergers or rescues as with Waskita).

### Gaps

- Malaysian equity and FDI outcomes under Mahathir's GLC/NEP era (1981-2003, including the 1998 capital controls), and Vietnamese FDI levels after doi moi, were not quantified this session.
- No quantified post-2012 private-investment share for China was retrieved (only the direction from Lardy/PIIE).
- No systematic cross-country study linking SWF governance to equity returns was retrieved.
