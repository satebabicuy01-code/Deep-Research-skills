# Indonesia: Openness to Foreign & Private Investment and Regulatory Direction (as of 30 Oct 2026)

Scope note: Research done 30 Oct 2026. About 22 search/fetch calls. Several key primary pages (Kontan, Forbes, US State Dept 2026 ICS, MSCI PDF) returned 403 or unreadable PDFs, so some figures come from search-result summaries of those pages and are marked as such. Pre-2025 background I know but did NOT re-verify this session is listed under Gaps, not as findings.

Overall read: the evidence does not support "socialist / state-controlled economy" as a literal description. Headline FDI is still large and roughly half of realised investment, the US trade deal commits Indonesia to cutting local-content barriers, and EU-CEPA and OECD accession are advancing. **But 2025–2026 shows a clear, quantifiable shift toward state direction of commodity value chains and state appropriation of assets.** Examples: Danantara's DSI becoming the sole exporter of coal, CPO and ferroalloys from 1 Jan 2027; about 6 million ha of plantation and forest land taken under Satgas PKH with about 4.1 million ha handed to SOE Agrinas; 100% export-proceeds (DHE) retention routed to state banks; and the threatened revocation of 28 firms' permits, including Astra's Martabe gold mine. Markets have priced this in. Moody's and Fitch moved to negative outlooks, citing policy unpredictability and centralisation. Foreign investors have net-sold about Rp95tn of equities YTD. MSCI proposed (Oct 2026) moving Indonesia to "Standalone" status. Net FDI (BoP) fell 26% y/y in Q1 2026.

## 1. FDI realisation (BKPM/Ministry of Investment & Downstreaming): totals, foreign vs domestic, sectors, source countries

### Takeaway
Realised investment keeps growing in rupiah terms. It was Rp1,714tn in 2024, Rp1,931tn in 2025 (+12.7%) and Rp528.7tn in Q3 2026 (+7.6% y/y). But **foreign investment has stagnated in nominal rupiah since 2024 while domestic investment, including SOE/Danantara-linked investment, drives growth.** PMA was flat at about Rp900tn in 2024–2025, and its share fell from about 52.5% (2024) to 46.6% (2025), recovering to about 50–51% in 2026. Basic metals (nickel smelting) remain the single largest FDI sector. Balance-of-payments net FDI (BI basis) is weaker than BKPM realisation: down 26% y/y in Q1 2026.

### Inferences
- The FDI story is stagnation and concentration, not collapse. Nominal-rupiah PMA was flat 2024→2025, and rupiah depreciation to about 18,000/USD by June 2026 means USD-terms FDI likely fell. The 2026 PMA share near 51% is partly a base effect.
- Growth leadership has passed to PMDN, which includes SOEs and Danantara vehicles. That fits a "state-led capex" pattern without implying foreign capital is being pushed out.
- BKPM realisation (gross, includes reinvested earnings and domestic) and BI net FDI diverge sharply. Investors should watch BI BoP net FDI for actual crowding-out signals.
- The heavy metals/smelting concentration of FDI is mostly Chinese-linked, in nickel parks such as Central Sulawesi (Rp47.3tn in Q3 2026). It is policy-induced, via the export ban, and shows that state direction channels foreign capital rather than excluding it.

## 2. Regulatory restrictiveness: OECD FDI RRI, Positive Investment List, Omnibus Law, deregulation

### Takeaway
On formal entry rules, Indonesia liberalised substantially from 2021. It replaced the Negative List with a Positive Investment List after the Job Creation Law. The US State Dept's 2026 Investment Climate Statement still credits this. Historically, however, Indonesia ranks among the most restrictive economies on the OECD FDI RRI. OECD accession (technical review since July 2026, with about 20 of 24 chapters submitted and a "two-year" target set in Oct 2026) is the main liberalising anchor. A current numeric RRI score was not obtained.

### Cited Findings
- **US State Dept 2026 ICS (Indonesia):** Indonesia dropped its Negative Investment List in 2021 and has opened many sectors to foreign investors since the Job Creation Law. Investors describe the climate as much improved over the past decade, but other barriers remain. — [US State Dept 2026 ICS (search summary; page 403 on fetch)](https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/indonesia)
- **OECD 2019 review:** an ASPI analysis described Indonesia as one of the most restrictive countries assessed on several benchmarks, including foreign ownership ceilings and approval procedures. — [ASPI Strategist](https://www.aspistrategist.org.au/wp-content/themes/aspi/print-noimages.php?pid=52565)
- **OECD on ASEAN:** the ASEAN average FDI RRI remains above OECD and non-OECD averages, though barriers are gradually being cut. — [OECD Southeast Asia](https://www.oecd.org/southeast-asia/data/)
- **Older OECD view:** a 2011 OECD review found Indonesia had liberalised its FDI regime over 25 years "with little backtracking". — [OECD (search summary)](https://search.oecd.org/daf/inv/mne/47556737.pdf)
- **OECD accession:**
  - Technical review stage began July 2026. — [Tempo](https://en.tempo.co/read/2100040/indonesias-oecd-accession-technical-review-to-resume-in-july); [ANTARA](https://en.antaranews.com/news/413151/indonesias-oecd-accession-to-enter-technical-review-in-july-minister)
  - In early Oct 2026, Coordinating Minister Airlangga set a goal of completing accession within two years, shortened from 3–4 years. About 20 of 24 chapters have been submitted. — [ANTARA, Oct 2026](https://en.antaranews.com/news/434313/indonesia-targets-oecd-accession-within-two-years)
  - An earlier statement aimed to finish the technical review by 2030. — [ANTARA](https://en.antaranews.com/amp/news/418077/indonesia-aims-to-finish-oecd-technical-review-by-2030)

### Inferences
- The de jure entry regime is more open than in 2014–2020. The 2025–26 shift toward state control works through operational and conduct rules (export channels, FX retention, land enforcement, SOE intermediaries) rather than through FDI entry caps. That is precisely what the FDI RRI does not capture well.
- OECD accession creates an external test. DSI's export monopoly, DHE retention and land seizures may conflict with OECD codes on capital movements and national treatment, so how Indonesia resolves this is a key signal to watch.

### Gaps
- Indonesia's latest numeric FDI RRI score (the OECD database, last update about 2020–2023 data) was not retrieved. From memory it is about 0.35 versus an OECD average of about 0.06, but this is NOT verified; pull it from stats.oecd.org.
- Not re-verified this session:
  - Perpres 10/2021 and 49/2021 details.
  - Constitutional Court ruling 91/PUU-XVIII/2020 (Nov 2021, "conditionally unconstitutional"), Perppu 2/2022, and Law 6/2023 re-enactment.
  - MK ruling Oct 2024 (168/PUU-XXI/2023) requiring a new Manpower Law.
  - Any 2025–2026 deregulation packages, including PP 28/2025 on risk-based licensing.

## 3. State-direction instruments: export channelling, DHE, TKDN, export bans, divestment, Danantara

### Takeaway
This is where the "state control" thesis has the strongest evidence. **Danantara Sumberdaya Indonesia (DSI), 99% owned by Danantara, becomes the sole exporter of coal, CPO and ferroalloys from 1 Jan 2027** under PP 24/2026 and Permendag 15–17/2026. Private producers become domestic suppliers to an SOE trading monopoly. **DHE rules require 100% of natural-resource export proceeds to be held onshore for 12 months**, in state-owned FX banks with conversion capped at 50%. Mining exporters from US/China/Australia/Canada-linked jurisdictions get a carve-out to 30%/3 months, effective 1 Sep 2026, under PP 21/2026. TKDN remains enforced aggressively: Apple was forced into about a $1bn investment pledge to lift the 2024 iPhone 16 ban. Countervailing signal: in the Feb 2026 US trade agreement, Indonesia committed to remove local content requirements and other non-tariff barriers for US goods.

### Cited Findings
**DSI export monopoly**
- **Legal basis:** PP 24/2026 designates PT DSI as sole exporter. Permendag 15/2026 covers coal, 16/2026 covers CPO and 17/2026 covers ferroalloys. — [Indonesia Business Post, ~10 Jun 2026](https://indonesiabusinesspost.com/6687/national-resilience/indonesia-enacts-centralized-export-regime-via-danantara-portal-despite-market-hesitation)
- **Timeline:**
  - From 1 Jun 2026 (transition): exporters must report activity and approvals to DSI. Existing contracts remain usable to 31 Dec 2026.
  - From 1 Jan 2027: exports are carried out exclusively by DSI. Private operators "effectively become domestic suppliers to DSI".
  — same source
- **Reaction:** several Chinese coal buyers postponed June shipments. INDEF and Andalas University economists warned buyers could shift to Australia, Russia or Malaysia. — same source
- **Ownership and stated rationale:** DSI is 99% owned by Danantara. The stated rationale is under-invoicing; the government estimates up to $150bn/yr in lost commodity-export revenue.
  - Earlier reporting described DSI handling documentation from 1 Jun and full contracts, shipping and payments from 1 Sep 2026. Later rules set exclusivity from 1 Jan 2027, so **the timeline conflicts across sources**. — [Reccessary](https://www.reccessary.com/en/news/indonesia-export-control-coal-nickel-palm-oil); [Petromindo](https://www.petromindo.com/news/article/indonesia-to-route-coal-palm-oil-exports-through-state-firm-from-june-2026)
- **Market and industry reaction:**
  - Palm oil producer shares fell sharply after the May 2026 announcement. — [Procurement Resource](https://www.procurementresource.com/news-and-articles/indonesia-centralizes-palm-oil-exports-state-agency)
  - Coal miners warned buyers may shift supplies. — [Petromindo](https://www.petromindo.com/news/article/indonesian-coal-miners-warn-buyers-may-shift-supplies-over-new-export-policy)
- Krungsri describes DSI as the sole export intermediary for coal and CPO from 1 Jun 2026, and cites "populist and state-interventionist policies" and "resource nationalism" as drivers of investor concern. — [Krungsri, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.krungsri.com/en/research/research-intelligence/indonesia-2026)

**DHE (export proceeds retention)**
- **Base regime:** GR 8/2025, refined by GR 2/2026, requires 100% retention of natural-resource export proceeds for 12 months.
- **GR 21/2026 (third amendment to GR 36/2023):** effective 1 Jun 2026. Proceeds must be placed in a special domestic account, reportedly only at state-owned FX banks, with rupiah conversion capped at 50%.
- **Article 18A:** from 1 Sep 2026, qualifying mining exporters need only retain a minimum of 30% for three months.
— [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/indonesia/international-law/new-implementation-of-foreign-export-proceeds-government-regulation-no-21-of-2026-on-the-third-amendment-to-government-regulation-no-36-of-2023-on-foreign-exchange-from-export-proceeds-from-natural-resources-business-management-andor-processing-activities); [MUC](https://muc.co.id/en/article/new-dhe-sda-rule-takes-effect-in-june-2026-offers-tax-incentives); [IDNFinancials](https://www.idnfinancials.com/news/68113/indonesia-relaxes-dhe-rules-mining-exporters-only-need-to-retain-30); [AHK](https://indonesien.ahk.de/en/infohub_news/indonesia-eases-export-proceeds-rules-for-qualifying-mining-exporters)
- The Jakarta Post (6 Aug 2026) calls the exemptions a "two-tier export regime" tied to bilateral commitments with the US, China, Australia and Canada. — [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2026/08/06/analysis-indonesias-dhe-exemptions-create-a-two-tier-export-regime)

**TKDN / local content**
- **The ban:** iPhone 16 sales were banned from Oct 2024 for failing the TKDN requirement (about 35–40% for 4G/5G phones). Apple had pledged $109.6m but realised about $94.5m.
- **The deal:** the ban ended after Apple raised its offer from $10m to about $1bn, mainly R&D and academies (no iPhone assembly). Sales resumed 11 Apr 2025.
— [AppleInsider, 26 Mar 2025](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/03/26/indonesias-iphone-16-ban-will-finally-be-lifted-on-april-11); [MacTech, 11 Apr 2025](https://www.mactech.com/2025/04/11/the-iphone-16-is-finally-available-in-indonesia); [StoneTurn on LCRs](https://stoneturn.com/insight/a-tale-of-two-industries-indonesias-local-content-requirements/)

**US Agreement on Reciprocal Trade (signed 19–20 Feb 2026)**
- Indonesia agreed to remove non-tariff barriers, "such as local content requirements and burdensome certification standards", and to eliminate tariffs on more than 99% of US goods. — [The Diplomat, Feb 2026](https://thediplomat.com/2026/02/indonesia-signs-reciprocal-trade-agreement-with-us-tariff-set-at-19/); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/02/united-states-finalizes-trade-deal-indonesia.html)

### Inferences
- The DSI single-gate export model is the most "state-capitalist" measure in the dataset. It puts a state trading monopoly over roughly the top two export commodities (coal, CPO) plus nickel-derived ferroalloys. It is comparable to marketing-board models, not to nationalisation of production. Upstream assets stay privately owned, but pricing power, revenue capture and information shift to the state.
- DHE plus DSI plus state-bank-only placement together raise state control over the FX and commodity-revenue chain. Carve-outs negotiated with the US, China, Australia and Canada show the regime bends under bilateral pressure. That is "negotiated statism", not autarky.
- TKDN functions as a tool to extract investment commitments, as in Apple's case. It points to industrial-policy coercion, not exclusion of foreign firms.
- Net: these are restrictions on conduct and revenue flows. They raise the cost of capital and policy risk but are not wholesale expropriation of foreign ownership.

### Gaps
- Not verified this session:
  - The nickel ore export ban (since Jan 2020) and bauxite ban (June 2023).
  - The copper concentrate export ban and Freeport/Amman relaxation status in 2025–26.
  - The 51% foreign mining divestment rule (PP 23/2010 as amended; Freeport 51% in 2018).
  - Import quota/licensing changes (Permendag 8/2024 reversal).
  - Any Danantara "right of first refusal" rule.
- I found no evidence of a formal Danantara right-of-first-refusal over private assets; this is an open question.
- DSI fee and pricing mechanism, and whether nickel is included, are unclear. Reccessary's headline mentions nickel, but the IBP regulation list says ferroalloys only.

## 4. Expropriation / property-rights events 2024–2026

### Takeaway
There is significant, large-scale state appropriation of land-based assets. **Satgas PKH (forest-area enforcement task force) has reclaimed about 6.0–6.2 million ha,** mostly oil palm plantations in designated forest areas. About 4.1 million ha has been transferred to new SOE PT Agrinas Palma Nusantara. Fines of about Rp21.9tn have been levied on 134 companies. Permits of 28 firms were targeted for revocation after the Nov 2025 Sumatra floods, including Astra International's Agincourt (Martabe gold mine). That was later settled via a Rp200.9bn fine after the energy ministry warned of international arbitration risk. Legal process is criticised as inverted (revoke first, prove later). No new ICSID case for 2025–26 was found.

### Cited Findings
**Satgas PKH land recovery**
- **Recovery data (to 7 Sep 2026):**
  - About 6.001 million ha recovered by Satgas PKH, of which 5.889 million ha are plantation areas.
  - 4.112–4.113 million ha transferred to SOE Agrinas Palma Nusantara.
  - 1.031 million ha handed to the Forestry Ministry.
  - 857,366 ha still pending.
- **Fines:**
  - Potential administrative fines of about Rp21.9tn across 134 companies.
  - About Rp11.4tn paid by 92 companies; about Rp10.5tn unpaid.
  - About Rp10.27tn deposited in the treasury.
- **Agrinas FY2025:** operating surplus about Rp2.86tn, net profit about Rp27.9bn.

— [Palm Oil Magazine, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.palmoilmagazine.com/hot-news/2026/09/30/indonesia-palm-oil-plantation-fines-reach-idr21-9-trillion-5-89-million-hectares-recovered/)
- **Attorney General figure (Oct 2026):** 6,206,212 ha reclaimed. Prabowo said another 4–5 million ha would be seized in 2026. Operating licences of 28 companies covering about 1 million ha of concessions were revoked. — [Palm Oil Magazine, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.palmoilmagazine.com/forest-task-force-satgas-pkh/2026/10/13/indonesia-reclaims-6-2-million-hectares-of-forest-area-used-by-palm-oil-and-mining/); [ANTARA "Two years of Satgas PKH"](https://en.antaranews.com/news/435082/two-years-of-satgas-pkh-reclaiming-state-control-over-forests)
- **Named case:** about 1,600 ha seized from PT Mutiara Intan Permai (Agung Sedayu Group) in March 2026. — [search summary of Palm Oil Magazine/ANTARA results](https://www.palmoilmagazine.com/tag/satgas-pkh-2/)
- **Criticism:** Auriga (Roni Saputra) questioned the legal basis for transferring seized land to Agrinas. Pulitzer-funded reporting frames Satgas PKH as "palm oil business command under the guise of forest restoration". — [Pulitzer Center](https://pulitzercenter.org/stories/pkh-task-force-and-palm-oil-business-command-under-guise-forest-restoration-bahasa)

**Mining/hydro permits (28 firms)**
- On 20 Jan 2026 the government announced it would revoke the Martabe (Agincourt Resources, Astra International group) licence, among 28 firms linked to the Nov 2025 Sumatra floods (more than 1,200 dead).
- Satgas PKH oversaw the revocations and said international firms' contracts would be "deemed null" if found guilty of crimes.
- In Feb 2026 the energy ministry pushed back, citing "high risk of international arbitration and subsequent erosion of investor trust".
- **Compromise:** Agincourt was fined Rp200.9bn (US$11.84m) and NSHE (Batangtoru hydro) Rp200.6bn, plus restoration costs, and operations resumed. Reportedly no formal revocation decree was ever issued for Agincourt.

— [Jakarta Post/Tenggara Strategics, 6–7 Apr 2026](https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2026/04/07/analysis-martabe-compromise-agincourt-pays-rp-200b-to-resume-gold-mining)
- Permit revocations of coal, tin, nickel, bauxite, copper and gold IUPs date from January 2022, and foreign investors may rely on BIT protections. — [A&O Shearman via JD Supra](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/indonesia-s-revocation-of-mining-6025263)

**Arbitration**
- Indonesia won Churchill Mining/Planet Mining (ICSID ARB/12/14 & 12/40). The award dismissed claims in Dec 2016 and annulment was rejected on 18 Mar 2019. Oleovest (ARB/16/26) was discontinued. — [UNCTAD ISDS Navigator](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/investment-dispute-settlement/country/97/indonesia); [Setkab](https://setkab.go.id/en/indonesia-wins-legal-dispute-at-icsid-international-arbitration/)
- An IMFA (Indian Metal & Ferro Alloys) arbitration (PCA, The Hague) was reported, but its date is unclear and it is unverified. — [Eco-Business](https://www.eco-business.com/news/lessons-from-the-2-billion-coal-mining-lawsuit-against-indonesia)
- No confirmed new ICSID registration against Indonesia in 2025–2026 was found.

### Inferences
- Satgas PKH is the clearest "state takeover" mechanism. About 4.1 million ha transferred to one SOE makes Agrinas plausibly among the world's largest oil palm estate holders, against Indonesia's roughly 16–17 million ha total oil palm area (approximate, from memory, not verified). That is a material shift of productive assets from private to state hands, justified as forest-law enforcement rather than nationalisation.
- The Martabe episode shows internal checks still work: the energy ministry's arbitration concern produced a fine rather than revocation. Outcomes are negotiated, not confiscatory, when large or listed or foreign-linked investors are involved. Process risk is high and predictability low.
- Seizures target land and permit legality (forest-area status), not foreign ownership per se. But the line between enforcement and expropriation is blurred, and no compensation is paid where land is deemed illegally used.

### Gaps
- No list of listed or foreign-owned palm oil firms affected by Satgas PKH was found. IDX/SGX disclosures (e.g., Astra Agro, Sinar Mas Agro, Wilmar, First Resources, Bumitama) would need checking.
- The legal basis (Perpres 5/2025 on forest-area enforcement; PP 45/2025 on administrative fines) was not verified this session.
- Tax disputes and fines against foreign companies more broadly were not researched.

## 5. Political/country risk ratings and market verdicts

### Takeaway
Sovereign credit and equity-index gatekeepers have turned negative explicitly because of policy unpredictability, centralisation and governance. Moody's moved Baa2 to a negative outlook (Feb 2026) and Fitch BBB to negative (Mar 2026). MSCI removed six Indonesian firms in April 2026 and in October 2026 proposed reclassifying Indonesia to Standalone, with a decision by June 2027. Foreign equity net selling was about Rp95tn YTD.

### Cited Findings
**Credit ratings**
- Moody's (Feb 2026) revised its Baa2 outlook from stable to negative, citing "reduced predictability in policymaking". Fitch (Mar 2026) followed, citing growing policy centralisation. — [Krungsri, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.krungsri.com/en/research/research-intelligence/indonesia-2026); also [US State Dept 2026 ICS (summary)](https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/indonesia)

**MSCI**

## 6. Trade policy alignment: US deal, EU-CEPA, OECD, CPTPP, BRICS

### Takeaway
Trade policy points to multi-alignment and continued openness: a US reciprocal deal at 19%, EU-CEPA near signing, OECD accession and BRICS full membership. The US deal includes commitments to cut local-content and non-tariff barriers, which directly runs against the statist trend in sections 3–4. The legal durability of US tariffs is uncertain after a US Supreme Court ruling.

### Cited Findings
**US Agreement on Reciprocal Trade**
- Signed 19–20 Feb 2026 by Airlangga Hartarto and USTR Jamieson Greer.
- The US keeps a 19% reciprocal tariff (down from 32% initially), with 0% for some products, reportedly CPO, coffee and cocoa, and a mechanism for textiles and apparel.
- Indonesia eliminates tariffs on more than 99% of US goods and removes non-tariff barriers including local content requirements.
- Snapback to 32% applies if Indonesia signs a pact jeopardising US interests.
- The deal is reportedly signed but not yet in force, and its future is uncertain after a US Supreme Court ruling on IEEPA tariffs.

— [Investing.com/Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/indonesia-us-sign-agreement-on-reciprocal-trade-indonesian-ministry-says-4515060); [The Diplomat, Feb 2026](https://thediplomat.com/2026/02/indonesia-signs-reciprocal-trade-agreement-with-us-tariff-set-at-19/); [The Diplomat, Mar 2026](https://thediplomat.com/2026/03/inside-the-u-s-indonesia-reciprocal-trade-agreement-a-quiet-reordering-of-economic-relations/); [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/us-indonesia-finalize-reciprocal-trade-agreement-trump-tariffs/812708/); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/02/united-states-finalizes-trade-deal-indonesia.html)

**IEU-CEPA**
- Political conclusion was reached in 2025.
- Signing was targeted for end-October 2026; Indonesia says its side is done in principle, while EU-side discussions continue.
- Indonesia will ratify via presidential regulation, with entry into force targeted for early 2027 (EU GSP for Indonesia ends end-2026).
- Signing was not confirmed as of the latest reports (early–mid Oct 2026).

- See section 2: technical review began July 2026, there is a two-year target, and about 20 of 24 chapters have been submitted. — [ANTARA](https://en.antaranews.com/news/434313/indonesia-targets-oecd-accession-within-two-years)

**BRICS**
- Indonesia has been a full member since January 2025, as announced by Brazil. — [Al Jazeera, 7 Jan 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/1/7/indonesia-joins-brics-group-of-emerging-economies); [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/blogs/latest-southeast-asia/latest-southeast-asia-indonesia-joins-brics)

### Inferences
- Simultaneous pursuit of OECD, EU-CEPA and the US deal alongside BRICS indicates non-aligned hedging, not ideological alignment with state-socialist blocs. External treaty commitments (US anti-LCR clause, EU-CEPA investment chapters, OECD codes) constrain how far domestic statism can go, and may be where the DSI/DHE regime gets tested.
- The DHE carve-out for US/China/Australia/Canada-linked mining exporters shows bilateral deals already producing exceptions to statist rules.
