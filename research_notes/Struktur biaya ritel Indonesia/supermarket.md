# Operating cost structure of Indonesian listed grocery retailers (minimarket, supermarket, hypermarket), FY2020 to FY2025 plus 1H2026

Scope and method notes (read first):
- Primary data comes from the audited financial statements (FS) notes "Beban Penjualan dan Distribusi" / "Beban Penjualan", "Beban Umum dan Administrasi", other income, finance cost, lease notes and note 1 (employees, stores). idx.co.id blocked automated access (HTTP 403), so the FS PDFs were taken from the Indo Premier mirror (indopremier.com/xdir), from company websites (alfamidiku.com, ranchmarket.co.id, mppa.co.id, indoritel.co.id), and from a cached copy of the MPPA 2021 annual report. URLs are listed per company.
- All Rp figures are converted to Rp billion. RANC reports in Rp thousand (FY2021 in full Rp). The others report in Rp million. Values marked "(derived)" are my arithmetic on reported figures: sums of selling plus G&A lines, % of revenue, per-store and per-employee figures.
- "Occupancy proxy" = ROU-asset depreciation + short-term/variable rent expense + interest on lease liabilities (PSAK 73). Indonesian store leases are usually prepaid for several years, so most occupancy cost shows up as ROU depreciation and lease interest stays small. AMRT is the clearest case: Rp2.5tn ROU depreciation against only Rp1.9tn of lease liabilities.
- 9M2026 FS could not be retrieved: the IDX was blocked and the mirror paths returned 404. 1H2026 (30 June 2026, unaudited) is used as the latest period.
- "Salaries" = the salaries/wages/employee-benefit lines in selling plus G&A. For MIDI it also includes the separate "imbalan kerja karyawan" (post-employment benefit) lines.

## Q1. Year-by-year cost tables per company (IDR bn and % of net revenue)

### Takeaway
I built full line-item tables from the audited FS notes for AMRT (FY2021 to FY2025 plus 1H26), MIDI (FY2020 to FY2025 plus 1H26), MPPA (FY2021 to FY2025, with FY2020 at summary level only) and RANC (FY2020 to FY2025). ## Q2. Who is most cost-efficient (minimarket vs hypermarket vs premium supermarket), and why?

### Takeaway
The large minimarket operators are the most cost-efficient. AMRT has the lowest total opex ratio among the profitable chains (about 19% of revenue) and the most stable operating margin (3.3–4.1%). MIDI carries a higher opex ratio (about 22–24%), but its gross margin is about 4 percentage points higher, and in 2025 to 1H2026 it posted the best operating margin (4.7–5.5%). The hypermarket (MPPA) and the premium supermarket (RANC) operate at or below break-even. MPPA is hurt by a 17–18% gross margin and a heavy occupancy and energy burden. RANC is hurt by high and rising payroll and occupancy costs on shrinking revenue.

## Q4. Management commentary: UMP/minimum wage, PLN tariffs, rent, and efficiency measures

### Takeaway
Management explicitly links rising salary costs to minimum-wage increases (MIDI) and store closures to landlord non-renewals and rent hikes (AMRT). Efficiency levers cited include solar panels and energy saving (MIDI, MPPA), store rationalisation (MPPA, RANC), franchise expansion (Alfamart, Alfamidi), divesting sub-scale formats (MIDI's Lawson, HERO's Hero Supermarket) and online channels (MPPA). No explicit commentary on PLN tariff changes was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Minimum wage (UMP) path:** The national average UMP rose 0.57% (2021), 1.4% (2022), 7.2% (2023) and 6.5% (2025, uniform national rise under Permenaker 16/2024) — [Bisnis.com (Kemnaker data)](https://ekonomi.bisnis.com/read/20251216/9/1937216/upah-minimum-pernah-naik-7-ini-data-kenaikan-rata-rata-ump-sejak-pandemi-covid-19). For 2024, Bisnis's table shows 6.5% while CNBC/Katadata report about 3.6% average; the sources conflict, so treat 2024 as about 3.6%. The 2026 average UMP rose 5.91% to Rp3,508,714 — [Bisnis.com](https://ekonomi.bisnis.com/read/20260121/12/1946028/kenaikan-rata-rata-ump-2026-di-bawah-khl-menaker-bilang-begini).
- **MIDI, wages:** MIDI said 2024 G&A rose 17.75% to Rp487.8bn "mainly due to the increase in the minimum wage (UMR) and expansion that resulted in an increase in salary expenses". S&D rose 15.26% "in line with the growth of new outlets that impacted on the increase in salary expenses, electricity expenses, depreciation expense, and distribution expenses". It also cited "minimum wage adjustments" and inflation as squeezing lower-middle-class purchasing power — [MIDI AR2024](https://alfamidiku.com/medias/uploads/AR2024.pdf).
- **MIDI, solar and energy:** Solar plants (PLTS) at warehouses and offices in six branches (1.21 MWp) produced 254 MWh in 2024 and cut average branch electricity load by 26% a month (12.7% in 2023). PLTS at Alfamidi Super stores (400.6 kWp) cut those stores' electricity load by 32% a month in 2024 — [MIDI AR2024](https://alfamidiku.com/medias/uploads/AR2024.pdf). AR2025 also lists using renewable energy through solar panels to meet operational energy needs — [MIDI AR2025](https://alfamidiku.com/medias/uploads/AR2025.pdf).
- **MIDI, portfolio:** MIDI divested Lawson (LWS) on 14 May 2025 to focus on Alfamidi. That year it had 2,587 stores, including 171 franchise units — [MIDI AR2025](https://alfamidiku.com/medias/uploads/AR2025.pdf).
- **AMRT, rent and closures:** AMRT opened more than 1,400 stores and closed more than 400 in 2024 (net +1,033 Alfamart). President Director Anggara Hans Prawira gave the reasons as landlords not renewing leases, rents rising sharply after the initial term ("sewanya naik tinggi") and then weak-sales stores. He stressed that closures were "bukan karena performance". In Q1 2025 the group closed 109 stores: 57 Alfamart, 11 Lawson, 19 Alfamidi and 22 Dan+Dan — [detik](https://finance.detik.com/berita-ekonomi-bisnis/d-7927461/alfamart-tutup-400-lebih-toko-sepanjang-2024). Kontan also reported management confirming that hundreds of stores closed and citing expensive store rent — [Kontan](https://industri.kontan.co.id/news/manajemen-benarkan-ratusan-toko-alfamart-tutup-cek-laporan-keuangan-terbaru).
- **AMRT, expansion and costs:** Capex of about Rp5tn was earmarked for 2025 store expansion — [Bisnis.com](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20250522/192/1879241/alfamart-amrt-sedia-capex-rp5-triliun-untuk-ekspansi-gerai-2025). Bloomberg Technoz noted that "seluruh pos beban AMRT mengalami kenaikan" (all expense lines rose) in 2024 — [Bloomberg Technoz](https://www.bloombergtechnoz.com/detail-news/66634/mayoritas-beban-naik-laba-amrt-jatuh-7-jadi-rp3-15-t-di-2024). The FY2022 FS also flags PP 35/2021 (the Job Creation Law implementing rules on contract and outsourced labour and termination) as able to affect minimum employee benefits — [AMRT FY2022 FS](https://www.indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/Q4%20-%202022/AMRT_Q4_2022.pdf).
- **MPPA:**
  - Operating expenses fell 13% in 2021 to Rp1.3tn: selling -26% to Rp207bn and G&A -10% to Rp1.1tn. Management said it remained committed "to further review and implement operational efficiency measures".
  - Electricity efficiency "continued to become important focus". The online business (Hypermart Online, Chat & Shop) is framed as supporting "reduced usage of store electricity & air conditioning needs, more flexible operating hours".
  - The AR cites "headcount productivity at stores", and lists "relatively high electricity & fuel tariffs" among pressures on consumers.
  - Source: [MPPA AR2021](https://mppa.co.id/misc/AR/AR-SR-2021.pdf). Store rationalisation shows in the count falling from 211 (2020) to 175 (2025) (same AR and FS note 1).
- **HERO:** HERO closed Giant in 2021 and sold the Hero Supermarket segment to a related party in June 2024, exiting grocery to focus on IKEA and Guardian (health and beauty) — [HERO FY2025 FS](https://www.indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/HERO_Q4_2025.pdf).
- **RANC:** RANC reduced stores from 70 to 57 between 2022 and 2025 and recognised impairment of ROU and fixed assets (for example Rp4.3bn ROU impairment in 2025 and Rp3.9bn in 2024) plus losses on asset disposals (Rp6.3bn in 2025 and Rp9.5bn in 2024). It sold an associate for a Rp110.7bn gain in 2024 — [RANC FY2025 FS notes 10/26](https://www.indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/RANC_Q4_2025.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Wages are the cost line management most often blames,** and the FS data agree. Salary ratios rose at AMRT and RANC, and per-employee salary cost rose from 2021/2022 to 2025 at AMRT (+13%, 2021→2025), MIDI (+9% per permanent employee, 2022→2025) and RANC (+14%, 2022→2025; +25% from 2021). The 2026 UMP rise of about 5.9% points to continued pressure. AMRT's 1H26 salary ratio of 10.55% (from 10.30% in 1H25) is consistent with this.
- **Rent pressure for minimarkets shows up mainly at renewal**, as closures and relocations, and only gradually in the P&L because rents are prepaid. AMRT's rent and ROU ratios stayed broadly stable at 2.4–2.6% of revenue.
