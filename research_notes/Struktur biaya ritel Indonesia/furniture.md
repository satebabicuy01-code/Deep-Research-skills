# Operating cost structure of furniture / home-furnishing retailers in Indonesia, FY2020 to FY2025 (+9M2026)

Scope note: Only one listed entity in Indonesia publishes audited numbers for a large-format furniture retailer: **PT DFI Retail Nusantara Tbk (ticker HERO; named PT Hero Supermarket Tbk until Dec 2024)**, which runs IKEA Indonesia. **Informa** sits in the unlisted Kawan Lama group and has no public financials. **Fabelio** went bankrupt in 2022. I found no public financials for Dekoruma, Vivere, Olympic (retail), JYSK Indonesia or Courts Indonesia. All amounts are IDR. "Rp bn" = billions of rupiah; FS tables are in Rp millions. "Derived" = my own arithmetic on the cited figures. "Est." = an estimate that rests on stated assumptions.

---

## 1. HERO / DFI Retail Nusantara: portfolio changes 2020–2026 and whether IKEA can be isolated

### Takeaway
IKEA cannot be cleanly isolated in any audited year. From FY2024 HERO reports **one single "special retail" segment** (Guardian plus IKEA). In FY2022 and FY2023 the segment note split "Supermarket" (Hero) from "Special retail" (Guardian plus IKEA), never IKEA alone. The cleanest proxy for IKEA revenue is the **royalty line divided by the 3% Inter IKEA franchise fee**. On that basis IKEA Indonesia's revenue was about **Rp 2.2–2.4 trillion in 2022–23, falling to about Rp 1.8 trillion in 2025**. This is an estimate: it assumes the royalty line is IKEA's alone. Purchases from IKEA Supply AG fell 36% from 2022 to 2025.

### Inferences
- **Est. IKEA Indonesia net revenue = royalty ÷ 3%**: 2022 ≈ Rp 2,238 bn; 2023 ≈ Rp 2,384 bn; 2024 ≈ Rp 1,962 bn; 2025 ≈ Rp 1,808 bn; 9M2025 ≈ Rp 1,365 bn; 9M2026 ≈ Rp 1,361 bn. On this basis IKEA was about 51% of 2022 group revenue and about 37% of 2025 revenue.
  - The pattern fits management's narrative: IKEA revenue rose in 2023 as 2022 store openings annualised, then like-for-like sales fell in 2024–25.
  - Assumptions behind the estimate: (a) the royalty line is wholly the IKEA franchise fee; Guardian is a DFI-owned brand, and from 2025 a separate "management fee" line appears. (b) The fee base equals net revenue in the IDR accounts.
  - Implied **Guardian revenue (derived)** ≈ Rp 3.04 trillion in 2025 and Rp 2.58 trillion in 2024. This is an estimate, not a disclosed figure.
- Cross-check: IKEA Supply AG purchases ÷ estimated IKEA revenue ≈ 42% (2024) and 39% (2025). That ratio is plausible for an IKEA franchisee whose gross margin is about 40–45% and which is increasing local sourcing. The 36% fall in IKEA Supply purchases from 2022 to 2025 is consistent with both weaker IKEA volumes and the stated "increased local sourcing" (17 local suppliers by 2025, per AR 2025).
- The "Special retail" segment's thin result margin (3.8% in 2022, 1.0% in 2023) next to Hero Supermarket's 13.4%/7.8% shows how much IKEA weighed on the segment. Special retail also carried about Rp 370–393 bn of net finance expense a year, mostly lease interest (Section 3). After finance costs the segment was deeply loss-making, so IKEA's big-box lease burden, not its operating line, was the main loss driver in 2022–23.

### Gaps
- There is no disclosed IKEA-only revenue, opex, store-level margin, selling area or headcount for any year. DFI Retail Group's own "Home Furnishings" segment combines IKEA Hong Kong, Taiwan, Macau and Indonesia, so it cannot isolate Indonesia.
- I could not open the FY2020 and FY2021 audited FS (IDX returns 403). FY2020/FY2021 expense-by-nature lines, ROU depreciation, lease interest and segment notes are therefore **not extracted**. Only headline revenue and net loss are available, from search snippets and news.
- The exact opening dates of IKEA stores 5–7 (Taman Anggrek, Surabaya, and the 7th store) could not be verified from primary sources.

## 2. Year-by-year cost table: HERO/DFIN consolidated (best available IKEA proxy)

### Takeaway
After two loss years (2022–23), the Guardian+IKEA business turned to a small operating profit (op. margin 1.6% in 2024, 1.9% in 2025, 4.0% in 9M2026) and a 0.8% continuing net margin in 2025. Most of the improvement came from (1) the occupancy/lease burden falling from about 12.4% to about 7.3% of revenue, (2) gross margin rising from about 40% to about 45%, and (3) utilities falling from 2.45% to 1.48% of revenue. Staff costs stayed stuck at about 14.4–15.1%. Card fees and the new DFI management fee are rising.

### Inferences
- **The occupancy burden is the biggest structural change.** It fell from 12.4% of revenue (2022) to 7.3% (2025), driven by the collapse in lease interest (5.8% → 0.75%).
  - Several things explain the drop: large ROU derecognitions in 2023 and 2024, the exit of Hero Supermarket leases, and lower-cost or remeasured IKEA leases. A remeasurement would also explain why short-term/variable rent rose to 3.2% in 2025.
  - I could not confirm which specific IKEA leases were modified, so this explanation is inferred.
  - In 2022–23 the lease-interest line alone exceeded the whole operating margin. That is the main reason the Guardian+IKEA big-box business lost money after finance costs.
- **Wages have been sticky.** Salaries stayed at about 14.4–15.1% of revenue in every full year, despite annual UMP increases, because headcount was cut (4,861 → 4,176 in 2024). Headcount rose again to 4,656 in 2025, and salaries were up 6.1% in 2025. The ratio dipped to 13.6% only in 9M2026, on +15.8% revenue growth.
- **Utilities fell from 2.45% to 1.48%**, mostly a mix effect: supermarkets carry chilled food. IKEA reports an energy-intensity programme (AR 2025 covers all IKEA stores and the distribution centre), but the size of its saving is not disclosed.
- **New cost pressures are payment and group charges.** Card charges rose from 0.9% to 2.3% (2.9% in 9M2026). Since 2025 DFI charges a group management fee (1.5% of revenue in 2025). Together these largely offset the occupancy savings in 2025, which is why total opex/revenue *rose* to 43.3% in 2025 even as operating margin improved on higher gross margin.
- The rise in gross margin (to 45.3%) is partly mix: Guardian is a higher-margin business that is growing, while lower-margin supermarkets left the perimeter. The falling share of IKEA imports also plays a part.

### Gaps
- FY2020 and FY2021 lines are missing. FY2020/21 cost ratios would also be on a different perimeter (Giant plus Hero, with Giant moved to discontinued in the FY2021 FS).
- ROU depreciation for 2024 comes from the PPE roll-forward and may include Hero Supermarket depreciation for H1 2024, which was discontinued. Treat the 2024 occupancy proxy as approximate.
- There is no separate "logistics" line other than "Distribusi" and "Pengepakan". IKEA home delivery and assembly costs may sit in cost of revenue or be netted, and are not disclosed.
- Selling area (m²) is not disclosed in the FS or AR extracts I reviewed.

## 3. Informa (Kawan Lama): ownership and available data

### Takeaway
Informa is run by **PT Home Center Indonesia**, a Kawan Lama Group company that is *not* consolidated in ACES (PT Aspirasi Hidup Indonesia Tbk). ACES's filings list it only as a related party under common control. There are no public Informa financials, so no cost table is possible.

### Cited Findings

## 5. Which furniture retailer is most efficient? How does IKEA's big-box model compare? What did management say?

### Takeaway
With only one audited reporter, no peer ranking is possible. IKEA Indonesia's big-box model was structurally inefficient through 2023: high lease and finance cost, weak like-for-like sales after the 2021–22 store roll-out. It reached profit only after cost cuts in 2024–25.

Management consistently blamed **weak furniture demand linked to the property market**, not wage, electricity or rent inflation. It pointed to **cost control, space optimisation of large stores, local sourcing, store-layout changes and online/marketplace channels** as the fixes.

### Cited Findings
- FY2023: IKEA's "Like-for-like sales… declined due to reduced home renovation and furniture demand caused by weak property market activity… Profit for the year reduced as a result of lower like-for-like sales and gross margins… IKEA management is executing a number of initiatives… including **space optimisation for some larger stores, cost control and inventory management**." — [DFI FY2023 RNS](https://www.dfiretailgroup.com/media/305jfown/p240313_0.pdf)
- FY2024: "effective cost-saving measures contributed to an improved operating result… IKEA remains focused on… enhancing store commerciality, optimising store layout, and refining shopping tools… increased local sourcing, supported by a more effective marketing strategy." — [DFI FY2024 RNS](https://www.dfiretailgroup.com/media/c50djqhz/p250313.pdf)
- Q1 2025: IKEA "showed improvement through effective cost-control measures". Its initiatives included "perluasan segmen online" (expanding the online segment). Q1 2025 group revenue was Rp 1.21 trillion (+13%) and profit Rp 27 bn (vs a Rp 1.12 bn loss). — [Investor.id, 1 May 2025](https://investor.id/market/396331/dfi-retail-hero-membalikkan-rugi-jadi-laba-terungkap-penyebabnya)
- FY2025 AR: IKEA is still "affected by weaker household furniture demand in line with adjustments in consumer spending patterns", but "cost efficiency measures and operational productivity improvements" improved performance. IKEA sells through Shopee and TikTok Shop, and works with 17 local suppliers. — [DFIN AR 2025](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/AR-SR-DFIN-2025-FINAL-2.pdf)
- 2021: IKEA's Q1 2021 result suffered from "high pre-opening expenses incurred for new stores", partly offset by "strong e-commerce growth". — [DFI Q1 2021](https://www.dfiretailgroup.com/media/5dsbw1yj/p210430.pdf)
- IKEA global layoffs did not affect Indonesian operations, according to HERO. — [Kontan](https://industri.kontan.co.id/news/ikea-global-pangkas-karyawan-hero-pastikan-operasional-di-ri-tetap-aman) (headline via search only)

### Inferences
- **IKEA's big-box economics (derived from the proxies):**
  - Estimated revenue of about Rp 1.8 trillion over 7 stores in 2025 is about Rp 258 bn per store. In 2023 it was about Rp 2.38 trillion over 6–7 stores, roughly Rp 340–400 bn per store. Store counts for 2022–23 are not verified, so these per-store figures are only indicative.
  - The same 2025 revenue over 367 Guardian stores implies Guardian at about Rp 8.3 bn per store, using the estimated Rp 3.04 trillion. Big boxes generate roughly 30 times the revenue per box of Guardian stores, but they also carry large fixed occupancy, energy and staff cost per site. When like-for-like sales fall, the whole margin is exposed.
- **What actually moved the cost ratios was not wage, electricity or rent inflation.** The cited management statements never name UMP, electricity tariffs or rent escalation as drivers. The ratios moved because of (a) perimeter changes (Giant closure, Hero divestment), (b) lease restructuring and lower lease interest, and (c) headcount cuts in 2024.
- 9M2026 shows operating leverage: revenue +15.8%, opex only +11.3%, opex ratio 39.9%. But gross margin slipped 2.3 pp (46.2% → 43.9%) and card fees rose. The operating margin (4.0%) was therefore slightly *below* 9M2025 (4.6%). That suggests the growth came at lower margin, through promotions, channel or mix. The FS does not say which.

### Gaps
- I found no management statements specifically quantifying UMP increases, electricity (PLN) tariff impacts or logistics-cost inflation for IKEA Indonesia.
- No peer data (Informa, Dekoruma, etc.) is available to rank efficiency. "Most efficient" can only be judged within DFIN over time, not across companies.
- 9M2026 management commentary (Oct 2026 results release) was not retrieved. A [Bisnis.com Dec 2025 article](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20251210/192/1935517/siasat-induk-ikea-dan-guardian-hero-dongkrak-pendapatan-jelang-tutup-tahun) on year-end revenue initiatives was found but not readable (403).
