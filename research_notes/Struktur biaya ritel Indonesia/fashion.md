# Operating cost structure of Indonesian listed fashion, apparel and department-store retailers, FY2020–FY2025 (+1H2026)

Coverage: MAPI, MAPA, LPPF (renamed PT MDS Retailing Tbk in 2026), RALS, SONA, ZONE.

Method: All primary numbers come from audited consolidated financial statements (laporan keuangan audited, notes "Beban Penjualan / Beban Umum dan Administrasi / Beban Usaha / Beban Keuangan"). I downloaded the PDFs from the IndoPremier filing mirror and company sites and read them in full text. Each later-year FS carries the prior year as a comparative. Where a figure was later restated, I used the latest FS. Figures marked **[derived]** are my own arithmetic on the cited numbers. "% rev" means % of reported net revenue (pendapatan bersih).

## Q1. Year-by-year operating cost tables per company (IDR bn and % of net revenue)

### Takeaway
Full cost-line detail from audited notes is available for every company for FY2022–FY2025, for MAPI for FY2020–FY2025, and for LPPF for FY2021–FY2025. For MAPA, RALS, SONA and ZONE in FY2020–FY2021, and LPPF in FY2020, I found only headline figures from secondary sources. The cost structures differ sharply by business model:
- **Department stores (LPPF, RALS):** heavy fixed costs. Salaries are about 15–16% of net revenue. Occupancy is about 22% for LPPF (leased stores) and about 9% for RALS (largely owned stores, so depreciation and repairs replace rent).
- **Brand/specialty retailers (MAPI, MAPA):** total opex is about 32–34% of revenue, but they also pay royalties (MAPI about 3.5% in total, MAPA about 3.7% inside COGS).
- **SONA (airport duty-free):** about 17% of revenue goes to airport concession fees.
- **ZONE:** the smallest company, with sub-scale occupancy costs of about 18% of revenue.

## Q2. Who is most cost-efficient? Department stores (LPPF, RALS) vs specialty/brand retailers (MAPI, MAPA)

### Takeaway
On FY2025 operating margin, LPPF is the most profitable per rupiah of net revenue (19.5%). This is an artefact of booking consignment revenue net. On a gross-merchandise basis, LPPF's operating margin is about 10%, close to MAPA's 13% and MAPI's 9%. MAPA is the best all-round operator: high gross margin (about 47%), opex of about 34%, rising revenue per employee, and stable occupancy cost at about 10.7%. RALS and ZONE are the least efficient on trend, with shrinking revenue against sticky fixed costs.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Department stores vs specialty retailers:
  - **Consignment model:** it lets LPPF and RALS shift inventory risk and part of staffing (brand promoters) to suppliers. This shows up as a high gross margin on net revenue (LPPF about 66%, RALS about 52%) but also a high fixed-cost ratio (opex 46–49% of net revenue).
  - **Operating leverage:** with heavy fixed costs, operating leverage is strongly negative when gross sales fall. That is what happened in 2023–2025 for LPPF (gross sales −12% from 2023 to 2025 [derived]) and RALS (−9% from 2023 to 2025 [derived]).
  - **Specialty/brand retailers (MAPI, MAPA):** they carry inventory and royalty costs but have grown their revenue base 2.3–2.9× since 2020–2022. This diluted fixed costs: MAPI's opex fell from 42% to 32% of revenue.
- **Most cost-efficient operator:** MAPA, on the combination of lowest salary ratio (about 9.7%), stable occupancy (about 10.7%), highest revenue per employee among the full-format retailers, and a recovering operating margin. LPPF is the most efficient department store, with best-in-class margin on net revenue. Its gross-sales economics, though, are only about 10% operating margin and are deteriorating.
- **Weakest cost structure:** ZONE, with occupancy at about 18% and a falling gross margin, and RALS, whose 2025 opex rose to 49% of shrinking revenue.

## Q3. Trends 2020→2025 by cost line (% of revenue): COVID, recovery, and the post-2023 consumer squeeze

### Takeaway
There are two diverging paths:
- **MAPI and MAPA (growth-driven dilution):** cost ratios fell or held as revenue compounded. MAPI's opex/revenue went from 42.0% (2020) to 32.4% (2025), salaries from 14.4% to 9.9%, and occupancy from 11.8% to 9.7%.
- **LPPF, RALS and ZONE (de-leveraging):** after the 2022 peak, cost ratios rose as revenue shrank. LPPF's opex went from 43.5% (2022) to 48.2% (2025) and occupancy from 20.4% to 22.2%. RALS opex went from 44.0% to 49.4%. ZONE's opex went from 38.9% to 46.1% and its operating margin from 17.1% to 5.4%. These companies cut absolute costs, but not as fast as sales fell.

  - MAPI's 2020 opex was 42.0% of revenue and its operating result about −0.4%. The café & restaurant segment margin was −7.9% and department stores −3.3% — [MAPI FY21 FS](https://www.map.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Laporan-Keuangan-MAP-31-Des-2021-Audited.pdf)

LPPF's concessions were Rp285.3 bn (2021) and Rp32.1 bn (2022), booked as a reduction of finance costs — [LPPF FY22 FS Note 7](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0666/9212/0727/files/LPPF_FS_Dec_2022_Audited_2.pdf). RALS booked Rp72.1 bn of rent-concession income in 2022 — [RALS FY23 FS Note 20](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/q4_2023/RALS_Q4_2023.pdf)
  - In 2020, LPPF's gross sales fell 52.3% and it lost Rp873 bn — [Bisnis.com](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20210217/192/1357600/pandemi-bikin-matahari-lppf-rugi-rp873-miliar-pada-2020)
- **Recovery peak (2022):**
  - LPPF's operating margin reached 28.6% with opex at 43.5% of net revenue. This was helped by Rp256 bn of other gains and residual rent concessions.
  - RALS's operating margin was 13.2% and ZONE's 17.1%.
  - MAPI's revenue rose 46% (2021→2022 [derived]) and its opex ratio fell to 33.3% — Q1 tables.
- **Post-2023 squeeze, department stores:**
  - LPPF's gross sales fell from Rp12.55 tn (2023) to Rp11.03 tn (2025) [derived]. Salaries per net revenue rose from 13.1% (2022) to 15.4% (2025), even though absolute salaries fell from Rp971 bn to Rp892 bn. Rent rose from 8.1% to 9.4%, and occupancy from 20.4% to 22.2%. Utilities have been stuck around 3.4–3.8%. Marketing was cut from 1.8% (2023) to 1.3% (2025) — [LPPF FS FY22–FY25](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/LPPF_Q4_2025.pdf)
  - RALS's salaries rose from 13.3% to 16.3% of revenue (2022→2025) and electricity from 4.8% to 6.1%. Depreciation jumped from 4.1% to 7.7% after store refurbishments. Per the fixed-asset note, store-equipment additions were Rp159.6 bn in 2024 (cost rising from Rp1,057.7 bn to Rp1,210.3 bn) and Rp191.6 bn in 2025. Separately, about Rp910.8 bn of store equipment cost was derecognised in 2025, with Rp910.7 bn of matching accumulated depreciation, so these were essentially fully depreciated assets. ROU depreciation fell from 9.0% to 6.4% as leased stores were closed. Promotion was cut from 1.5% to 0.9% — [RALS FY23 and FY25 FS](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/RALS_Q4_2025.pdf)
- **Post-2023, MAPI and MAPA:**
  - MAPI's gross margin fell from 45.3% (2023) to 41.7% (2025) because of mix: Apple purchases rose to 15.14% of revenue. Opex efficiency (34.5% → 32.4%) partly offset this.
  - MAPI's salaries/revenue fell from 11.1% to 9.9%. Rent & service charge was stable at about 4.1–4.4%, ROU depreciation at about 4.8–5.0%, and utilities at about 1.1–1.2%. A&P edged up from 1.6% to 1.8% — [MAPI FY25 FS](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/MAPI_Q4_2025.pdf)
  - MAPA's opex/revenue drifted up from 31.8% (2022) to 34.0% (2025). PP&E depreciation rose from 2.2% to 2.7% (new store capex), utilities from 0.7% to 0.9%, and royalty in COGS from 2.6% to 3.7%. Its operating margin fell from 16.1% (2022) to 12.5% (2024) before recovering to 13.1% (2025) — [MAPA FY23 and FY25 FS](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/MAPA_Q4_2025.pdf)
- **Reported rent jump from 2022:** MAPI's rent jumped from Rp94 bn (2021) to Rp932 bn (2022) and LPPF's from Rp178 bn to Rp525 bn. This reflects the end of COVID concessions and turnover/variable rents returning as sales recovered (variable rent is expensed outside PSAK 73). It is not a like-for-like rent inflation signal — [MAPI FY23 FS Note 34 footnote](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/q4_2023/MAPI_Q4_2023.pdf); [LPPF FY22 FS Note 2k](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0666/9212/0727/files/LPPF_FS_Dec_2022_Audited_2.pdf)
- **1H2026 signals:**
  - LPPF's opex/revenue improved to 40.7% (vs 43.9%), with salaries −4% and utilities −16% YoY [derived].
  - RALS's revenue fell another 12.6% and its opex ratio rose to 45.9%.
  - MAPI's opex ratio improved to 32.0% and MAPA's to 35.4%.
  - ZONE's revenue jumped 35% with operating margin up to 9.2%.
  - Sources: the 1H26 FS links in Q1.

### Inferences
- The post-2023 middle/lower-middle-class squeeze shows up almost entirely in the department-store P&Ls. Revenue declined while salary, rent and electricity costs (largely fixed or indexed to UMP/PLN tariffs) fell more slowly, pushing cost ratios up by 3–5 pp.
- The brand retailers escaped this pattern through store growth, premium/aspirational and digital (iPhone) categories, and regional expansion. Their cost ratios fell, but their gross margin is eroding from mix.
- RALS's rising depreciation alongside falling ROU depreciation suggests a strategic shift: closing leased stores while reinvesting in owned/remodelled ones. This raises the fixed-cost base per remaining store.

### Gaps
- Variable vs fixed rent is not separately disclosed by LPPF or MAPI, so I could not quantify turnover-rent sensitivity.
- No consistent like-for-like electricity tariff analysis was done. Utilities ratios are the only evidence, and I did not verify PLN tariff changes in 2020–2026 for this note.

## Q4. Management commentary: minimum wage (UMP), rent, electricity, and efficiency measures

### Takeaway
Management at LPPF and RALS explicitly ties weak results to slowing middle- and lower-class spending. Their response is store rationalisation (LPPF closed 13 stores in 2024 and planned 8–10 closures in 2025; RALS closed 6 in 2024 and at least 4 in 2025), reviews of rent and labour costs, and headcount reduction (LPPF payroll −22% and RALS payroll −28% from 2022 to 2025 [derived]). MAP Group's framing is "Trim Expenses", "Optimizing Headcount" and "Brand & Store Rationalization", alongside continued expansion. Minimum wages rose 6.5% nationally for 2025 and by a formula-based, province-specific increase for 2026. I found no company-specific quantification of UMP impact.

### Inferences
- With UMP rising about 6–6.5% a year and payroll per employee rising faster still (LPPF +35% and RALS +36% per head, 2022→2025 [derived]), department stores have relied on headcount cuts and closures rather than wage restraint to hold absolute salary costs flat. Salary as % of revenue still rose because sales fell.
- Electricity/utilities are not discussed by management in the sources found. The numbers show utilities sticky in absolute terms (LPPF about Rp220–250 bn; RALS about Rp145–160 bn) and rising as a share of falling sales at RALS (4.8% → 6.1%).
- The brand-retail playbook (MAPI/MAPA) treats efficiency as headcount optimisation within growth, not retrenchment. This works while revenue grows at a double-digit pace, but leaves the cost base exposed if growth slows, given salary costs of about Rp4.3 tn and occupancy of about Rp4.2 tn at MAPI.

### Gaps
- No company-specific statement quantifying the impact of UMP increases on payroll was found for any of the six companies.
- No management commentary on electricity (PLN) tariffs or rent-renewal escalation rates was found.
- The 2026 provincial UMP figures (e.g., DKI Jakarta) were seen only in search snippets and are not verified against a primary source.
- Omnichannel/online cost impacts (fulfilment, platform fees) are not separately disclosed.
