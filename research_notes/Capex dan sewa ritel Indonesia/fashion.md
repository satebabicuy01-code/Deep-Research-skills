# Capex and rent of Indonesian listed fashion / department-store retailers (MAPI, MAPA, LPPF/MDS Retailing, RALS, ZONE, SONA), FY2020–FY2025 (+1H2026)

## Method and conventions

**Tags**
- [R] reported in the source.
- [D] my arithmetic on reported figures.
- [E] my estimate, with the method stated.
- Units are IDR bn unless noted. "% rev" means % of reported net revenue. LPPF and RALS book consignment net, so for them I also show % of gross sales (outright + gross consignment, from the cost-structure notes).

**Capex definition (cash)** [D]
- Capex = cash paid for fixed assets + advances for fixed assets + intangibles/licences + investment property.
- For MAPI and MAPA, it also includes "payments of liabilities for purchases of PP&E". These are capex accrued in the prior year and paid this year. MAPI moved this line from financing to investing in its FY2024 FS, and the 2023 comparative was restated from Rp241.4 bn to Rp232.3 bn.
- MAPI's own "Capital Expenditure" KPI in its Annual Report excludes this payables line. It equals PP&E acquisitions + advances + investment property: 913 (2022), 1,798 (2023), 1,712 (2024) [R]. My reconciliation reproduces those numbers exactly [D].
- "Capex ÷ depreciation" uses depreciation of owned fixed assets only (excluding ROU).

**Cash rent definition** [D]
- Cash rent = lease-liability cash payments (principal + interest) + short-term/variable rent expense, taken as a cash proxy.
- For RALS it also includes **ROU prepayments shown in investing cash flow**: RALS pays many store leases up front.
- For SONA, airport concession fees are shown separately.

## Q1. Capex from the cash flow statement (IDR bn, % revenue, capex ÷ depreciation), incl. MAPI segment capex

### Takeaway
- **MAPI group (incl. MAPA):** the clear capex spender. Cash capex rose from about Rp0.5 tn (2020) to Rp2.2 tn (2024; 5.9% of revenue, 2.2× depreciation), then eased to Rp1.8 tn in 2025 (4.3%, 1.5×). Its own KPI shows Rp1.5 tn in 2025.
- **MAPA:** carried about 55% of the group's spend at 5–7% of revenue (3.1–3.4× depreciation in 2023–24).
- **LPPF:** halved capex after 2023, to about Rp140 bn (2.4% of net revenue; about 1.3% of gross sales) and **below depreciation** (0.7–0.8×) in 2024–25, i.e. harvest mode.
- **RALS:** raised capex to Rp195–235 bn (7–10% of revenue) while shrinking its store count, so all of it is refurbishment.
- **ZONE and SONA:** small, at 1–6% of revenue.

### Inferences
- **Capex intensity, 2023–25 average [D]:** RALS about 7.8% of net revenue, MAPA about 6.5%, MAPI about 5.5%, ZONE about 4.9%, LPPF about 3.0%, SONA about 1.6%. On a gross-sales basis the department stores are much lighter: LPPF about 1.6%, RALS about 4.4%.
- **Trend 2020→2025:**
  - MAPI and MAPA went through a post-COVID catch-up. Capex was below depreciation in 2020–21 (0.6–0.7×), peaked at 2.2–3.4× in 2023–24, and normalised to 1.5–1.8× in 2025. 1H26 is running higher again (MAPI +32% YoY cash capex [D]).
  - LPPF moved from 1.2–1.4× (2022–23) to 0.7–0.8× (2024–25 and 1H26), i.e. under-investing relative to the depreciation run-rate.
  - RALS raised its capex/revenue ratio from 3.4% to 9.9% even as sales fell.

### Gaps
- No audited cash flow statements were obtained for FY2020–21 for MAPA, RALS, ZONE or SONA, or for FY2020 for LPPF. IndoPremier has no FY2020–21 files for these tickers, and IDX blocks bots. An attempt to read stockanalysis.com cash-flow pages failed (tool usage limit). These years are blank.
- MAPI segment capex is only reported including ROU additions. Cash PP&E capex by segment is not disclosed.
- ZONE's 1H26 intangible capex line was not extracted.

## Q2. Expansion (new stores) vs renovation / maintenance / IT split

### Takeaway
- No company discloses a numeric split in its FS, so the splits below are estimates [E].
- **MAPI/MAPA:** management says most capex goes to new stores. This fits the facts: 616 gross openings in 2025, and net +580 stores / +108,857 m² in 2024.
- **LPPF:** its 2024–25 capex (about Rp140 bn) is mostly refurbishment of existing stores and IT. It closed 13 stores in 2024 and 7 in 2025 and opened only a handful.
- **RALS:** opened no net stores (104 → 91 from 2022 to 2025), so effectively 100% of its capex is rejuvenation and maintenance, chiefly store equipment.

## Q6. Synthesis: who is most capital-efficient? (expansion vs renovation burden and cash-rent burden, 2020→2025)

### Takeaway
- **MAPA:** the most capital-efficient grower. In 2025 it spent capex of 5.0% plus cash rent of 10.8% of revenue (total "store-asset cash cost" about 15.8%) and delivered a 13.1% operating margin with 12% revenue growth.
- **MAPI consolidated:** total 14.3% for a 9.3% margin.
- **LPPF:** the least capital-intensive (2.4%) but the most rent-burdened (22.0%), totalling about 24.4% of net revenue, or about 12.9% of gross sales, for a 19.5% margin (about 10% on gross sales). With capex below depreciation, its high free-cash conversion partly reflects under-investment.
- **RALS:** total about 27% in 2025 (about 16–17% normalised), with no growth.
- **ZONE:** 21% for a 5.4% margin, the least efficient.

### Cited Findings
- Store-asset cash cost, 2025 = cash capex % rev + cash rent % rev [D, from the tables above]:
  - MAPA 5.0 + 10.8 = 15.8%
  - MAPI 4.3 + 10.0 = 14.3%
  - LPPF 2.4 + 22.0 = 24.4% (gross-sales basis 1.3 + 11.6 = 12.9%)
  - RALS 9.9 + 17.4 = 27.3% (on the 2022–25 average rent, about 9.9 + 9.9 = 19.8%)
  - ZONE 3.2 + 17.9 = 21.1%
  - SONA 2.0 + 23.0 = 25.0%
- Operating margins 2025: LPPF 19.5%, MAPA 13.1%, SONA 11.6%, MAPI 9.3%, RALS 8.3%, ZONE 5.4% — prior cost note citing [LPPF](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/LPPF_Q4_2025.pdf), [MAPA](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/MAPA_Q4_2025.pdf), [MAPI](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/MAPI_Q4_2025.pdf), [RALS](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/RALS_Q4_2025.pdf), [ZONE](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/ZONE_Q4_2025.pdf) and [SONA](https://indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2025/q4/SONA_Q4_2025.pdf) FS.
- Incremental revenue per rupiah of cumulative capex, 2022→2025 [D]:
  - MAPI: revenue +Rp16.1 tn on cumulative cash capex 2023–25 of Rp6.1 tn, i.e. about Rp2.6 of revenue per Rp1 of capex.
  - MAPA: +Rp9.5 tn on Rp3.2 tn, i.e. about Rp3.0.
  - LPPF: revenue −Rp0.7 tn on Rp0.58 tn of capex.
  - RALS: −Rp0.63 tn on Rp0.60 tn.

### Inferences
- **Expansion burden:** borne almost entirely by MAPI/MAPA, at roughly Rp1.0–1.3 tn per year of growth capex in 2023–24, falling to about Rp0.6 tn+ in 2025 [E].
- **Renovation burden:** highest relative to sales at RALS (about 7–10% of revenue, all rejuvenation). LPPF's renovation spend (about Rp110–130 bn per year [E]) looks insufficient given its ageing estate: 58% of FA cost is fully depreciated [D: 1,859.5/3,219.3] and impairments are rising.
- **Rent burden trend 2021/22→2025:** LPPF worsened (+4 pp of net revenue); MAPI/MAPA were stable; RALS was low but lumpy; ZONE worsened (14.7% → 17.9%).
- **Bottom line:** MAPA is the most capital-efficient (highest incremental revenue per capex rupiah, stable rent ratio, margin recovery). LPPF is the most cash-generative per rupiah of capex today, but this is partly harvest-mode under-investment, and it carries the heaviest rent. RALS and ZONE are the least capital-efficient.

### Gaps
- These ratios exclude working capital and royalties (MAPI/MAPA), and they mix cash capex timing (e.g. RALS prepayments, MAPI payables). Treat them as directional.
- No FY2020–21 comparables for MAPA, RALS, ZONE or SONA (data not obtained).
