# Capex and rent of Indonesian listed grocery retailers (AMRT/Alfamart, MIDI/Alfamidi, MPPA/Hypermart, RANC/Ranch Market), FY2020 to FY2025 plus 1H2026

## Method notes (read first)

**Sources**
- All financial statement (FS) figures come from audited FY statements and the unaudited 1H2026 interim statements, read from locally downloaded PDFs. The download URLs are listed per company below; most are the Indo Premier mirror of the IDX filings, plus company websites.
- 9M2026 statements were not obtained, because idx.co.id blocks automated access. 1H2026 is the latest period.

**Units and tags**
- All figures are in Rp billion. RANC reports in Rp thousand (FY2021 in full Rp); I converted it.
- [R] = reported in the FS or by management. [D] = derived by me from reported numbers (sums, ratios, subtractions). [E] = estimate; the method is stated next to it.

**Capex definition [D]**
- Capex = cash paid for fixed assets + advances for fixed assets + intangibles. For AMRT the intangible element is "other non-current assets", which the FS defines as deferred charges for software and business licences. For MPPA it is software.
- Refundable rental deposits are excluded.
- Prepaid leases are excluded from capex and counted as rent.

**Cash rent definition [D]**
- Cash rent = prepaid right-of-use (ROU) acquisitions paid in cash (investing cash flow) + lease-liability payments + short-term/variable rent.
- AMRT, MIDI and RANC lease-liability payments include interest; their lease-movement notes add interest accretion and deduct total payments.
- MPPA pays lease principal in financing and lease interest through finance cost. For MPPA I add principal plus lease interest expense (assumed paid as accrued) plus "Payment for rental expenses" from operating cash flow.
- For AMRT, MIDI and RANC, short-term rent is the P&L "sewa" line, used as a proxy for cash paid.

**AMRT ex-MIDI [D]**
- AMRT consolidates MIDI, so I subtract MIDI's reported figures line by line. Intercompany capex is assumed negligible.
- MIDI includes Lawson until 14 May 2025.

**Key presentation point**
- AMRT and MIDI pay store rent up front (multi-year prepaid leases). The PSAK 73 cash outflow therefore sits mainly in investing cash flow: "Perolehan aset hak guna" at AMRT and "Penambahan aset hak-guna" at MIDI. Only the remaining leases run through lease liabilities.
- MPPA's ROU additions are entirely non-cash, booked through lease liabilities, and paid over time.

## Q1. Capex burden per company 2020→2025 (cash-flow capex, % revenue, capex ÷ depreciation) and the expansion vs renovation/maintenance split

### Takeaway
- **Minimarkets reinvest well above depreciation; the two large-format operators were starved and only recently stepped up.**
  - **AMRT consolidated:** capex rose from Rp2.0tn (2021) to about Rp3.0tn (2024–2025), steady at about 2.4–2.55% of revenue and 1.5–1.8x fixed-asset depreciation.
  - **MIDI:** a heavier 3.5–4.9% of revenue, at 1.4–2.0x depreciation.
  - **MPPA (Hypermart):** spent only 0.6–1.4% of sales, 0.2–0.9x depreciation, an under-invested box estate.
  - **RANC:** cut from about 5% of revenue (2020–21) to 1.1–1.9% (2022–25), 0.4–0.75x depreciation.
- **Management's "capex" figure for Alfamart includes prepaid store rent.** Fixed-asset capex plus prepaid ROU ex-MIDI matches the announced budgets closely: Rp2.9tn in 2021 against a Rp2.5–3tn budget, and Rp3.4tn in 2022 against Rp3.4–3.5tn.
- **No company publishes a formal expansion vs maintenance split.** My estimate [E] for Alfamart ex-MIDI is roughly 45–55% for new stores and DCs, with the rest going to lease renewals, renovation, equipment replacement and IT.

Leasehold improvements of Rp420bn a year in 2024–25 are mostly new-store fit-outs and refurbishments.
- **MPPA and RANC have been in harvest mode.** At 0.2–0.9x depreciation, MPPA's capex barely covers renovation (building renovation additions Rp18–53bn a year) and equipment replacement, so it is effectively 100% maintenance or remodel spending. ## Q2. Rent cash burden per company: lease payments, prepaid rent, short-term rent, ROU additions; trend, and comparison with the P&L occupancy proxy

### Takeaway
- **Cash rent at the minimarkets is about 2.2–3.3% of revenue, and roughly two thirds is paid up front.** At Alfamart, about 57% is prepaid ROU acquisitions (Rp2.3tn of Rp4.0tn in 2025, consolidated). At MIDI it is 64% (2025).
- **Trend:** AMRT consolidated rose from 2.80% of revenue (2021) to about 3.1–3.25% (2023–25). MIDI peaked at 3.2% in 2023 and fell to 2.75% in 2025 and 2.2% in 1H26 after the Lawson exit.
- **Large formats carry 2–3x the burden.** MPPA fell from 9.0% (2021) to 6.1% (2025) through closures and lower lease interest. RANC rose from 3.9% (2020) to about 6.6–6.7% (2023–25).
- **Cash vs P&L:** For growing minimarkets, cash rent runs about 0.4–0.6pp of revenue above the P&L occupancy proxy (ROU depreciation + rent + lease interest), because prepayments lead amortisation. For shrinking MPPA it runs slightly below.

### Inferences
- **Openings are slowing.** Alfamart's 2026 target of 800 Indonesian openings, against 1,000+ in 2022–25, combined with a rising group capex budget, implies a higher share of renewal and refurbishment spending in 2026 [E].
- **A new Alfamart store costs roughly one year of its sales at most.** At Rp1.2–1.4bn per store against about Rp5.0–5.2bn revenue per group store a year, a new minimarket costs about 25% of annual sales. A RANC store, at an upper bound of Rp21–42bn, compares with about Rp51bn of revenue per store. That is consistent with far heavier capital intensity per box for large formats.

### Gaps
- **Alfamart history and renovations:** end-2020, end-2021 group and end-2023 Alfamart counts were not confirmed from primary sources. Gross closures per year are available only for 2024 and Q1 2025. No renovation or refurbishment counts were found for AMRT or MIDI.
- **Dan+Dan:** no store counts found, apart from the 2026 target of 30–40 openings.
- **Large-format store costs:** no hypermarket new-store or remodel cost per store disclosed after 2021.
- **Indomaret (via DNET):** no capex or store data in DNET's associate note, so Indomaret is excluded.
