**Definitions**
- **Cash capex** is the investing cash flow for acquisition of fixed assets, plus intangibles, plus advances for fixed-asset purchases. Investment property and acquisitions are shown separately.
- **Accrual capex** is the "additions" column in the fixed-asset note. It is used for the category splits, because no company reports an expansion-versus-maintenance split.
- **Cash rent** is the cash outflow for leases (principal plus interest), plus upfront or long-term rent prepayments, plus short-term/variable rent expense (a P&L proxy for that cash).
- **Occupancy proxy** (from the prior study) is ROU depreciation plus short-term rent plus lease interest.

## Q1. How large is capex (expansion vs renovation/maintenance) per company/brand, and how did it trend 2020→2025?

### Takeaway
On cash capex as a share of revenue, the four companies split into three groups:
- **ACES (AZKO): lowest.** Capex is only about 1.3–2.3% of revenue and roughly 0.6–1.6x depreciation. The business is mall-based and leased, and the AR calls its capex "business as usual". Most of the spend is store fit-out and renovation plus store equipment.
- **MDIY: the heaviest spender relative to revenue.** Capex was 15–19% of revenue in 2021–23 and fell to about 9% in 2025, but it is almost all expansion: more than 260 openings a year, at roughly IDR 1.6–2.8bn of capex per new store.
- **DEPO and CSAP: lumpy, because they buy land.**
  - DEPO's capex peaked at 12.1% of revenue in 2023 (land plus three new stores) and fell to 3.8% in 2025.
  - CSAP spent IDR 1.17tn (7.1% of total revenue) in 2023, about 22% of retail-segment sales, mainly on land and buildings (545 + 288bn). This was funded by a IDR 854bn rights issue. Capex then fell back to about 2.4% of revenue in 2025 as expansion was slowed to a single Mitra10 opening.

No company discloses an expansion-versus-maintenance split. The [E] splits below use two methods: (a) growth capex equals capex minus non-ROU depreciation; (b) new-store capex equals openings times capex per new store.

**[E] expansion vs maintenance**
- Depreciation is a poor maintenance proxy for MDIY, because renovation assets are depreciated over the short (1–5 year) lease terms. That is why depreciation reaches 6% of sales.
- Method (b): new-store capex [E] = gross openings × about 2.0–2.5bn. The midpoint of the 2022–23 per-store figures (2.1 and 1.6bn), uplifted for inflation and MIY-format stores, gives about 545–680bn of the 726bn cash capex in 2025, or about 75–95% expansion [E].
- The remainder (about 50–180bn) covers refurbishments, the 2nd flagship (Summarecon Mall Bekasi, 1,100 m²), IT and DC.

### Inferences
- **Expansion intensity ranking, 2025 capex ÷ depreciation:** DEPO 2.5x, MDIY 1.5x, CSAP 1.5x, ACES 1.4x. On the 2020–25 average: DEPO about 4.3x and CSAP about 3.4x (both land-driven), MDIY about 3.7x (declining from 6.6x to 1.5x as the base matured), and ACES about 1.1x (near maintenance).
- **Trend 2020→2025:**
  - ACES capex roughly flat at 0.1–0.2tn, with a COVID trough in 2021.
  - MDIY grew capex 4.6x (159 to 726bn) while capex intensity halved.
  - DEPO and CSAP both peaked in 2023 on land purchases, then normalised by 2025. CSAP's cut was explicitly because of weak demand; it opened only one Mitra10 in 2025.

### Gaps
- No company discloses capex split into new stores versus renovation/IT/DC. All splits are [E].
- ACES capex is not split by brand (AZKO, Toys Kingdom, NEKA). The cost of the Ace→AZKO rebranding was not separately disclosed.
- CSAP capex is not split between Mitra10 and Atria, and the 9M2026 advances line is missing. The identity of the 2023 land purchases was not extracted.
- MDIY FY2020 capex is not available; the prospectus starts in 2021. The 2023 cash-versus-accrual capex gap is unexplained.
- DEPO 9M2026 FS was not located.
