# Capex and rent of furniture retail in Indonesia: IKEA Indonesia inside PT DFI Retail Nusantara Tbk (HERO), FY2020 to FY2025 (+9M2026), plus Informa (qualitative)

Scope and conventions
- **Entity.** HERO (renamed PT DFI Retail Nusantara Tbk, "DFIN", in Dec 2024) is the only listed company that consolidates a big-box furniture retailer in Indonesia.
  - IKEA is run by the subsidiary PT Rumah Mebel Nusantara.
- **Perimeter changes.** The perimeter changes every year:
  - 2020: Giant + Hero + Guardian + IKEA.
  - 2021: Giant closed by end-July 2021 and shown as discontinued.
  - 2022–23: Hero + Guardian + IKEA.
  - 2024: Hero Supermarket was sold at end-June 2024 and shown as discontinued.
  - 2025–26: Guardian + IKEA only.
- **No IKEA-only figures exist in any year.** The segment note split "Food/Supermarket" from "Non-food/Special retail" (Guardian + IKEA) up to FY2023. From FY2024 there is a single segment.
- **Units.** Figures are IDR. "bn" = Rp billion. FS tables are in Rp million.
- **Tags.** [R] = reported in the source. [D] = my arithmetic on reported figures. [E] = estimate; the method is stated.
- **Cash-flow perimeter.** Cash-flow lines (capex, lease payments) cover the **whole group, including discontinued operations**. So "% of revenue" uses total-perimeter revenue: continuing + discontinued revenue, wherever the discontinued revenue is disclosed.

## 1. How big is capex (cash flow), capex % of revenue, capex ÷ depreciation, and by segment?

### Takeaway
Group capex peaked in **2021 at Rp 463bn (8.6% of revenue)** and stayed high in **2022 at Rp 275bn (6.2%)**. Both years were driven by the IKEA roll-out: Bandung, Jakarta Garden City (JGC) and Bali in 2021, then Mal Taman Anggrek and Surabaya in 2022. Capex then fell to **Rp 119–133bn (about 2.5–2.7%) in 2024–25**, below owned-asset depreciation (capex ÷ depreciation ≈ 0.7–0.8x). It picked up again in **9M2026 to Rp 166bn (4.1%)**.

Nearly all non-food capex sat in the Guardian+IKEA segment, at Rp 160bn (2020), 303bn (2021), 235bn (2022) and 113bn (2023).

### Inferences
- **2021–22 was the IKEA investment cycle.**
  - 2021 capex/depreciation was 1.3x and 2022 was 2.1x. Non-food capex was 11% of non-food revenue in 2021.
  - From 2023 the group was in **harvest/maintenance mode**: capex below depreciation (0.7–0.8x of owned-PPE depreciation in 2024–25), funded by property sales.
- **The 9M2026 cash capex is a clear step-up**: Rp 166bn already exceeds FY2025's Rp 119bn. It fits the higher end-2025 commitments and the Guardian expansion. However, 9M2026 accrual PPE additions were only Rp 93bn, so part of the 9M2026 cash capex may be payment of capex payables or intangibles (IT). The FS does not break this down.

### Gaps
- There is no IKEA-only capex in any year. FY2024–25 has a single segment.
- The FY2020 capex figures do not reconcile (MD&A Rp 290bn vs segment total Rp 327bn vs PPE additions Rp 168bn), and the FS gives no explanation.

## 2. Expansion vs renovation/maintenance split (IKEA vs Guardian)

### Takeaway
Management attributes capex as follows:
- **2021**: "mainly" to the 3 new IKEA stores.
- **2022**: to new Guardian and IKEA stores.
- **2023, 2024, 2025**: "mainly" to new Guardian stores.

So IKEA growth capex effectively stopped after December 2022. On my estimate, IKEA's 2023–25 capex is maintenance and refit (space optimisation, layout) of roughly Rp 20–50bn a year [E]. No reported split exists.

### Cited Findings
- **2020.** The group spent Rp 290bn, of which Rp 160bn went to Non-Food (2019: Rp 301bn, of which Rp 186bn Non-Food). The decline reflected lower Food capex during the pandemic [R]. — [AR 2020, MD&A "Belanja Modal Tahun 2020"](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/1058a.pdf)
- **2021.** "The total investment value of capital goods realized in fiscal year 2021 was Rp419.4 billion, **mainly for the addition of assets under construction related to the opening of 3 (three) IKEA outlets**." [R] — [AR 2021 MD&A p.103](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/AR-SRHERO2021.pdf)
- **2021 Bali.** IKEA "opened its first store outside of Java **through the conversion of a Giant store in Bali**" (November 2021) [R]. This is the only documented Giant→IKEA conversion. — [AR 2021 MD&A p.89](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/AR-SRHERO2021.pdf)
  - In May 2021 the Jakarta Post reported a plan to convert 5 Giant sites into IKEA — [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2021/05/26/hero-group-to-close-all-giant-supermarkets-by-august). The IKEA count only reached 7 and the 2022 stores were mall sites, so only one conversion (Bali) is confirmed.
- **2022.** "Rp245 billion, mainly for the additional assets in expanding Guardian and IKEA stores" [R]. — [AR 2022 MD&A](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/AR-SRHERO2022-1.pdf)
- **2023.** "Rp118 billion, mainly for the additional assets in expanding Guardian stores" [R]. — [AR 2023 MD&A](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/HEROARSR23Final.pdf)
- **2024.** "Rp126 billion, primarily due to additional assets in opening Guardian stores" [R]. — [AR 2024 MD&A](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ARSRDFI2024.pdf)
- **2025.** "Rp129 billion, primarily for increase in assets related to the construction of Guardian stores" [R]. — [AR 2025 MD&A p.126](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/AR-SR-DFIN-2025-FINAL-2.pdf)
- **IKEA turnaround levers.** Management named "space optimisation for some larger stores, cost control and inventory management" (FY2023), then "optimising store layout" (FY2024), as IKEA initiatives. These were not presented as expansion. — [DFI FY2023 RNS](https://www.dfiretailgroup.com/media/305jfown/p240313_0.pdf); [DFI FY2024 RNS](https://www.dfiretailgroup.com/media/c50djqhz/p250313.pdf)

Method for the estimates:
- I allocated the segment/MD&A totals using the store-opening calendar (Section 4) and MD&A attributions.
- Guardian capex was bounded at **about Rp 1.5–3.5bn per new store including refits**. This is an assumption, not disclosed. It is consistent with 2023's Rp 112.6bn special-retail capex alongside +23 net Guardian stores and no IKEA openings.
- **Treat every [E] cell as indicative only.**

### Inferences
- About 60–70% of 2020–22 non-food capex was IKEA growth [E]. From 2023 IKEA has received only maintenance/refit capex, and Guardian absorbs most of the budget.
- Converting Giant sites into IKEA was minor in capex terms: only Bali is confirmed. The bigger Giant-related cash flow was the *sale* of ex-Giant property (Rp 405–478bn a year in 2021–23 proceeds).

### Gaps
- There is no reported split between maintenance and expansion capex, or between IKEA and Guardian capex, for any year.
- The Bali conversion capex is not disclosed.

## 3. Rent: lease cash outflows, short-term/variable rent, ROU additions, lease modifications

### Takeaway
On my reconstruction, total cash occupancy cost (lease principal + lease interest + short-term/variable rent) moved as follows:
- **Low point**: ≈ Rp 345–367bn in 2020–21, when the IKEA leases had not yet annualised.
- **Peak**: ≈ **Rp 592–606bn (12–13% of revenue) in 2022–23**.
- **After restructuring**: ≈ **Rp 348–364bn (7.1–7.5%) in 2024–25**.
- **9M2026**: ≈ **6.4%**.

The decline came from two big lease re-negotiations into lower or variable rents, which I infer were mainly IKEA leases:
- **Nov 2023**: Rp 1,171bn of lease liability derecognised.
- **June 2024**: Rp 746bn derecognised.

Gross (undiscounted) lease liabilities collapsed from **Rp 8.85 trillion (end-2021) to Rp 0.46 trillion (end-2025)**.

**B. Lease events**
- **2020: PSAK 73 (IFRS 16) adopted.** It added Rp 2,230bn of ROU cost and Rp 686bn of lease liability at 1 Jan 2020. — [AR 2020](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/1058a.pdf)
- **2021: IKEA leases plus Sentul sale-and-leaseback.**
  - Non-current liabilities rose by Rp 1.4 trillion "due to increase in lease liabilities resulted from addition of IKEA stores".
  - On 27 Aug 2021 HERO sold the IKEA Sentul City land and building to affiliate **PT Archipelago Property Development (APD)** for **Rp 280bn**, valued by KJPP Wiseso Saladin/JLL. APD leases it back to PT Rumah Mebel Nusantara.
  - Gross lease liabilities jumped from Rp 0.98tn to Rp 8.85tn, with 81% of the PV maturing in more than 5 years, which points to very long IKEA leases.
  - Source: [AR 2021 MD&A pp.103–107](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/AR-SRHERO2021.pdf), Note 14
- **1 Nov 2023: lease modification.** "Some of the lease contracts were changed to a lower lease rate without the change in the scope and terms of the lease." The group derecognised Rp 1,140bn of ROU assets and Rp 1,171bn of lease liabilities, and booked a **gain of Rp 30.6bn** "for the lease contract modification to a variable lease payment arrangement". — FY2023 FS Note 15 ([mirror](https://www.indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2023/q4/HERO_Q4_2023.pdf))
- **1 June 2024: switch to fully variable rent.** "Some of the lease contracts were changed to fully variable lease rate which result in derecognition of right-of-use assets and lease liabilities". The amounts were **Rp 636bn of ROU and Rp 746bn of lease liabilities**. — FY2024 FS Note 15 ([mirror](https://www.indopremier.com/xdir/news/LAPORAN%20KEUANGAN/2024/q4/HERO_Q4_2024.pdf))
- **Variable rent terms.** The accounting policy states that some property leases contain variable payments linked to store sales, "for individual stores, up to 100 per cent" of lease payments. — [9M2026 FS, accounting policy on leases](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/0-2026_DFIN-Q3-FS-Pack.pdf)
- **Related-party landlord (APD).**
  - Finance cost paid to APD: **Rp 18.1bn (2024), Rp 16.7bn (2025)**, which is 8.8% and 13.8% of total finance cost.
  - Operating expense to APD ("technical service, property lease"): Rp 73.0bn (2024), Rp 26.5bn (2025).
  - Source: [FY2025 FS Note 25](https://dfinusantara.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/1-2025_DFIN-FY-FS-BOD-Stat-Opinion.pdf)
- **Hero lessor role.** Since the June 2024 Hero sale, DFIN also *leases out* some stores, a distribution centre and offices to PT Hero Retail Nusantara, and provides transition services until Nov 2026. — FY2025 FS Note 25

### Inferences
- **Which leases were modified.** The modified leases are not named. Two pieces of evidence point mainly to IKEA big-box leases [E]:
  - After the Giant closures, only IKEA's long leases (2021 additions of Rp 1.9tn, gross Rp 8.6tn with mostly more than 5 years remaining) were large enough to produce Rp 1.2tn and Rp 0.75tn liability derecognitions.
  - The variable-rent switch coincides with IKEA's 2023 "space optimisation for larger stores" programme.
  - The June 2024 date also coincides with the Hero Supermarket divestment, so part of that modification could relate to Hero-related leases. The FS does not say.
- **Interest on the APD lease.** It made up roughly **46% of 2025 lease interest** (16.7 ÷ 36.4) [D]. So the IKEA Sentul leaseback is now the single biggest remaining capitalised lease.
- **The shift from fixed to variable rent changed the economics.** Lease interest fell from 5.8% of revenue (2022) to 0.75% (2025). Meanwhile variable/short-term rent rose from Rp 116bn to Rp 156bn (2.6% → 3.2%). The landlord now shares IKEA's sales risk.
- **IKEA-only rent [E].** IKEA's share of the 2022–23 peak cash occupancy cannot be isolated. If roughly 2/3 of 2022 lease liabilities were IKEA, as the 2021 additions suggest, IKEA cash occupancy would have been about Rp 300–400bn on about Rp 2.2–2.4tn of IKEA revenue (royalty-based estimate from the prior note), i.e. ~13–17% of IKEA sales [E, low confidence].

### Gaps
- No IKEA-only lease liability, rent or variable-rent split is disclosed.
- The modified leases are not identified by store or brand.
- The FY2021 split of lease payments between principal and interest is inferred, not reported.
- The FY2020 ROU additions figure is internally inconsistent (Rp 267.9bn vs Rp 818.1bn).

## 6. Is IKEA's big-box model capital-intensive versus small formats (Guardian)?

### Takeaway
Yes, on both owned capex and especially leases. The 2021–22 IKEA roll-out pushed group capex to 6–9% of revenue and lease liabilities from Rp 0.7tn to Rp 2.25tn (gross Rp 8.8tn). Lease costs then took **12–13% of revenue** in 2022–23 while IKEA like-for-like sales fell.

Guardian expansion since 2023 runs at about **2.5% of revenue** with small ROU additions (Rp 160–260bn a year). Profitability improved only after IKEA's leases were renegotiated into lower or variable rents in 2023–24.

### Cited Findings
- Capex % revenue: 8.6% (2021) and 6.2% (2022) in the IKEA years, vs 2.5–2.7% (2024–25) in Guardian-led years [D] (Section 1 table).
- ROU additions: Rp 1,944bn in 2021 (IKEA store additions per MD&A) vs Rp 159–258bn a year in 2023–25 [R] (Section 3 table).
- Cash occupancy: 13.3% (2022) and 11.9% (2023), falling to 7.1–7.5% (2024–25) after the Nov 2023 and June 2024 lease modifications [D] (Section 3).
- Segment result margin of Special retail (Guardian + IKEA): 3.8% (2022) and 1.0% (2023). Net finance expense of Rp 370–393bn a year was larger than the segment result. — FY2023 FS Note 26 (from the prior cost note)
- Impairment of underperforming stores: Rp 51.5bn in 2025 (FY2025 FS Note 9). The 2023 impairment was Rp 56.4bn (FY2023 FS PPE note).

### Inferences
- **Per box [E].** An IKEA big box ties up roughly Rp 120–145bn of owned capex plus Rp 400–600bn of capitalised lease, for about Rp 250–350bn of sales. That is total invested capital (owned + leases) of ~2x annual sales.
- **Guardian [E].** At an estimated Rp 1.5–3.5bn of capex per store and ≈ Rp 8bn of sales per store (prior note estimate), Guardian needs ~0.2–0.4x sales in owned capex with short mall leases. Big-box furniture is therefore several times more capital- and lease-intensive per rupiah of sales.
- **Shifting rent to variable.** Converting fixed IKEA rents to variable or turnover-based rent (2023–24) was the key de-risking step. It turned a fixed-cost burden into a sales-linked cost and explains most of the occupancy-ratio decline.
- **Growth path.** With IKEA expansion frozen since 2022 and DFI promoting capital-light franchising, future growth is likely to be capex-light. IKEA's constraint has shifted from capex to sales productivity per m².

### Gaps
- No IKEA-only ROIC, store-level P&L, selling area or capex figure exists. All IKEA-specific intensity measures above are estimates built on the royalty-based revenue proxy and the segment capex allocation.
