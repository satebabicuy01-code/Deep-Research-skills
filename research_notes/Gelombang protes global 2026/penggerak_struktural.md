# Structural Drivers of the Global Rise in Anti-Government Protest and Civil Unrest (as of November 2026)

Research date: 2 November 2026. About 20 searches and fetches were run. Most numbers come from primary sources: Carnegie, ACLED, Maplecroft, IEP, the ILO, the IMF, the OECD and the Journal of Democracy. Where a figure came only from a secondary source, that is flagged.

## 1. What do protest datasets show about the trend (2017–2026)?

### Takeaway
All the main datasets point the same way. ACLED recorded more than 148,000 demonstrations in 2025, the third straight annual rise, though still below the 2021 record of more than 170,000. Carnegie's tracker records roughly 100–120 "significant" anti-government protests per rolling 12 months in 2025–2026, which secondary sources describe as the highest since 2017. The Global Peace Index ranks violent demonstrations among the three indicators that have deteriorated most since 2008. Verisk Maplecroft forecast 2026 to be more disruptive than 2025, with Europe and the US now among the highest-risk places.

### Cited Findings
**Carnegie Global Protest Tracker** (covers significant anti-government protests since 2017)
- Live page as of 9 September 2026: more than 97 significant anti-government protests in the last 12 months, in more than 55 countries. 33 were active and more than 16 took place in "not free" countries. The page gives no breakdown by motivation and no yearly totals. — [Carnegie Global Protest Tracker](https://carnegieendowment.org/features/global-protest-tracker)
- **Conflicting figures:** a secondary blog cites the same 9 September 2026 update as "more than 107 protests in more than 57 countries, 33 active". — [Voluntouring.org](https://voluntouring.org/2026/10/21/protests-around-the-world/). Wikipedia says that as of March 2026 Carnegie counted at least 120 significant protests in at least 73 countries over the previous 12 months, "the largest figure since 2017". — [Wikipedia: Gen Z protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen_Z_protests). The "highest since 2017" claim could not be confirmed on Carnegie's own page. The rolling windows differ, so these figures do not form one series.
- Carnegie's 2025 year-end analysis by Judy Lee and Thomas Carothers (3 December 2025) says new anti-government protests broke out in more than 70 countries in 2025. The tracker catalogued 70 of them: 26 in "free", 27 in "partly free" and 17 in "not free" countries. — [Carnegie Emissary, "Corruption, Overreach, and Hardship"](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2025/12/global-protests-2025-genz-corruption-economy)
- The same analysis names three main triggers: corruption (a "sizeable portion"), antidemocratic overreach and economic hardship. It also says "rates of youth-led protests between 2017 to 2019 were similar or higher to the rate of youth-led protests in 2025". — [Carnegie Emissary](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2025/12/global-protests-2025-genz-corruption-economy)

**ACLED** (Conflict Index, 11 December 2025, covering December 2024 to November 2025)
- More than 148,000 distinct demonstrations in 197 countries and territories in 2025, the third consecutive annual increase. The record is 2021, with more than 170,000 demonstrations in 217 countries and territories. Protest activity rose in at least 88 countries compared with 2024. Counter-demonstrations rose 33%. Protests "have become more confrontational," though most remain peaceful. — [ACLED, What's driving conflict today?](https://acleddata.com/report/whats-driving-conflict-today-review-global-trends)
- ACLED's Raleigh Report gives a higher figure: more than 173,900 protests and riots between mid-November 2024 and mid-November 2025, close to a 5% increase. This figure probably counts riots together with protests. — [ACLED Raleigh Report, Dec 2025](https://acleddata.com/newsletter/raleigh-report-december-2025)
- Other ACLED counts: more than 51,000 pro-Palestine protests in 104 countries since October 2023 (18,000 of them in 2025), and at least 7,400 US "No Kings" demonstrations. ACLED lists governments toppled amid Gen Z protests in Peru, Madagascar, Nepal, Serbia and South Korea, and concessions in Morocco, Togo, the Philippines and Kenya. — [ACLED](https://acleddata.com/report/whats-driving-conflict-today-review-global-trends)
- ACLED recorded 204,605 political-violence events from 1 December 2024 to 28 November 2025, against 208,219 the year before. The Index uses only political violence, not demonstrations. — [ACLED on X](https://x.com/ACLEDINFO/status/2001986738206757036)

**Verisk Maplecroft Civil Unrest Index** (4 December 2025, Torbjorn Soltvedt)
- Unrest in 2026 was expected to be "more disruptive" than in 2025, and protest activity had risen worldwide over the previous two years. 53 countries recorded more attacks on commercial property, with the biggest increases in Kenya, Mozambique, India, Mexico and Peru. — [Verisk Maplecroft](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/)
- The top 10 for unrest risk over the next 12 months included five European countries (Germany, France, Spain, Italy and the UK), plus the US (ranked 3rd), Brazil, Mexico, India and Myanmar. Seven of the world's largest economies are in the list. Europe is the worst-performing region. — [Verisk Maplecroft](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/); [The Insurer](https://www.theinsurer.com/ti/news/verisk-maplecroft-warns-of-escalating-protests-in-europe-in-2026-2025-12-05/)
- Average monthly protest size in the US rose from 172,000 in Q4 2024 to 696,000 in Q4 2025, the sharpest rise of any country measured. About 90% of protests are peaceful, about 10% turn violent, and fewer than 1% damage private property. Insured losses: Indonesia (August 2025) above US$50m, Nepal on a par with the 2015 earthquake (more than US$200m). — [Verisk Maplecroft](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/)
- Coverage in October 2026 says losses from France's latest unrest could exceed those of the 2023 riots, with more than 3,000 shops damaged or looted in one weekend. — [CIR Magazine](https://www.cirmagazine.com/cir/c2026102001.php)

**Global Peace Index 2026** (IEP, June 2026)
- Peacefulness fell for the 12th consecutive year. 99 countries deteriorated, the most since the Index began 20 years ago. Violent demonstrations, internal conflicts fought and external conflicts fought are the three indicators that have deteriorated most since 2008. "Political instability and a surge in violent demonstrations" drove a 4% decline in the US, to 134th, its lowest-ever ranking. Nepal fell 26 places, the steepest drop in the world. The economic impact of violence reached US$21.81 trillion (10.5% of global GDP). Global military spending hit a record US$2.9 trillion in 2025. — [IEP GPI 2026 press release (PDF)](https://www.visionofhumanity.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2026-GPI-Press-Release.pdf); [GPI 2026 report](https://www.economicsandpeace.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Global-Peace-Index-2026-Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- Event counts (ACLED) and counts of large, significant protests (Carnegie) measure different things. 2025–2026 looks like a peak in the number of major, government-threatening mobilizations, the kind that toppled at least five governments in about 18 months. It is not a record for the raw number of events, which peaked in 2021 during COVID.
- Risk has shifted toward rich democracies: Europe and the US now sit in Maplecroft's top 10. That points to fiscal and polarization drivers, not only Global South development drivers.

### Gaps
- No clean yearly series of Carnegie significant-protest counts for 2017–2026 was found. The tracker lists individual protests but publishes no yearly totals; producing them would require downloading and tallying the data.
- No 2025 or 2026 update from the Mass Mobilization Project was found. The dataset historically covered 1990–2020 and is treated as background only.
- No mid-2026 update of the Maplecroft index was found beyond secondary coverage of the December 2025 edition.

## 2. Economic drivers

### Takeaway
The economic picture in late 2026 combines several pressures. A second energy price shock followed the Iran war and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz (oil above $100, and the IMF forecasts 4.7% global inflation in 2026). Fiscal pressure leads to austerity, and it is made worse by NATO's 5% rearmament goal (France, Italy, Belgium, Romania). Youth unemployment is rising again: 12.4% globally and 257 million NEET (young people not in employment, education or training). Housing costs are high, and inequality is felt sharply. Carnegie's 2025 data lists austerity and the cost of living as one of three main triggers.

### Cited Findings
**Inflation, energy and the cost of living (2026)**
- IMF WEO, October 2026: global growth of 3.1% in 2026 (3.5% in 2025) and global inflation of 4.7% in 2026, falling to 4.1% in 2027. The IMF puts the chance that inflation exceeds 5% in 2027 at about 35%. It ties the inflation rise mainly to energy and food prices and expects the Strait of Hormuz to reopen in March 2027. Downside risks include trade tensions and fiscal vulnerabilities. 32 countries account for 95% of global growth. — [IMF WEO Oct 2026](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/weo/issues/2026/10/13/world-economic-outlook-october-2026); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/12/imf-world-bank-annual-meetings-october-bangkok-asia-.html); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/13/imf-global-growth-forecast-2026-ai-economic-divide/)
- The closure of the Strait of Hormuz (more than 20% of world oil trade) caused a major supply disruption. — [Wikipedia: 2026 Iran war fuel crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis) (secondary)
- Fuel-price protests in 2026: Ireland in April (diesel up about 28% and petrol about 25% after the war began); the Philippines under a national energy emergency, with marches toward the Malacañang palace in March; trucker blockades in Guatemala in September; protests in Portugal, Syria, France (a blockade at the Fos-sur-Mer depot) and Pakistan (Karachi, Lahore); oil back above $100. — [Soufan Center IntelBrief, 22 Sep 2026](https://thesoufancenter.org/intelbrief-2026-september-22/); [CNN, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/15/world/fuel-prices-protests-iran-war-intl); [Wikipedia: 2026 Irish fuel protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Irish_fuel_protests)
- Italy, late October 2026: tens of thousands demonstrated nationally against the cost of living and high military spending. Farmers and truckers blockaded highways and ports, and the prime minister deployed troops. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/italy/italy-strikes-violent-protests-rome-colosseum-meloni-army-rome-milan-rcna606503)
- Verisk Maplecroft (October 2026) warned that war and weather are raising food-security risk in Asia, with potential for unrest. — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/lifestyle/living/2026/10/21/analysts-war-and-weather-raise-food-security-risk)

**Austerity, debt and defence spending**
- Carnegie, 2025: austerity triggered protests in Belgium, France, Romania, Argentina and Slovakia. The cost of living and fuel prices triggered protests in Angola and Ecuador, and housing costs in Spain. — [Carnegie Emissary](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2025/12/global-protests-2025-genz-corruption-economy)
- Maplecroft names mounting public-finance pressure, and higher defence spending straining European budgets, as drivers. The French protests of 2025 were sparked by opposition to spending cuts (about 195,000 protesters in early October 2025), and protests in the UK and Germany each exceeded 100,000. — [Verisk Maplecroft](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/)
- The NATO Hague summit (June 2025) agreed a 5% of GDP target by 2035 (3.5% core defence plus 1.5% security-related). Spain capped its defence budget at about 2.1%, citing social spending. — [Wikipedia: NATO 5% agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_on_5%25_NATO_defence_spending_by_2035); [ICDS](https://icds.ee/en/natos-new-defence-spending-target-whos-planning-what-in-2026/)
- France: the 2026 budget was passed on 2 February 2026 with a deficit target of about 5.0% of GDP. Other estimates put the deficit at 5.4%, the highest in the eurozone. The 2027 draft budget reportedly adds about $7.2bn for the military against about $1.2bn for education. These figures come from advocacy or opinion outlets and are unverified. — [CEPA](https://cepa.org/article/french-defense-funding-the-say-do-gap/); [Yahoo/Bloomberg](https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/france-protests-fuel-wider-fight-140914839.html); [Consortium News (opinion)](https://consortiumnews.com/2026/10/12/french-student-revolt-vs-nato-spending-push/)
- France's school and student revolt (autumn 2026): about a third of the 3,700 lycées were disrupted by October, and the protests spread to other European countries. — [Fortune, 26 Oct 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/10/26/gen-z-underemployment-riots-france-cockroach-india-rebellion/). Consortium News claims more than 250,000 marched on 6 October (unverified, opinion source). — [Consortium News](https://consortiumnews.com/2026/10/12/french-student-revolt-vs-nato-spending-push/)
- Germany exempted defence spending above 1% of GDP from its debt brake but kept the brake in force for everything else, with offsetting pressure on pensions and health (commentary). — [Modern Diplomacy](https://moderndiplomacy.eu/2026/09/05/europe-rearmament-welfare-state-tradeoff/)
- Kenya: protests were triggered by the 2024 Finance Bill tax increases, which were tied to fiscal consolidation, with dozens killed. — [Soufan Center, Oct 2025](https://thesoufancenter.org/intelbrief-2025-october-13/); [Journal of Democracy](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/why-gen-z-is-rising/)

**Youth unemployment and inequality**
- ILO Global Employment Trends for Youth 2026 (11 August 2026): youth unemployment rose to 12.4% in 2025, about 67 million people aged 15–24, up from a two-decade low in 2023. The NEET share rose to 20% (257 million, 9 million more than in 2023). Youth unemployment is highest in the Arab States (26.2%) and Northern Africa (22.6%). North America rose from 8.3% to 9.8%. The ILO expects about 12.3% in 2026–27, and about 6.1% of jobs held by 15–29-year-olds are highly exposed to AI. — [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168120); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/jobs/story/ilo-global-youth-unemployment-rises-12-4-in-2025-with-67-million-young-people-unemployed-world-wide-548670-2026-08-12)
- Country examples: youth unemployment of about 20% in Nepal, where remittances are nearly a third of GDP, and about 35–36% in Morocco, where nearly one in five graduates is unemployed. Madagascar's protests began over water and power cuts. — [Journal of Democracy](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/why-gen-z-is-rising/); [Vision of Humanity](https://www.visionofhumanity.org/the-rise-and-spread-of-gen-z-protests/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/10/04/world/gen-z-protest-movement-explainer-intl)
- US: four in ten recent graduates work in jobs that do not need a degree. The gap between recent-graduate and overall unemployment reached a record 1.8 percentage points in April 2025. Unemployment among 20–24-year-olds was 7.1% (September 2026), against 3.6% for 25–54-year-olds. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/26/gen-z-underemployment-riots-france-cockroach-india-rebellion/)
- The Soufan Center (October 2025) describes the Gen Z protests as "motivated by rising inequality". — [Soufan Center](https://thesoufancenter.org/intelbrief-2025-october-13/)

### Inferences
- The 2026 energy shock is a cyclical trigger stacked on structural fiscal pressure. In Europe, the rearmament-versus-welfare trade-off has become a new fault line that links the "guns vs. butter" debate directly to street mobilization (France, Italy).
- Grievances about economic exclusion among the young (NEET, underemployed graduates) cut across regions. They appear in the Global South (Morocco, Nepal) and in rich economies (the US, France).

### Gaps
- No quantitative study isolating the effect of the 2025–2026 US tariffs on protests was found. The IMF lists trade tensions as a risk but gives no figure linking them to unrest.
- No 2025–2026 data was found on the number of IMF programmes or on protests tied specifically to IMF conditionality (background: Kenya, Pakistan).
- Real-wage and housing-cost data (OECD or ILO Global Wage Report) were not collected in this pass.

## 3. Political drivers (trust, corruption, backsliding, polarization)

### Takeaway
Corruption is the single most cited trigger of 2025 (Carnegie, Chenoweth/Cebul). Distrust is now the majority position: 44% of people in OECD countries have low or no trust in their national government, against 39% with high trust. Edelman 2026 describes a slide from "grievance" to "insularity", with 70% holding an insular mindset. V-Dem 2026 records 44 countries autocratizing, home to 41% of the world's population, a record. Protest is increasingly a reaction against power grabs (term limits, laws against NGOs) and against gerontocratic elites.

### Cited Findings
- **OECD Trust Survey 2024** (fielded October–November 2023, 30 countries): 44% have low or no trust in their national government and 39% high or moderately high trust. Country figures: UK 27%, France about 34%, Germany 36%, Denmark and the Netherlands 44%. — [OECD press release, July 2024](https://oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2024/07/governments-must-better-engage-all-citizens-to-tackle-growing-gaps-in-trust.html); [OECD UK country note](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2024/06/oecd-survey-on-drivers-of-trust-in-public-institutions-2024-results-country-notes_33192204/united-kingdom_a657f98f.html) (background data from 2023)
- **Edelman Trust Barometer 2026** (33,938 respondents, 28 countries): 70% have an "insular" trust mindset, meaning they are hesitant or unwilling to trust people whose values, facts or background differ. Among insular respondents, 61% report moderate or high grievance against business, government or the rich, against 45% of those with an open mindset. The insularity rate is 89–90% in Japan, 81% in Germany, 76% in the UK and 73% in Canada. — [Edelman, Insularity—The Next Crisis of Trust](https://www.edelman.com/newsroom/richard-edelmans-6am-blog/insularity-next-crisis-trust); [ASIS](https://www.asisonline.org/security-management-magazine/latest-news/today-in-security/2026/january/edelman-trust-barometer/); [2026 Global Report PDF](https://www.edelman.com/sites/g/files/aatuss191/files/2026-01/2026%20Edelman%20Trust%20Barometer%20Global%20Report_Final.pdf)
- Edelman 2025 (background): 6 in 10 respondents said the system unfairly favours the rich, the "grievance" theme. — [Edelman blog](https://www.edelman.com/newsroom/richard-edelmans-6am-blog/insularity-next-crisis-trust). A headline 2026 grievance figure of "57%" appears only on a Substack and is unverified. — [Purple Stories](https://purplestories.substack.com/p/the-2026-edelman-trust-barometer)
- **V-Dem Democracy Report 2026** (10th edition, "Unraveling the Democratic Era?"): 44 countries are autocratizing (up from 42), home to 41% of the world's population, a record for the current wave. Only 18 countries are democratizing (about 5% of the population). There are 92 autocracies and 87 democracies, and about 74% of people live under autocracy. The US is no longer classified as a liberal democracy, and global democracy is back at 1978 levels. These figures come from secondary summaries; the PDF could not be fully read. — [V-Dem report PDF](https://www.v-dem.net/documents/75/V-Dem_Institute_Democracy_Report_2026_lowres.pdf); [Democracy Without Borders](https://www.democracywithoutborders.org/41651/v-dem-report-finds-global-democracy-is-back-at-1978-levels/)
- **Corruption as a trigger:** specific scandals sparked protests in North Macedonia, Spain, the Gambia, Nepal and the Philippines, and longer-running anger fed protests in Mongolia, Indonesia, Morocco, Mexico and Serbia. Protests ousted Mongolia's prime minister Oyun-Erdene and Nepal's prime minister Oli. — [Carnegie Emissary](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2025/12/global-protests-2025-genz-corruption-economy)
- **Antidemocratic overreach:** term-limit changes and bids for extended rule (Central African Republic, Mali, Togo), laws weakening institutions (Ukraine, Brazil, Indonesia's military law) and laws targeting the opposition or NGOs (Israel, Ivory Coast, Türkiye, Slovakia, Hungary, the UK). Electoral fraud triggered deadly protests in Cameroon and Tanzania. — [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2025/12/global-protests-2025-genz-corruption-economy); [ACLED](https://acleddata.com/report/whats-driving-conflict-today-review-global-trends)
- Chenoweth and Cebul argue the drivers are economic precarity, exclusion from power through "gerontocratic political systems" with elites "far older than the average citizen", and "above all corruption". — [Journal of Democracy, Jan 2026](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/why-gen-z-is-rising/)
- **Polarization:** Maplecroft names rising political polarization and security-force conduct as drivers. In the US, the second Trump term triggered at least 7,400 "No Kings" demonstrations. — [Verisk Maplecroft](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/); [ACLED](https://acleddata.com/report/whats-driving-conflict-today-review-global-trends)

### Inferences
- There is a "dual squeeze". In autocratizing states, protest is a defensive reaction to power grabs. In established democracies, low trust and polarization lower the threshold for taking to the streets. Both feed the global count.
- The 61% vs 45% grievance gap between insular and open respondents in Edelman suggests that polarization (insularity) amplifies economic grievance.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 Pew figure on satisfaction with democracy was collected in this pass (background: Pew Spring 2024).
- No Transparency International CPI 2025 figure was collected.

## 4. Demographic and technological drivers (youth bulge, social media, Gen Z, AI)

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 wave is concentrated in countries that combine a young population, a weak economy and an aging elite. Its organization relies on Discord, TikTok and decentralized platforms, it is often leaderless, and it uses shared symbols (the One Piece flag). Bloomberg's analysis finds that social media penetration and a low median age raise the risk that discontent turns into unrest. Carnegie cautions that youth-led protest is not new: its rate in 2017–2019 was similar or higher.

### Cited Findings
- There are an estimated 2.8 billion people aged 10–29, about a third of the world's population. Many Gen Z protest countries combine weak economies, high youth unemployment and "swelling youth populations governed by an aged political elite". — [Journal of Democracy](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/why-gen-z-is-rising/)
- Bloomberg's analysis: social media penetration and a low median age increase the risk of discontent tipping into unrest. This is a secondary summary via search; the article was not read in full. — [Bloomberg, Gen Z protest worldwide](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2025-gen-z-protest-worldwide)
- Nepal's interim government was chosen by Gen Z activists coordinating on Discord. The protests followed a social media ban and anger at corruption among the elite. — [Journal of Democracy](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/why-gen-z-is-rising/); [Vision of Humanity](https://www.visionofhumanity.org/the-rise-and-spread-of-gen-z-protests/)
- CNN describes the movement from Morocco to Madagascar as "taking digital dissent offline". — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/10/04/world/gen-z-protest-movement-explainer-intl)
- Chenoweth and Cebul: internet and social media "likely eased protest diffusion". Leaderless structures complicate negotiation (citing Tufekci 2017). Youth campaigns face more intense repression: hundreds killed in Bangladesh (2024), dozens in Kenya in 2024 and 2025, and more than 1,000 in Tanzania (late 2025). — [Journal of Democracy](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/why-gen-z-is-rising/)
- Maplecroft names social media as an intensifier of protest. — [Verisk Maplecroft](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/)
- Carnegie: the youth-led cluster "is not a new trend", and "it remains uncertain whether they will combine to constitute a substantially interconnected wave". — [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2025/12/global-protests-2025-genz-corruption-economy)
- AI: the ILO estimates about 6.1% of young workers' jobs are highly exposed to AI, which adds to economic anxiety. — [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/08/1168120). The GPI 2026 highlights AI and drones in warfare (drone attacks up more than 11,500% between 2018 and 2025), but this concerns conflict, not protest. — [IEP press release](https://www.visionofhumanity.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2026-GPI-Press-Release.pdf)
- The wave also reached aging rich countries in 2026 through students (French lycées, then elsewhere in Europe). Fortune links it to graduate underemployment. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/26/gen-z-underemployment-riots-france-cockroach-india-rebellion/)

### Inferences
- The demographic mechanism differs by region. In the Global South it is a youth bulge combined with unemployment. In aging Europe it is a smaller young generation squeezed by budgets that favour pensions and defence over education.
- Digital platforms lower the cost of mobilization and speed up diffusion across borders. Combined with the lack of leaders, this may help explain why protests spread fast but struggle to turn into lasting institutional change.

### Gaps
- No primary empirical study from 2025–2026 quantifying the role of AI-driven disinformation in triggering protests was found.
- No platform-specific data (TikTok or Discord usage among protesters) was found beyond anecdotes.

## 5. Scholarly views (Chenoweth, Carothers, Turchin, Brannen/CSIS)

### Takeaway
Scholars agree that protest is frequent but has become less effective. Chenoweth finds the success rate of nonviolent campaigns fell to about a third in the 2010s, and the Journal of Democracy piece (2026) says youth participation still doubles the chance of success. Carothers and Lee (Carnegie) stress corruption, overreach and hardship, and are sceptical that this is a single connected wave. Turchin's "elite overproduction" thesis is being revived to explain underemployed graduates, but it remains a contested hypothesis.

### Cited Findings
- **Chenoweth (background, pre-2024):** success rates of nonviolent campaigns have fallen since 2001 (NAVCO 2.1). In the 2010–2019 decade the rate dropped to about 33–34%, against a historical 51% for nonviolent campaigns and 27% for violent ones (1900–2015). In the 2010s, violent campaigns succeeded about 12% of the time. — [HKS, NAVCO 2.1](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/updating-nonviolent-campaigns-introducing-navco-21); [HKS talk transcript](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/wiener-conference-calls/erica-chenoweth); [Peace Magazine](https://peacemagazine.org/archive/volno.php?q=v36n4p02); [Portside reprint](https://portside.org/node/10773/printable/print)
- Explanations Chenoweth (2020) offers for the decline include lower average participation, a rise in tolerated violent flanks, and states learning to repress (summary). — [Pax et Bellum (Uppsala)](https://journals.uu.se/pax-et-bellum/article/view/140/147)
- **Chenoweth and Cebul (January 2026):** 80% of nonviolent campaigns in 1990–2020 had substantial youth participation (209 campaigns). Campaigns where more than 50% of frontline participants are young are about twice as likely to succeed as those with under 10%. However, one study finds no link between youth protest and later falls in youth unemployment. Outcomes are mixed: a military takeover in Madagascar, a replacement in Peru (Boluarte to Jerí) that is itself contested, and elections in Nepal (March 2026). — [Journal of Democracy](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/why-gen-z-is-rising/)
- **Carothers and Lee (Carnegie, December 2025):** corruption, antidemocratic overreach and economic hardship are the three main drivers. ### Inferences
- Many protests with low success rates create a cycle. Repeated failures and quick government turnover without structural reform (Peru, Madagascar) can keep grievances alive and feed new rounds of protest, which may partly explain why counts stay high.
