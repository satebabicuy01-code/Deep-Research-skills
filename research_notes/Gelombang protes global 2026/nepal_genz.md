## Q1. What happened in Nepal: the uprising, the March 2026 election, and the situation through 2 Nov 2026

### Takeaway
A social media ban, together with anger at corruption and "nepo kids", set off two days of protest on 8–9 Sept 2025. Security forces killed many protesters, parliament was burned, and PM K.P. Sharma Oli resigned. A Discord poll then helped choose former Chief Justice Sushila Karki as interim PM. In the 5 March 2026 election, the Rastriya Swatantra Party (RSP), with ex-Kathmandu mayor Balen Shah as its PM candidate, won an outright majority of 182 of 275 seats, wiping out the old parties. Shah became PM on 27 March 2026, and Oli was arrested the next day. By mid-2026, Gen Z protests had already begun against Shah's own government over squatter evictions and policing. Accountability for the 2025 killings is still contested.

### Inferences
- Nepal is the clearest case in the wave where the uprising led to a durable electoral result: an anti-establishment party won an outright majority within six months, and the old leaders were prosecuted. Unlike Madagascar, the army stepped in as an arbiter and not as a ruler.
- The movement also shows how fast disillusion sets in. Within four months of Shah taking office, Gen Z protesters were turning on him over evictions and policing. The cycle of the street demanding results may repeat in Nepal, though at lower intensity.
- The accountability process is fragmented: the Karki commission, the rights commission and the Prem Raj Karki panel reached conflicting conclusions. Prosecutions of security chiefs therefore look uncertain, and any prosecution of Oli is politically contested.

## Q2. Patterns and outcomes across the Gen Z wave: did movements succeed or get hijacked?

### Takeaway
From Sri Lanka in 2022 to Serbia in 2026, the wave has toppled or forced out governments in Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Mongolia, Nepal, Madagascar, Bulgaria, Peru (through Congress) and Serbia. Movements share leaderless, online organising (Discord, TikTok), the One Piece Straw Hat flag, anger at corruption and elite privilege, and triggers tied to a single policy. Outcomes vary widely:
- Elected newcomers won in Nepal and Serbia.
- Old elites returned in Bangladesh (BNP) and Peru (Keiko Fujimori).
- The military took over in Madagascar.
- Repression won in Morocco, Togo, Tanzania, Cameroon and Uganda.

In 2026 the wave spread to India and across Europe (France, Germany, Spain, Italy and others).

He says governments mainly answer with repression, which has inflamed the movements in France and India. — [Al Jazeera, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/opinions/2026/10/23/why-is-gen-z-revolting)

### Inferences
- Outcomes depend mostly on two things: whether security forces defect or refuse to shoot (Nepal, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Madagascar), and whether an organised vehicle exists to take power afterwards.
  - Where a ready-made outsider vehicle existed (Nepal's RSP plus Balen Shah, Serbia's Student List), protesters converted the street into votes.
  - Where none existed, established parties (Bangladesh's BNP, Peru's Fujimori) or the army (Madagascar) captured the transition.
- Leaderless organising on Discord is good at mobilising and at quick decisions, such as picking Nepal's interim PM. It is weak at building parties: Bangladesh's NCP was routed.
- Concession-only outcomes are common where regimes keep the security forces loyal and offer targeted fixes: Morocco's budget increase, Timor-Leste's car purchase, Paraguay's agency, France's admissions platform.

### Inferences
- The necessary conditions for a Nepal-style collapse, drawn from the cases:
  1. A large, urban, online, under-30 population with poor job prospects.
  2. Visible corruption and elite display of wealth ("nepo kids", MPs' perks).
  3. A sudden, insulting trigger that hits everyone at once: a social media ban, MPs' allowances, an exam leak, a tax bill.
  4. Lethal over-reaction by police, which multiplies turnout.
  5. Above all, a security force (usually the army) that refuses to keep defending the incumbent.
  6. Weak institutional channels for the anger: parties seen as a cartel, no imminent election.
- Applying these:
  - **Indonesia** meets most conditions (youth bulge, perks scandal, record-weak rupiah, falling approval, repeated triggers). But the military is the regime's base under ex-general Prabowo, so the more likely outcomes are repression or a reshuffle, or a military-arbitrated outcome, not a Nepal-style handover. This is moderate-high risk of severe unrest and lower risk of regime collapse.
  - **Kenya, the Philippines, Madagascar** (a second-round risk if the 2027 transition slips), **Peru under Fujimori**, **Bangladesh** (if disillusion with the BNP sets in) and the **Bloomberg list** (Angola, Ethiopia, Guatemala, Malaysia) fit the structural profile.
  - **India** has massive mobilisation, but its electoral and federal institutions, and a loyal security apparatus, make a Nepal-style collapse very unlikely.
- Successor governments born of the wave (Nepal's Shah, Serbia's Student List, Madagascar's junta) are themselves vulnerable to a "second reckoning" if they fail to deliver.

## Q4. Is a Nepal-style regime collapse plausible in Europe (France, Italy, UK)?

### Takeaway
Europe has the highest civil unrest risk scores in the world, and in October–November 2026 France, Germany, Italy and Spain saw large, sometimes violent youth protests. Even so, a Nepal-style collapse (parliament burned, government falling, an interim leader picked online) remains implausible. The evidence shows the anger flowing into concessions, snap elections and changes of government through institutional routes, not state collapse. Serbia is the closest European analogue, and even there change came at the ballot box.

### Cited Findings
- Europe holds half of Maplecroft's top 10 civil unrest risk countries, and protests are growing in size and damaging property more often, driven by fiscal pressure from defence spending, trade volatility and competitiveness worries. However, about 90% of protests are peaceful and fewer than 1% directly damage private property. — [Verisk Maplecroft, Dec 2025](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/)
- **France:**
  - 2025: the "Bloquons tout" protests coincided with the fall of the Bayrou government, its replacement by Lecornu's, and a pause in pension reform. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen_Z_protests)
  - Oct 2026: the government answered student riots with curfews, more than 2,500 arrests and negotiations with student unions (26 Oct). The main trade unions did not back further escalation, and rivals were accused of exploiting the protests ahead of the 2027 vote. — [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/26/world/france-schools-protests-macron/); [Al Jazeera, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/10/20/france-to-impose-new-curfews-where-required-after-calm-night); search summary
- **Germany:** Berlin's riot police, water cannons and armoured vehicles contained the 1 Nov 2026 riots, and the organisers distanced themselves from the violence. — [Euronews, 1 Nov 2026](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/01/germany-gen-z-protests-turn-violent-as-shops-looted)
- **Spain:** a snap election was called in response to the housing protests. — [Al Jazeera opinion](https://www.aljazeera.com/opinions/2026/10/23/why-is-gen-z-revolting)
- **Italy:** the Gaza protests of 19 Sep–29 Nov 2025 drew up to 2 million people and failed to change policy. In Oct 2026 there was looting in Milan during cost-of-living protests. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen_Z_protests)
- **Serbia and Bulgaria**, the European cases where governments fell, did so through resignation and elections: the Student List's win on 25 Oct 2026, and Progressive Bulgaria's win in the 2026 election. — [Meduza](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2026/10/26/serbia-s-student-backed-opposition-has-defeated-vucic-at-the-ballot-box-he-says-the-winners-will-impose-sanctions-on-russia-but-they-say-otherwise); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen_Z_protests)
- **UK:** in Maplecroft's top 10 for civil unrest risk in Dec 2025, but I found no 2026 Gen Z mass-mobilisation reporting for the UK. — [Verisk Maplecroft](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/)

### Inferences
- Why collapse is unlikely in France, Italy and the UK, despite high unrest scores:
  1. **Age structure:** the populations are old, so youth are a minority of voters and of the street, unlike Nepal's or Kenya's youth bulges.
  2. **Institutional outlets:** regular and snap elections, motions of no confidence, and courts let governments fall without the state falling. France has changed PM repeatedly since 2024.
  3. **Security forces:** professional, apolitical and loyal, with no history of arbitrating politics. The army as kingmaker was decisive in Nepal, Madagascar, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka.
  4. **Fiscal capacity:** governments can buy off grievances with concessions.
  5. **Lethal force:** Nepal-scale shootings are far less likely, and those shootings are what turned Nepal's protests into insurrection.
- What is plausible in Europe:
  - Repeated large, violent youth protests with high SRCC (property) losses.
  - Falls of governments or prime ministers through parliamentary means.
  - Snap elections.
  - Gains for anti-establishment parties of the left or right.
  - In weaker, more clientelist states (Serbia, Bulgaria, Albania, Georgia), a leader being forced out under protest pressure, as already happened in Serbia and Bulgaria.
- Verisk's index measures the likelihood and disruptiveness of unrest, not regime-collapse risk. It should not be read as saying France or Germany are closer to a Nepal scenario than Indonesia or Kenya.

- I found no expert analysis that directly assesses the "Nepal-style collapse in Europe" question. The reasoning above is inference.
