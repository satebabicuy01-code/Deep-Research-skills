# Where Global and European Civil Unrest Is Heading: Forecasts and Scenarios for 2027 and Beyond (as of 2 November 2026)

Context note for the writer: these notes were compiled on 2 Nov 2026. Two things drive most of the late-2026 outlook: (1) a 2026 US–Iran war / Strait of Hormuz closure that pushed up fuel and food prices, and (2) a youth-led protest wave across Europe in September–October 2026, centred on France. Many of the risk indices below were published *before* that wave (Dec 2025 – Jan 2026), so they forecast it rather than measure it. Publication dates are given throughout.

## 1. What do the 2026/2027 risk outlooks say?

### Takeaway
Every major outlook published for 2026 expected unrest to rise, and they named Europe and the US as the hotspots, not only the Global South. Verisk Maplecroft (Dec 2025) ranks Europe the highest-risk region, with 5–6 of the global top 10. The WEF puts societal polarisation and misinformation in its top five near-term risks. Eurasia Group ranks "Europe under siege" #4. Insurers report that SRCC (strikes, riots and civil commotion) has become a structurally larger loss class. Nothing published so far covers 2027 specifically: Control Risks RiskMap 2027 launches 10 Nov 2026.

### Cited Findings

It expected unrest in 2026 to be more disruptive than in 2025 — [Verisk Maplecroft, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/)

Europe holds half the top 10. Europe and the Americas are the only regions with no "lower risk" city of over 1 million people — [Verisk Maplecroft, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/)
- The US had the largest rise in average monthly protest size: about 172,000 people in 2024-Q4, about 696,000 in 2025-Q4 — [Verisk Maplecroft, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/)
- Attacks on commercial property rose in 53 countries. The biggest increases were in Kenya, Mozambique, India, Mexico and Peru. Named drivers are economic volatility, inequality, security-force conduct and corruption — [Verisk Maplecroft, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/)

### Inferences
- The forecasters were right about the direction. Maplecroft's Dec 2025 call ("2026 more disruptive than 2025", Europe the worst region) was borne out by the Sept–Oct 2026 European wave. This gives some credibility to its view that risk stays elevated through end-2027.
- The structural story is a shift in where the risk sits. Unrest is now a rich-democracy and insurance-market problem (Europe, US), not only a fragile-state one, and SRCC is being repriced as a peak-loss class.
- Inflation of 4.7% globally, weak eurozone growth and tight fiscal space (France's budget) are the classic macro preconditions for continued cost-of-living mobilisation in 2027.

## 2. Key upcoming flashpoints and calendar

### Takeaway
The flashpoints through 2027 come in three layers. The first is a dense European election calendar: France presidential (first round 18 Apr 2027), Italy (by Dec 2027), Spain, Poland and others. The second is fiscal consolidation fights over 2027 budgets, sharpest in France. The third is the post-Iran-war fuel and food price shock. In the US, the 3 Nov 2026 midterms come after a year of record protest size and heightened political violence.

### Cited Findings
**France**
- The current wave began in mid-to-late September 2026. It is driven by education, cost of living, public services and high fuel prices, with a youth/student core. Hundreds of thousands have marched, more than 3,000 shops were "sacked" (MEDEF) and Limoges Town Hall was burned. Bonds, stocks and the euro fell — [CNBC, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/20/france-protests-curfews-macron-gulf-trip.html)
- The fiscal trigger: the 2027 budget targets cutting the deficit to 5.0% of GDP from 5.4%, while borrowing costs are at multi-decade highs. The government has no parliamentary majority. The presidential election is in April 2027 — [CNBC, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/20/france-protests-curfews-macron-gulf-trip.html)
- Earlier in Oct 2026, police were accused of excessive force after a 15-year-old's hand was blown off by a stun grenade — [CNBC, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/20/france-protests-curfews-macron-gulf-trip.html)
- Arrest counts conflict across sources: "2,400 arrests" in one weekend ([Fortune, 20 Oct 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/10/20/europe-france-spain-albania-protests-young-people/)); at least 410,000 marchers and more than 1,000 arrests on 17 Oct, per the Interior Ministry ([Wikipedia: 2026 French fuel protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_French_fuel_protests)); about 300 arrests on 31 Oct against 800 at the same point the week before ([search summaries of Al Jazeera / NPR, 31 Oct–1 Nov 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/31/g-s1-151121/france-seethes-again-as-youth-led-protests-sweep-europe)). 31 Oct was the third consecutive weekend of rallies, with comparisons to the Yellow Vests — [PBS/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/a-third-weekend-of-protests-in-france-as-youth-led-rallies-sweep-europe)
- Verisk Maplecroft says the damage could exceed the 2023 Nahel riots (about 2,500 properties, about €1bn insured) — [CIR Magazine, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.cirmagazine.com/cir/c2026102001.php)
- Municipal elections (March 2026) were mixed. The Socialists held Paris and Marseille, and the RN under Bardella expanded in smaller towns — [GZERO, 14 Apr 2026](https://www.gzeromedia.com/news/analysis/has-far-right-populism-peaked-in-europe)
- 2027 polling: Odoxa (May 2026) had Bardella beating Édouard Philippe 52–48 in a second round, with Mélenchon rising to about 16% in the first round. A recent RTL-published poll (about mid-Oct 2026) has Le Pen at 35–37% in the first round, despite Mediapart allegations about antisemitic writings attributed to Bardella from 2013. Le Pen's eligibility depends on her appeal; Bardella runs if her ineligibility is upheld — [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/world/france-s-far-right-le-pen-leads-2027-presidential-poll-despite-bardella-controversy/4088722); [Wikipedia polling table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_2027_French_presidential_election)

The "Oct. 17 Movement" calls weekly Saturday demonstrations. Milan police arrested 271 people (mostly minors) over two days. Italian inflation was 4.2% in Sept against 3.8% for the eurozone — [Wikipedia: 2026 Italian unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Italian_unrest); [Fortune, 20 Oct 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/10/20/europe-france-spain-albania-protests-young-people/)
- Belgium: student protests over education funding. About 30,000 people in Brussels on 9 Oct (police figure). Greece: students have occupied Syntagma Square since 13 Oct over housing and living costs. Albania: more than a week of protests in Tirana — [search summary of RTÉ/Wikipedia "2026 European unrest"](https://www.rte.ie/news/a-european-perspective/2026/1022/1596424-europe-protests/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/20/europe-france-spain-albania-protests-young-people/)

Carnegie expects the radical right to do well in all four big tests — [Carnegie Europe, Stefan Lehne, 14 Sep 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/09/how-europes-2027-elections-could-test-eu-governance)

**US**
- Midterms on 3 Nov 2026. Princeton's Bridging Divides Initiative describes a "dangerous escalation" in the 2025 political-violence environment: high-profile assassinations and aggressive immigration operations — [Princeton BDI, 2026](https://bridgingdivides.princeton.edu/updates/2026/special-report-key-political-violence-and-resilience-trends-2025). **Global South / other**
- Kenya's 2027 presidential election: Gen Z activists launched #NikoKadi to register young voters. Turnout fell from 78% (2017) to 65% (2022) — [France 24, 5 Apr 2026](https://www.france24.com/en/asia-pacific/20260405-nepal-bangladesh-morocco-madagascar-what-have-the-gen-z-protests-achieved)

### Inferences
- France is the single most consequential flashpoint. The 2027 budget vote (late 2026), the run-up to the April 2027 election and a likely RN-led contest create several windows for renewed mobilisation and for policing incidents that can re-ignite it.
- Fuel prices are the near-term accelerant. If the Hormuz disruption persists, Italy, Spain and Portugal follow the French pattern. If prices ease (G7 reserve release), the wave is more likely to fade into electoral politics.

## 3. Scenarios: which do experts consider most likely in Europe vs the Global South?

### Takeaway
No source gives a formal probability-weighted scenario set. The expert framing points to different modal paths. In Europe the most likely path is (a)+(b): protests are partly absorbed into a 2027 electoral contest that favours the radical right, with "realignment" but no EU-existential break. That comes with an edge of (c), as order-restoration policing and protest-law tightening intensify. In the Global South, outcomes have been more varied and more severe. Leaders fell in Nepal, Madagascar and Bangladesh, but post-uprising power went to established parties or the military, and repression (Morocco) is common. Regime collapse (d) is a real but uneven risk.

### Cited Findings
**Europe**
- Carnegie: there is "a considerable risk of 2027 bringing a realignment of political forces," with Euroskeptic parties entering government. An immediate existential threat to the EU is unlikely because no major grouping seeks exit and coalitions moderate. Multiple disruptor governments could paralyse the EU. A Le Pen/Bardella win "would probably have the biggest impact." "The center can hold—but only if those committed to a liberal democratic Europe show determination" — [Carnegie Europe, 14 Sep 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/09/how-europes-2027-elections-could-test-eu-governance)
- The counter-view: Eurasia Group's Mujtaba Rahman says "the sense of a rising populist tide has ended," citing Orbán's defeat in Hungary (Tisza won over two-thirds of seats, turnout above 74%) and Meloni's referendum loss. He also warns that "a recession generated by the Iran war may bring a new wave of inchoate anger against 'elites'" — [GZERO, 14 Apr 2026](https://www.gzeromedia.com/news/analysis/has-far-right-populism-peaked-in-europe)
- The populist vote share in EU national elections rose from 10% (mid-2000s) to 23% — [Carnegie, cited via search summary](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/09/how-europes-2027-elections-could-test-eu-governance)
- Eurasia Group (Jan 2026): "Europe under siege", with the political centre collapsing in all three major powers at once — [Eurasia Group](https://www.eurasiagroup.net/issues/top-risks-2026)
- Maplecroft: further violence "is not inevitable, but protests linked to the underlying grievances" will likely persist, pressuring governments to restore order — [CNBC, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/20/france-protests-curfews-macron-gulf-trip.html)

**Global South**
- Nepal: the protests toppled Oli. Elections were held in March 2026, and protest-linked Balendra Shah became prime minister. Bangladesh: after the 2024 youth uprising, the established BNP won decisively in Feb 2026, and youth parties struggled. Established parties kept "structural advantages within the electoral arena" (Imran Ahmed, NUS). Madagascar: the army sided with protesters, ousted Rajoelina (Oct 2025) and installed Col. Randrianirina; no protest figures are in the cabinet. Morocco: more than 5,000 arrests and an 8-year sentence for a rapper; the movement is described as near "clinical death." Kenya: no leadership change, and the movement has turned to voter registration — [France 24, 5 Apr 2026](https://www.france24.com/en/asia-pacific/20260405-nepal-bangladesh-morocco-madagascar-what-have-the-gen-z-protests-achieved)
- In both Nepal and Madagascar the head of state was replaced by interim arrangements organised by military figures, and the influence of the young instigators has diminished. The Gen Z frustration wave is "only just getting started" — [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/new-atlanticist/gen-z-protests-have-spread-to-seven-countries-what-do-they-all-have-in-common/)
- CIVICUS warns that Madagascar risks joining "the growing list of African states under ongoing military rule" unless pressure continues — [CIVICUS Lens](https://lens.civicus.org/madagascars-gen-z-uprising-leads-to-uncertain-future/)
- Nepal shows elections "remained the broadly accepted mechanism" for resolving conflict — [search summary of analyses, 2026](https://www.france24.com/en/asia-pacific/20260405-nepal-bangladesh-morocco-madagascar-what-have-the-gen-z-protests-achieved)
- New Lines Institute: the protests carry "the possibility of turbulence and political collapse" as well as renewal. It cites the 3.5% participation threshold (Chenoweth) for major political change — [New Lines Institute](https://newlinesinstitute.org/sustainable-futures/the-transformative-potential-of-the-global-gen-z-movement/)
- Lethal force by security forces escalated protests in Indonesia, Nepal and Angola. Corruption drove unrest in Indonesia, Nepal and the Philippines — [Verisk Maplecroft, Dec 2025](https://www.maplecroft.com/solutions/consulting/political-risk/insights/escalating-unrest-polarisation-economic-woes-set-stage-for-disruptive-2026/)

### Inferences
- In Europe, (a) and (b) are not alternatives but a sequence. Street mobilisation in late 2026 is likely to channel into the 2027 ballot, where the beneficiary is more likely the radical right (France, Italy, Spain) or, in some cases, the far left (Mélenchon rising). (c) runs in parallel as governments prioritise "return to order."
- In the Global South, the evidence for 2024–2026 suggests a three-way split. Some cases go to elections and the old parties win (Bangladesh). Some get a protest-aligned outsider (Nepal). Some end in military custodianship (Madagascar), and others in repression (Morocco). Scenario (d) is concentrated where militaries are pivotal and elections are distant.
- A recession triggered by the Iran war is the swing variable that experts on both sides name. It would reverse the "peaked populism" thesis.

### Gaps
- No source gives explicit scenario probabilities, such as "X% chance of regime collapse."
- No ECFR or Chatham House 2026 scenario pieces were found in my searches.
- No 2026 Latin America-specific (Peru, Mexico, Brazil) or Sub-Saharan Africa-wide forecast was found beyond Maplecroft's mentions.

## 4. Historical analogies used by analysts

### Takeaway
Analysts reach most often for 1968, the Arab Spring and the Yellow Vests, and they usually reject a close analogy. They see the 2026 European wave as an economic, cost-of-living "October 2026," not a cultural-utopian "May 68 2.0," and as union-backed from the start. The Global South Gen Z wave is described as more diffuse and reform-seeking than the regime-toppling Arab Spring, although it is the largest multi-country mobilisation since 2011–14. No 2025–26 source using 1848 was found.

### Cited Findings
- 1968 vs 2026: in 1968 the trigger was cultural and value-based, during economic expansion. In 2026 it is economic and cost-of-living, amid precarity. Unions have backed the 2026 mobilisation from the start, whereas student–worker convergence in 1968 was spontaneous — [Tiempo Argentino, Oct 2026](https://www.tiempoar.com.ar/ta_article/estallido-estudiantil-en-francia-de-mayo-del-68-al-presente/)
- "Not a May 1968 2.0 but an 'October 2026'," driven by anxiety and the defence of basic rights rather than revolutionary utopia — [Brasil de Fato, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.brasildefato.com.br/2026/10/20/eclode-um-maio-de-1968-20-na-europa/)
- As in 1968, France inspired movements in Belgium, Germany and Italy. Serbia's student movement follows a different timeline — [Ahram Online, 2026](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/580249.aspx)
- Yellow Vests: AP links the student marches to the Yellow Vests movement — [PBS/AP, ~31 Oct 2026](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/a-third-weekend-of-protests-in-france-as-youth-led-rallies-sweep-europe)

## 5. What are governments doing in response?

### Takeaway
The dominant response in Europe is "order first, targeted concessions second." France has deployed curfews and Sentinelle troops, with compensation funds for businesses and fuel relief. The UK has escalated legal tools, using terrorism powers against Palestine Action supporters, with thousands arrested. In the Global South there is mass arrest and repression (Morocco), and lethal force that backfires (Indonesia, Nepal, Angola). Courts are emerging as a check: the Paris court suspended a curfew on minors, and the UK High Court ruled the Palestine Action ban unlawful.

### Inferences
- The pattern of "order first" plus liability for looters, alongside court pushback, suggests Europe is drifting toward scenario (c)-lite. Protest rights are being tested in the courts rather than abolished. The UK Supreme Court ruling (Nov 2026) is a near-term marker for how far terrorism law can be used against protest.
