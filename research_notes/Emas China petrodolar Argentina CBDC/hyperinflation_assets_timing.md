# Hyperinflation and high-inflation crises: what breaks, who wins, and asset timing (Argentina primary; Weimar, Zimbabwe, Venezuela, Turkey, Lebanon comparators)

Research date: October 20, 2026. "LATEST" marks 2026 data; everything else is historical. Many search results came back as synthesized snippets. Where I could not open the primary page, I say so. Figures that rest on one secondary source are flagged.

## 1. Argentina episodes (1989-91, 2001-02, 2018-23, Milei 2024-26): asset performance and timing

### Takeaway
In every Argentine episode, USD-measured asset prices (stocks, dollar bonds) bottomed **before or at** the policy turning point (election or plan announcement). The biggest gains came in the 6-18 months after a credible fiscal anchor appeared, not after inflation itself peaked. The Milei cycle shows the reversal risk:
- Merval in USD rose **+114.9% in 2024**.
- It then fell **-50% in 2025 up to September 9**, and jumped **20%+ in one day** after the October 2025 midterm win.
- As of **October 19, 2026**, country risk is back at **675-676 bps, the year's high**, poverty has risen again to **32.3%** (H1 2026), and inflation is stuck near **2% a month (33.4% y/y)**.

Stabilization is not a one-way trade.

### Cited Findings

**1989-1991 (hyperinflation → Plan Bonex → Convertibility)**
- **Hyperinflation dates:** May 1989 to March 1990. Monthly inflation peaked at **197% in July 1989** (per Fischer-Sahay-Végh 2002, Reinhart-Savastano 2003, Hanke-Krus 2013). Alfonsín's presidency ended early because of it. — [Wikipedia: 1989 hyperinflation in Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_hyperinflation_in_Argentina); [ResearchGate: A Brief History of Hyperinflation in Argentina](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/376700907_A_Brief_History_of_Hyperinflation_in_Argentina)
- **Annual inflation:** 1989 annual inflation is often cited as **3,079.5%**. Another source says only that inflation was "above 2,600%" in both 1989 and 1990, so the exact number varies by source. — [Wikipedia: BONEX Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BONEX_Plan); [Wikipedia: 1989 hyperinflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_hyperinflation_in_Argentina)
- **Plan Bonex (January 1990):** a **forced conversion of bank time deposits into 10-year USD Treasury bonds (BONEX)**. The bonds traded at a heavy discount, so this was "a substantial confiscation of private asset holdings." It was a partial default from the Central Bank and Treasury to the banks, which then partially defaulted on their depositors. — [Wikipedia: BONEX Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BONEX_Plan); [Dallas Fed WP 9107, "Hyperinflation and internal debt repudiation in Argentina and Brazil"](https://www.dallasfed.org/~/media/documents/research/papers/1991/wp9107.pdf)
- **Convertibility Law (1991, Cavallo):** pegged ARS 1 = USD 1. It ran from 1991 to 2002, and hyperinflation ended only when it started. **Timeline:** hyperinflation peak (July 1989) → confiscation (January 1990) → credible anchor (April 1991) took about **21 months**. — [Wikipedia: Convertibility plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convertibility_plan)

**2001-2002 (corralito, default, pesification)**
- **Corralito (December 1, 2001):** all accounts were frozen, initially for 90 days. Withdrawals were capped at **ARS 250 a week**, and only from peso accounts. **USD accounts were blocked unless the owner agreed to convert to pesos.** — [Wikipedia: Corralito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corralito)
- **Asymmetric pesification:** **USD deposits were converted at 1.40 pesos per USD, while USD loans were converted at 1:1.** At the market rate of about 2 pesos per USD, depositors lost about **USD 23bn**, and banks lost a further **USD 12bn** net. Once the final unfreezing began (April 2003), dollar depositors could recover **80-85%** of original value, for a net cost of about **USD 9bn**. — [US Congress JEC (Saxton), "Argentina's Economic Crisis: Causes and Cures"](https://cdi.mecon.gob.ar/bases/docelec/dp3063.pdf)
- **Conflicting estimate:** Wikipedia's Corralón entry says dollar savers **lost 65%** of deposited value. The difference comes from method: the 65% is measured at the trough FX, while the 80-85% reflects later recovery via CER indexation and bonds. — [Wikipedia: Corralón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corral%C3%B3n)
- **Peso collapse:** the peso lost **about 66%** of its value against the USD within months (the "devaluation ~3x" in the brief). — [Counselors of Real Estate, "Devaluation and Real Estate Values: The Argentine Case" (2004)](https://cre.org/wp-content/uploads/2004/06/Real-Estate-Issues-Devaluation-and-Real-Estate-Values-The-Argentine-Case.pdf)
- **Buenos Aires real estate:** in the late 1990s apartments were usually priced around **USD 1,000/m²**. Prices were quoted in USD even under the peg, which is why property held up in USD terms better than peso assets. — [CRE paper](https://cre.org/wp-content/uploads/2004/06/Real-Estate-Issues-Devaluation-and-Real-Estate-Values-The-Argentine-Case.pdf)
- **Merval in USD:** one economist's note says the **historic low of the Merval in USD was ~USD 57**, followed by a **~5-year bull run to USD 740** (about 13x). The note gives no date; context points to 2002. — [El Economista: "Merval: ¿esta vez será distinto?"](https://eleconomista.com.ar/negocios/merval-esta-vez-sera-distinto-n34417/amp)

**2018-2023 (currency crisis → IMF → 2019 PASO shock → 2023 near-hyperinflation)**
- **2018:** Merval in USD **-49.96%**. The peso lost almost 50%, and Macri took the **record USD 57bn IMF program**, with inflation above 40%. **2008** was the other comparable crash, at **-54.17%** in USD (global crisis plus nationalization of the AFJP pension funds). — [Elos Ayta via Poder360 (Sept 2025)](https://static.poder360.com.br/2025/09/indice-spmerval-levantamento-elos-ayta-10set2025.pdf)
- **August 12, 2019 (PASO primary shock):** the Merval fell **37.93% in pesos in one day**, its largest one-day loss on record, after Macri lost the primary. The USD loss was larger because the peso was devalued at the same time. — [Wikipedia: MERVAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MERVAL); [Diario de Cuyo, Aug 12, 2019](https://www.diariodecuyo.com.ar/noticias/sismo-en-los-mercados-con-el-dolar-a-5730-y-caida-historica-en-la-bolsa-299593.html)
- **June 2023:** the gap between NY-law GD30 and local-law AL30 bonds reached about **28.6%**, near a record. Local-law bonds carry higher confiscation risk, and the market prices it. — [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/latinoamerica/argentina/en-que-bonos-argentinos-invertir-conviene-vender-gd30-y-comprar-al30-opinan-expertos/)
- **Inflation peak:** monthly inflation hit **25.5% in December 2023**, right after Milei's devaluation (12.8% in November). It then ran **20.6% (January 2024) → 13.2% (February) → 11% (March) → 8.8% (April 2024)**, the first single-digit month since October 2023. Year-on-year inflation peaked around **289.4% (April 2024)**, and 2024 cumulative inflation was about **65%** (snippet; not cross-checked against INDEC). — [El Economista](https://eleconomista.com.ar/economia/la-inflacion-perforo-10-precios-subieron-88-abril-n73626); [Chequeado](https://chequeado.com/el-explicador/inflacion-de-abril-en-vivo-el-indec-da-a-conocer-el-dato-oficial/)
- **Poverty:** peaked at **52.9% in H1 2024**, about 6 months after the inflation peak. — [Buenos Aires Times, Sept 24, 2026](https://batimes.com.ar/news/economy/poverty-in-argentina-rose-to-323-in-first-half-of-2026.phtml)

**Milei stabilization 2024-2026**
- **2024:** **Merval +114.91% in USD.** — [Elos Ayta/Poder360](https://static.poder360.com.br/2025/09/indice-spmerval-levantamento-elos-ayta-10set2025.pdf)
- El Cronista put the 2024 Merval gain in USD at about 120-125% and **Argentine bond prices up 200%+** as country risk collapsed.
- **Mid-2024:** country risk broke below 800 (772), with long bonds up **66-79% year to date**. The drivers cited were fiscal surplus, reserve accumulation and disinflation. — [El Cronista: Merval toca máximos desde 2018](https://www.cronista.com/finanzas-mercados/merval-toca-maximos-desde-2018-y-el-riesgo-pais-ya-cae-debajo-de-los-800-puntos/); [El Cronista: ADRs que le ganaron al Merval en 2024](https://www.cronista.com/columnistas/los-seis-adrs-argentinos-que-le-ganaron-al-merval-en-2024/)
- **2025 drawdown:** by **September 9, 2025** the Merval was **-31.78% in pesos and -50.35% in USD** year to date. That was the worst of 21 indices tracked and the biggest fall since 2008. It came right after the ruling party lost the Buenos Aires province election. — [Elos Ayta/Poder360](https://static.poder360.com.br/2025/09/indice-spmerval-levantamento-elos-ayta-10set2025.pdf)
- **October 26, 2025 midterms:** La Libertad Avanza won about 41%, and its seats roughly tripled (37 → 101 deputies, 6 → 20 senators). The Merval rose **20-22% the next day**. The peso briefly gained 10%+ and closed about 3% stronger at about ARS 1,460/USD. The **Bonar/Global 2035 rose 13+ cents to a record 70.34 cents**. Before the vote, the **US Treasury signed a USD 20bn swap with the BCRA** and discussed another USD 20bn bank facility (Trump: USD 40bn total). — [CNN, Oct 27, 2025](https://www.cnn.com/2025/10/27/business/argentina-stock-market-milei-party-win); [BA Times](https://batimes.com.ar/news/argentina/markets-cheer-milei-midterms-triumph.phtml); [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/48437393); [MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/argentina-markets-soar-after-us-treasury-pledges-support-ce7d58dfd18dfe22)
- **May 2025:** GD30 traded at about **77.2 per 100 nominal**. — [Portfolio Personal reports](https://cdn1.portfoliopersonal.com/Attachs/24044.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Timing pattern, 2023-24:** USD assets bottomed **about 3-4 months before the inflation peak**. Bonds and the Merval in USD bottomed around the August-October 2023 PASO and election period. Inflation peaked in December 2023, poverty in H1 2024 (about 6 months after), and real wages around Q1 2024. Asset bottom → inflation peak → poverty peak is the typical sequence, with roughly **4-9 months between asset bottom and social bottom**.
- **The trigger was a political or fiscal regime signal, not an inflation print.** In 1991 it was the Convertibility law. In 2023 it was the Milei election and the January 2024 fiscal surplus. In October 2025 it was the midterm win plus a US Treasury backstop.
- **"Buy the stabilization" works, but the drawdowns are large.** After +115% in 2024 came -50% in 2025 (to September), and country risk is back to about 675 in October 2026. Investors need position sizing and exit rules even inside a credible program, especially around elections (2019 PASO, 2025 PBA, 2027 presidential).
- **Depositors in every Argentine crisis.** In both 1990 (Bonex) and 2001-02 (corralito/pesification), people who kept savings **inside the local banking system, even in USD**, took 15-65% losses or years of illiquidity. Those holding **USD cash or offshore assets** kept full value.
- **Real estate:** the BA USD/m² series behaves like a slow, illiquid USD asset. It falls 30-40% in USD over multi-year busts, bottoms **later** than stocks and bonds (around 2020-2023), and recovers slowly (single digits a year).

### Gaps
- **Bond prices in cents:** I could not verify exact 2023 Global/Bonar lows (the brief's "~20-30 cents"). Searches gave no sourced 2023 minimum. The brief's 60-80 cents for 2024-25 is consistent with GD30 at about 77 (May 2025) and the 2035 bond at 70.34 (October 2025).
- **Merval in USD for 1989-91 and the exact 2002 date:** not found. The 1991 Convertibility stock boom and the bank/energy stock (Galicia, Pampa) troughs in 2002 and 2023 in USD are unverified.
- **BA m² history:** I found no sourced series for 2001-02, the 2018 peak (often cited around USD 2,500+), or the 2023 trough. Only the 1990s benchmark (USD 1,000) and 2026 levels are sourced. Mortgage credit return (2024-25 UVA mortgages) was not verified in this session.
- **USD cash, gold and CEDEAR returns by episode, and USD wage series:** not quantified with sources in this session.
- **Riesgo país history:** I did not find a sourced figure for the late-2023 peak or the 2025 low (around 500-550).

## 2. Weimar Germany 1921-1923: real-asset behavior, winners and losers, Rentenmark aftermath

### Takeaway
German stocks lost about 97% in real terms by October 1922, but that was **the low**: they recovered in real terms during the final hyperinflation year. Debtors, including the state, were wiped clean, until the 1925 revaluation law partly restored mortgages (25%) and some state bonds (2.5%). The Rentenmark (November 16, 1923) stabilized the currency within weeks, and the stabilization was followed by a wave of bankruptcies.

### Cited Findings
- **Paper marks per USD:** about 4.2 (1914) → 7.9 (1918) → **48 (late 1919)** → **90 (H1 1921)** → **320 (H1 1922)** → **7,400 (December 1922)** → **about 4.2 trillion (November 1923)**.
- **Key events:**
  - London Schedule: 132bn gold marks of reparations (May 1921).
  - Reichsbank started buying FX "at any price" (August 1921).
  - Rathenau's assassination (June 1922) tipped the mark into hyperinflation.
  - The Ruhr was occupied (January 1923).
- **Prices:** a Berlin loaf cost about 160 marks at the end of 1922 and **about 200 billion in late 1923**. — [Wikipedia: Hyperinflation in the Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation_in_the_Weimar_Republic)
- **Stabilization:**
  - Monetary decrees: October 13-15, 1923. Schacht became currency commissioner on November 12, 1923.
  - The **Rentenmark came in on November 16, 1923, cutting "twelve zeros"** (1 Rentenmark = 1 trillion paper marks).
  - By 1924, USD 1 bought about 4.2 Rentenmark, the pre-war parity.
  - The Reichsmark law followed on August 30, 1924, and the **Dawes Plan** resumed reparations in 1924 without renewed inflation. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation_in_the_Weimar_Republic)
- **Government:** inflation greatly reduced domestic war debt relative to revenue, and the state repaid domestic debt in worthless marks. Debts were "effectively wiped out." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation_in_the_Weimar_Republic)
- **Revaluation (Aufwertung):** the law of **July 16, 1925** restored **mortgages held at least five years to 25% of face value**, and **some government bonds to 2.5%**. The Reichsgericht upheld it (November 4, 1925). Reinstating debts and resuming effective taxation reportedly triggered a wave of corporate bankruptcies (marked "citation needed" on Wikipedia). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation_in_the_Weimar_Republic)
- **Debt-inflation channel:** Brunnermeier, Correia, Luck, Verner and Zimmermann find that inflation **sharply reduced real debt burdens and firm bankruptcies** for leveraged firms, and that balance sheets were later restated in Goldmarks. They cite Bresciani-Turroni (1937) on wild stock swings and money illusion (investors confusing nominal gains with real ones). — [Princeton: "The Debt-Inflation Channel of the German Hyperinflation"](https://markus.scholar.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf2651/files/documents/Hyperinflation_Weimar_Germany.pdf); [Banca d'Italia version](https://www.bancaditalia.it/pubblicazioni/altri-atti-seminari/2022/Verner_paper.pdf)
- **Real stock values** (secondary blog citing Fergusson's *When Money Dies*):
  - **About -66%** from 1913 to the end of the war.
  - **About -80% by 1921.**
  - **About -97% by October 1922**, which it calls the low for shareholders.
  - Real prices then rose while the mark kept collapsing. — [HardMoneyHistory](https://www.hardmoneyhistory.com/weimar-hyperinflation/) (secondary; verify against Fergusson or Bresciani-Turroni)
- **Landlords:** a 100,000-mark mortgage became worth a fraction of a cent by November 1923. Rent controls held rents far below inflation, so landlords were asset-rich and income-poor, while mortgagors won. — [The Timeless Investor (Substack)](https://thetimelessinvestor.substack.com/p/the-weimar-landlord) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **Equities bottomed about 13 months before the currency peak** (October 1922 vs November 1923). That is the clearest historical case of stocks leading. Once prices index to FX, the real claims of equity on physical capital reassert themselves, even while the currency is still collapsing.
- **Leveraged owners of real assets** (industrialists such as Stinnes, mortgaged homeowners) were the biggest winners. The partial 25% mortgage revaluation in 1925 shows **post-crisis legal clawbacks can claw back part of debtor gains**.

### Gaps
- **Not verified:** Hauszinssteuer (the 1924 house-interest tax that captured landlords' debt windfall), Stinnes' empire specifics, post-stabilization unemployment (1924 "stabilization crisis"), and the Beer Hall Putsch (November 1923). These are well known, but no source was retrieved here. Bresciani-Turroni's real stock index figures were not obtained directly.

## 3. Comparison cases: Zimbabwe 2007-09, Venezuela 2016-21, Turkey 2021-24, Lebanon 2019-24

### Takeaway
In each case, people improvised hard-currency pricing: the Old Mutual implied rate in Zimbabwe, transactional dollarization in Venezuela, household gold in Turkey, and "lollar" discounts in Lebanon. Locally listed equities and property served as **imperfect stores of value for trapped money**, and they **deflated in hard-currency terms once the trap opened**. Each recovery started with dollarization (Zimbabwe 2009, Venezuela 2019) or an orthodox policy pivot (Turkey June 2023). Lebanon had no plan for years, and its depositor losses stayed unresolved.

### Cited Findings

**Zimbabwe**
- **Old Mutual Implied Rate (OMIR):** Old Mutual was listed in both Harare and London, so the ratio of its two prices gave a market ZWD/GBP (→ USD) rate. Through 2008 the OMIR was **used as the benchmark for pricing all ZSE stocks**. Volatility was unprecedented, and there was evidence of share-price manipulation by major players. — [Cato Journal (Hanke & Kwok), "On the Measurement of Zimbabwe's Hyperinflation"](https://www.cato.org/sites/cato.org/files/serials/files/cato-journal/2009/5/cj29n2-8.pdf); [Wits thesis: Effect of dollarization on ZSE performance](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/216aca68-485e-45fc-8dbb-1e8caacf6664/content)
- **Competing exchange rates:** in August 2008 there were six (cash, transfer, UN, Old Mutual, interbank, government). — [Wits thesis](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/216aca68-485e-45fc-8dbb-1e8caacf6664/content)
- **Dollarization (early 2009):** official multi-currency use, with the rand as reference. ZSE trading moved to hard currency, credited with restoring "some degree of sanity." — [Cato Journal (Noko), "Dollarization: The Case of Zimbabwe" 2011](https://www.cato.org/sites/cato.org/files/serials/files/cato-journal/2011/5/cj31n2-9.pdf); [IMF Country Report 09/139](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/scr/2009/cr09139.pdf)
- **Zimbabwe hyperinflated again** less than a decade later (around 2019), so the dollarization anchor was abandoned and the cycle repeated. — [Hanke & Bostrom, JHU](https://sites.krieger.jhu.edu/iae/files/2018/07/Zimbabwe-Hyperinflates-Again-Hanke-Bostrom-.pdf); [Cato commentary](https://www.cato.org/commentary/zimbabwe-hyperinflates-again-entering-record-books-second-time-less-decade)

**Venezuela**
- **Monthly inflation:** fell from **almost 200% (January 2019) to 31% (May 2019)**. It rekindled after July 2019, with weekly rates around 10%. — [PIIE Policy Brief 19-13](https://www.piie.com/sites/default/files/documents/pb19-13.pdf)
- **2019 controls:** Maduro lifted controls in 2019, which partly tamed inflation until May 2020. — [Wikipedia: Hyperinflation in Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation_in_Venezuela)
- **Ecoanalítica (November 2019):** de facto transactional dollarization. Foreign currency was used in **86% of purchases in Maracaibo** and 59% in Barquisimeto and Lecherías, while more than 46% of purchases nationally were still in bolívars. Maduro had called dollarization "unconstitutional" in 2018 and tolerated it by late 2019. — [Ecoanalítica WR 39-2019](https://www.ecoanalitica.net/wp-content/uploads/WR_39_2019_14_11_eng.pdf)

**Turkey**
- **Policy pivot after the May 2023 election:** Şimşek became finance minister and Erkan central bank governor (June 2023). The policy rate went from **8.5% (May 2023) to 50% (March 2024)**, and yields fell from about 26% in 2023 to about 15% in 2024 as stabilization took hold. The OSW calls the process a "turbulent stabilisation." — [OSW, July 2025](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/analyses/2025-07-09/turbulent-stabilisation-turkeys-economy-under-simseks-supervision)
- **Household gold:** estimated at **up to USD 400bn, more than 33% of GDP**. A 2024 proposal would have let people swap gold into the central bank to fund 20-30% of home purchases. — [AGBI, October 2024](https://www.agbi.com/real-estate/2024/10/turkeys-real-estate-proposal-turn-gold-into-bricks/)
- **Istanbul housing (single commentary source):** prices rose about **80% in lira in 2022**. By the 12 months to April 2026 they had flattened in nominal lira and **fallen 15-20% in USD**. The same source says the BIST-100 in USD was among the worst EM performers in 2022-23. — [Middle East Insider, April 2026](https://themiddleeastinsider.com/2026/04/20/turkish-lira-april-2026-erdogan-rate-gamble/) (secondary, unverified)
- BIS real residential property price series for Turkey: [FRED QTRR628BIS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/QTRR628BIS) (values not retrieved).

**Lebanon**
- **"Lollars" (USD trapped in banks)** traded via cheques at about **13 cents on the dollar (-87%)**. The government considered writing off about **USD 60bn** of FX deposits. A draft plan would have protected up to USD 100k per account and converted the rest into bank shares, written it off, or converted it at non-market lira rates. — [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1303927/how-solidere-shares-became-the-only-game-in-town.html)
- As of the 2023 State Department review, the haircut plan had not been implemented. — [US State Dept 2023 Investment Climate Statement](https://www.state.gov/reports/2023-investment-climate-statements/lebanon)
- **Solidere:** shares rose about **220% from late 2019 to mid-2020** as depositors used trapped cheques to buy them. An analyst attributes the rise to escaping bank deposits, not fundamentals; the company lost USD 32m in 2023. — [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/real-estate/456017-why-lebanons-real-estate-sector-is-bucking-the-economic-malaise); [Executive Magazine](https://www.executive-magazine.com/business-all/finance/betting-on-lebanon-the-high-stakes-game-of-bonds-stocks-real-estate-and-bank-rates)
- **Real estate (Bank Audi):** prices in lollars rose **300-400%** in some cases, while **fresh-dollar prices fell, by no more than about 30% on average** in some regions. By 2022 the market was cash-only and fully in fresh dollars, and cheque payments had been phased out. — [Bank Audi real estate report](https://pwstg02.blob.core.windows.net/pwfiles/Library/Files/62fae88a-5758-4a3e-b9c4-257334aa4af3.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Trapped-money rule:** when deposits are frozen but can be spent on local assets (Lebanon's Solidere and property bought by cheque, Zimbabwe's ZSE, and arguably Argentina's CEDEAR and stock buying under the 2019-23 cepo), those assets **inflate in the trapped unit while falling in fresh hard currency**. Measure performance in **freely transferable USD** (Lebanon's "fresh" dollars, Argentina's CCL or MEP rate), never in the trapped unit.
- **Turkey without a freeze:** households used **physical gold** as the main hedge, and it worked because Turkey never froze accounts or confiscated gold. Real estate was a strong lira hedge in 2022, then gave back 15-20% in USD once real rates turned positive. **Property tends to underperform after the stabilization pivot**, because high real interest rates crush property valuations.

### Gaps
- **Venezuela:** I found no sourced data on the Caracas Stock Exchange in USD (it was famously the "best-performing" index in nominal terms) or on Caracas real estate USD prices.
- **Turkey:** exact BIST-100 USD returns for 2021, 2022 and 2023 and the timing of the BIST USD bottom (likely around late 2021 or around the June 2023 pivot) were not retrieved from a primary source.
- **Zimbabwe:** ZSE USD-equivalent levels around dollarization were not quantified.
- **Lebanon:** the status of the 2024-26 deposit-recovery and bank-resolution laws was not verified.

## 4. Winners vs. losers taxonomy

### Takeaway
The transfers are consistent across all six cases:
- **Winners:** borrowers in local currency (including the state, through the inflation tax), owners of real and hard assets held **outside the banking system**, exporters and people with USD income, and those who could arbitrage multiple exchange rates.
- **Losers:** local-currency savers and bondholders, pensioners, fixed-wage workers, and **depositors of any currency inside a captured banking system**.

The middle class is hit hardest because it holds bank deposits and pensions, not hard assets or debt.

### Cited Findings
- **Debtors and the state win:**
  - In Weimar, inflation wiped out domestic war debt and private debts. Firm bankruptcies fell during the inflation, and creditors were only partly restored in 1925 (mortgages 25%, some state bonds 2.5%). — [Wikipedia: Weimar hyperinflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation_in_the_Weimar_Republic); [Brunnermeier et al.](https://markus.scholar.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf2651/files/documents/Hyperinflation_Weimar_Germany.pdf)
  - In Argentina 2002, borrowers with USD loans repaid them at **1 peso per USD** while the market rate was about 2 (later about 3.5). This was a direct transfer from depositors and banks to debtors. — [JEC/Saxton](https://cdi.mecon.gob.ar/bases/docelec/dp3063.pdf)
- **Depositors lose, even in USD:**
  - Argentina 1990 (Bonex): forced conversion to discounted bonds. — [Wikipedia: BONEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BONEX_Plan)
  - Argentina 2002: USD 23bn gross, about 9bn net after recovery, with 20-65% value losses depending on method. — [JEC](https://cdi.mecon.gob.ar/bases/docelec/dp3063.pdf); [Wikipedia: Corralón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corral%C3%B3n)
  - Lebanon: lollars worth about 13 cents. — [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1303927/how-solidere-shares-became-the-only-game-in-town.html)
- **Poor and young lose most:** in Argentina, poverty peaked at **52.9% (H1 2024)**. In H1 2026 child poverty (ages 0-14) was **33.7%** versus **14.8% for ages 65+**, which suggests pension indexation has protected the elderly better than wage earners in the current phase. — [BA Times](https://batimes.com.ar/news/economy/poverty-in-argentina-rose-to-323-in-first-half-of-2026.phtml)
- **Landlords under rent control** lose income (Weimar), even though property values in hard currency survive. — [Timeless Investor](https://thetimelessinvestor.substack.com/p/the-weimar-landlord)
- **Holders of liquid hard assets outside the system win:** in Turkey, household gold of up to USD 400bn (more than 33% of GDP) served as the de facto savings system. — [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/real-estate/2024/10/turkeys-real-estate-proposal-turn-gold-into-bricks/)

### Inferences
- **Disinflation phase:** a second round of winners and losers appears. Peso carry traders and peso-bond holders win, because real rates turn positive and the currency appreciates in real terms. Those who stayed fully in USD cash lose in purchasing power, because Argentina became expensive in USD in 2024-25. Exporters and the tradable sector (manufacturing jobs) lose. Weimar 1924-25 saw a similar bankruptcy wave when credit got tight.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures in this session for real wage losses (e.g., Argentine private real wages in 2023-24, or Weimar real wages), pensioner real losses, or exporter windfalls.

## 5. Timing re-entry: phase-by-phase crisis playbook and bottom indicators

### Takeaway
Hard-currency asset bottoms cluster at the **moment of maximum political and policy uncertainty just before a regime change**:
- Weimar stocks: October 1922.
- Argentina: PASO and election, August-November 2023.
- Turkey: around the May-June 2023 election and pivot.
- Argentina 2025: Buenos Aires province election shock, then the October midterm.

Assets bottom **months before** the monthly inflation peak and the poverty or real-wage trough. The best signal is a **credible fiscal anchor plus an external backstop** (IMF or US Treasury), together with the parallel FX gap compressing and the country-risk spread peaking.

### Cited Findings (timing anchors)
- **Weimar:** real stock low October 1922 → hyperinflation peak November 1923 → Rentenmark November 16, 1923 → Dawes 1924 → revaluation and bankruptcies 1925. Stocks led the currency peak by about **13 months**. — [HardMoneyHistory (secondary)](https://www.hardmoneyhistory.com/weimar-hyperinflation/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation_in_the_Weimar_Republic)
- **Argentina 1989-91:** monthly peak July 1989 (197%) → Bonex confiscation January 1990 → hyperinflation ends March 1990 → Convertibility April 1991. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_hyperinflation_in_Argentina); [Convertibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convertibility_plan)
- **Argentina 2001-02:** freeze December 2001 → peso about -66% in 2002 → final unfreezing April 2003. The Merval USD low (~USD 57) was followed by a **5-year rally to USD 740**. — [JEC](https://cdi.mecon.gob.ar/bases/docelec/dp3063.pdf); [El Economista](https://eleconomista.com.ar/negocios/merval-esta-vez-sera-distinto-n34417/amp)
- **Argentina 2023-24:** monthly CPI peak December 2023 (25.5%) → single digits by April 2024 (8.8%) → poverty peak H1 2024 (52.9%). The 2024 Merval was +115% in USD and bonds +200%. — [El Economista](https://eleconomista.com.ar/economia/la-inflacion-perforo-10-precios-subieron-88-abril-n73626); [BA Times](https://batimes.com.ar/news/economy/poverty-in-argentina-rose-to-323-in-first-half-of-2026.phtml); [Elos Ayta](https://static.poder360.com.br/2025/09/indice-spmerval-levantamento-elos-ayta-10set2025.pdf); [El Cronista](https://www.cronista.com/columnistas/los-seis-adrs-argentinos-que-le-ganaron-al-merval-en-2024/)
- **Argentina 2025:** Merval -50% in USD to September 9 → US USD 20bn swap → midterm win on October 26 → +20-22% in one day, with the 2035 bond at a record 70.34. — [Elos Ayta](https://static.poder360.com.br/2025/09/indice-spmerval-levantamento-elos-ayta-10set2025.pdf); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/10/27/business/argentina-stock-market-milei-party-win)
- **Turkey:** rate pivot from June 2023 (8.5% → 50% by March 2024), then lower yields and higher equities. — [OSW](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/analyses/2025-07-09/turbulent-stabilisation-turkeys-economy-under-simseks-supervision)
- **Zimbabwe:** dollarization in early 2009 restored the exchange's function. — [Noko, Cato Journal](https://www.cato.org/sites/cato.org/files/serials/files/cato-journal/2011/5/cj31n2-9.pdf)

### Inferences: phase playbook (synthesized from the cases above)

| Phase | Typical duration | What breaks | Asset behavior (hard-currency terms) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Pre-crisis warning** (twin deficits, monetization, multiple FX rates or a widening parallel gap, reserves falling, local- vs foreign-law bond spread widening such as Argentina's 28% GD30/AL30 gap in June 2023) | 1-3 years (Weimar 1919-21; Argentina 2018-22; Turkey 2018-21) | Real wages start eroding; credit shortens; FX controls begin | Stocks and bonds fall in USD (Argentina 2018 Merval -50% in USD); real estate is flat to down in USD; USD cash and gold outperform |
| **2. Acceleration** (monthly inflation over 10-20%; parallel gap of 100%+) | 6-18 months (Weimar June 1922 to mid-1923; Argentina 2023; Venezuela 2016-18) | Imports, pensions, peso deposits and fixed salaries; deposit flight; bank freeze risk peaks | **Equities often bottom here in real terms** (Weimar October 1922; Argentina August-October 2023). Local-currency bonds go to zero; USD bonds trade at 20-30 cents |
| **3. Peak or collapse** (monthly peak; confiscation event: Bonex, corralito, pesification, lollar haircut; regime or government change) | 1-6 months | Bank deposits (frozen or converted), social order (riots December 2001), the presidency (Alfonsín 1989, De la Rúa 2001) | Assets already off the lows. **Depositors take losses here.** Trapped-money assets (Solidere, ZSE) spike in trapped units |
| **4. Stabilization** (new anchor: Rentenmark, Convertibility, dollarization, fiscal surplus plus crawl, IMF or US backstop) | 3-12 months to single-digit monthly inflation (Weimar: weeks; Argentina 2024: about 4 months to single digits) | Activity collapses further (stabilization recession); bankruptcies (Weimar 1924-25); poverty peaks 0-6 months after the inflation peak | **Biggest gains**: Merval +115% in USD (2024), bonds +200%, Merval 13x over 5 years after 2002 |
| **5. Recovery or relapse** | Years. Relapse is common: Zimbabwe re-hyperinflated around 2019; Argentina's 2025 -50% drawdown; 2026 country risk back at 675 and poverty rising again | Real exchange-rate overvaluation hurts tradables; election risk returns | Real estate recovers late and slowly (BA +1.6% y/y in 2026); equities are volatile; **re-test risk around elections and financing walls** |

- **Practical bottom indicators** (from the cases):
  1. A political or regime change, or a credible anchor law.
  2. A primary fiscal surplus actually delivered.
  3. An external backstop (IMF program; US Treasury swap in October 2025).
  4. Parallel FX gap compressing.
  5. Country risk rolling over from its peak.
  6. Monthly CPI turning down for 2-3 consecutive prints.
  7. Reserves starting to grow.
- **Typical lags:**
  - Asset bottom to monthly inflation peak: **0-13 months** (stocks lead).
  - Inflation peak to poverty or real-wage trough: **about 3-6 months**.
  - Real estate in USD lags equities by **1-3 years**.

- No sourced daily or monthly series aligning riesgo país peaks, parallel-gap peaks and reserve turns with asset bottoms for 2001-02 and 2023. The lags above are inferred from the case anchors, not computed from data.

## 6. Lessons for an individual EM investor (e.g., Indonesia)

### Takeaway
- **The biggest risk is not inflation itself but confiscation inside the local financial system**: freezes, forced conversion to bonds, asymmetric pesification, and lollar haircuts. These hit USD deposits held in local banks too.
- **Assets that protected value** were held outside the captured system: offshore brokerage, physical USD cash, physical gold (Turkey), and hard assets with USD pricing.
- **Local equities are a re-entry asset, not a hiding place.** They fall 50-97% in hard currency during the run-up, but deliver the largest rebounds once a credible anchor appears.

### Cited Findings
- **Local-bank USD deposits are not safe in a systemic crisis:** Argentina 2001 (USD accounts blocked unless converted, pesified at 1.40 versus a market rate of about 2), Argentina 1990 (time deposits swapped for discounted Bonex), Lebanon (lollars at about 13 cents). — [Wikipedia: Corralito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corralito); [JEC](https://cdi.mecon.gob.ar/bases/docelec/dp3063.pdf); [Wikipedia: BONEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BONEX_Plan); [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1303927/how-solidere-shares-became-the-only-game-in-town.html)
- **Jurisdiction matters for sovereign bonds:** NY-law GD30 traded about 28% above the otherwise-identical local-law AL30 in June 2023. — [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/latinoamerica/argentina/en-que-bonos-argentinos-invertir-conviene-vender-gd30-y-comprar-al30-opinan-expertos/)
- **Household gold** held outside banks served as Turkey's main inflation hedge (up to USD 400bn). — [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/real-estate/2024/10/turkeys-real-estate-proposal-turn-gold-into-bricks/)
- **Measure in hard currency, not local:** Merval 2025 was -32% in pesos but -50% in USD. — [Elos Ayta](https://static.poder360.com.br/2025/09/indice-spmerval-levantamento-elos-ayta-10set2025.pdf)
- **Local real estate in a cash-only, dollarized market** fell about 30% or less in fresh USD in Lebanon. It is illiquid but survived far better than deposits (-87%). — [Bank Audi report](https://pwstg02.blob.core.windows.net/pwfiles/Library/Files/62fae88a-5758-4a3e-b9c4-257334aa4af3.pdf)

### Inferences (informational, not personal advice)
- **Phase 1-2:**
  - Shift savings from local deposits into (a) offshore custody (foreign broker or bank), (b) a slice of physical gold and physical USD, and (c) real assets without leverage.
  - Avoid local-law sovereign and corporate bonds and long local-currency deposits.
  - Fixed-rate local-currency debt on a productive real asset is the classic winning liability. Caveat: post-crisis revaluation can partly claw back gains (Weimar mortgages restored at 25%).
- **Phase 3:**
  - Watch for confiscation signals: withdrawal caps, deposit conversion decrees, multiple official FX rates, a widening local-law vs foreign-law bond spread.
  - Liquidity outside the system is what lets you buy the bottom.
- **Phase 4:** rotate part of the hard-currency reserve into local equities (banks and energy have the highest beta) and foreign-law sovereign USD bonds **once a fiscal anchor plus an external backstop appear**. Do not wait for inflation to reach single digits; by then the bulk of the bond and equity re-rating is done (Argentina 2024).
- **Phase 5:**
  - Take profits before election cycles and financing walls (Argentina 2019 PASO -38% in a day; 2025 -50% in USD; October 2026 country risk back at 675 ahead of the 2027 presidential election).
  - Real estate is a late-cycle, slow asset. Buy it in phases 4-5 when mortgage credit returns, not as a crisis trade.
- **Indonesia-specific relevance:**
  - The 1997-98 rupiah crisis (outside this research's scope) is the local precedent.
  - The same playbook applies: bank-system concentration risk, IDR-denominated deposits, and the value of offshore and physical diversification.
