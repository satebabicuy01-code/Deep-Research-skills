## (a) Mechanics: how the petrodollar and the wider dollar system actually work

### Takeaway
The "petrodollar" was an informal 1974 bargain: US security guarantees and secret preferential access to Treasury auctions in exchange for Saudi surpluses being recycled into US assets. No treaty required oil to be priced in dollars. Today oil invoicing is a small part of dollar dominance. The real pillars are a ~$14.7tn offshore dollar credit system (eurodollars), dollar invoicing of trade, 89% of FX turnover, ~57% of reserves, Treasuries as global collateral, and Fed swap lines as the backstop.

### Cited Findings

**1974 arrangements (what they were and were not)**
- [FACT] No known formal deal ever stipulated that Saudi Arabia must sell oil only in US dollars. In practice, Saudi Arabia has conducted its oil deals in dollars for decades without being formally bound to. — [RFA fact check](https://www.rfa.org/english/news/afcl/afcl-us-saudi-oil-deal-07082024043619.html)
- [FACT] PolitiFact rated the "petrodollar deal expired" claim false. Experts knew of no such US-Saudi agreement, and dollar oil sales are expected to continue. — [PolitiFact, 20 Jun 2024](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2024/jun/20/facebook-posts/the-us-dollar-will-continue-to-be-used-for-oil-sal/)
- [FACT] The 1974 framework, which grew out of the June 1974 Joint Commission on Economic Cooperation, tied Saudi oil revenue and US security cooperation together. None of it was a binding requirement to price oil in dollars. — [RFA](https://www.rfa.org/english/news/afcl/afcl-us-saudi-oil-deal-07082024043619.html); [PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2024/jun/20/facebook-posts/the-us-dollar-will-continue-to-be-used-for-oil-sal/)
- [TRAINING-KNOWLEDGE] Treasury Secretary William Simon's 1974 mission produced a secret "add-on" arrangement. Saudi Arabia could buy Treasuries outside regular auctions, and the US Treasury hid Saudi holdings within an "oil exporters" aggregate until 2016. The 1974–75 OPEC price shock produced the original "petrodollar recycling": surpluses were deposited in US and London banks (the eurodollar market) and re-lent to developing countries, which laid the ground for the 1980s LatAm debt crisis. — [Bloomberg, "The Untold Story Behind Saudi Arabia's 41-Year U.S. Debt Secret" (2016)](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2016-05-30/the-untold-story-behind-saudi-arabia-s-41-year-u-s-debt-secret) (not re-fetched this session)

Total turnover was **$9.6tn/day**, up 28% from 2022. — [BIS press release, 30 Sep 2025](https://www.bis.org/press/p250930.htm); [BIS statistics](https://www.bis.org/statistics/rpfx25_fx.htm); [WEF summary](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/10/dollar-still-dominates-forex-trading-2025-finance-news)

### Inferences
- **Mechanical chain** ([MAINSTREAM] textbook synthesis, by the researcher). Oil exporter earns dollars → sterilizes them through its SAMA/sovereign fund → buys Treasuries, agencies, US equities and bank deposits → those dollars become collateral and funding in the eurodollar and repo market → non-US banks and corporates borrow dollars offshore → their dollar liabilities create structural *demand* for dollars, especially in stress. That demand forces the Fed to act as global backstop through:
  - standing swap lines with the ECB, BoJ, BoE, SNB and BoC
  - temporary lines with others
  - the FIMA repo facility, which lets central banks repo Treasuries for dollars without selling them [TRAINING-KNOWLEDGE].
  The *liability* side (the ~$14.7tn debt that must be serviced in dollars) is what locks in the system. The *invoicing* of a barrel of oil does not.
- **Triffin dilemma** [MAINSTREAM theory]. To supply the world with safe dollar assets, the issuer must run persistent external deficits and accumulate net foreign liabilities, which slowly erodes confidence in the asset. Modern versions, from Pettis and from Miran's paper, argue the reserve role *forces* US deficits and an overvalued dollar onto the US.
- **"Exorbitant privilege"** (Giscard d'Estaing, 1960s). The US borrows cheaply in its own currency, earns a positive return differential on its net foreign position, and can impose financial sanctions. The 2022 Russia reserve freeze showed the last of these at full strength.
- Oil is only ~2–3% of world GDP by trade value [TRAINING-KNOWLEDGE]. Even if *all* oil were invoiced in yuan, exporters would still have to put the proceeds somewhere. The binding constraint is the lack of a deep, open, convertible yuan asset market, not the currency of the invoice.

## (b) What has actually changed through October 2026: myth vs fact, and evidence of erosion

### Takeaway
The headline "petrodollar expired June 2024" is **false**. But real, measurable erosion is occurring at the margins:
- dollar reserve share ~56.7% (Q2 2026), down from 72% in 2001
- gold surpassing Treasuries in official reserves (driven by valuation)
- yuan oil settlement corridors expanded by the 2026 Iran war
- Russia–China trade ~90–95% in ruble/yuan (per Russian officials)
- mBridge ~$55–69bn cumulative settled
- Indonesian LCT volumes up ~260–310% y/y in 2026

US-side shocks (the 2025 tariffs, the April 2025 bond/dollar sell-off, the Moody's downgrade, $38–40tn debt, Fed-independence pressure) have weakened the *safe-haven* narrative. Even so, the dollar's transactional dominance (89% of FX) is intact.

### Inferences
- **Two distinct trends** must not be conflated:
  1. *Safe-haven erosion.* In April 2025 the dollar fell while yields rose, an EM-like pattern. In 2025 gold overtook Treasuries in reserves. US fiscal and Fed-independence risk is now being priced.
  2. *Transactional/network dominance*, which remains unchanged: 89% of FX and $14.7tn of offshore debt, still growing 7% y/y.
  De-dollarization so far is mostly reserve diversification into gold and euro plus sanctions-driven bypass corridors (Russia, Iran, China). It is not a systemic switch.
- The 2026 Iran war is a two-sided signal. COFER's dollar share *rose* in Q1 2026 (a flight to safety) and the dollar rallied after the Fed hike. At the same time, Hormuz turned yuan settlement into a physical-access condition for the first time. That ties energy security to the yuan for buyers that depend on Gulf oil, which is precisely the "petro" link the 1974 bargain was built on.
- The Q2 2026 euro gain from "active purchasing" fits euro credit growing faster than dollar credit to EMDEs in BIS data. The euro, not the yuan, is the main near-term beneficiary of reserve diversification.
- A Fed *hiking* into an energy shock with 10y ~5.3% while debt is ~$40tn raises interest-cost and fiscal-dominance risk. That is the channel most relevant to a disorderly scenario.

## (c) Counter-evidence: why the dollar remains dominant

### Takeaway
No challenger combines the deep, open, legally predictable capital markets, convertible currency, and global safe-asset supply that dollar dominance requires. The yuan is ~2% of reserves and ~3% of SWIFT payments. The euro is ~20% of reserves but has only ~$700bn of joint debt versus $28tn+ in Treasuries.

### Inferences
- **China's dilemma** [MAINSTREAM view of Pettis and Eichengreen, from training]. Internationalizing the yuan requires open capital accounts and running current-account deficits to supply yuan assets abroad. That is incompatible with China's export- and investment-led model and its capital controls. The yuan therefore spreads mainly as a *settlement/invoicing* currency in sanctioned or China-centric corridors, not as a *reserve/store-of-value* currency.
- **Network effects and "dominant currency pricing"** (Gopinath) mean the switching costs are coordination problems. Erosion will be slow unless a discrete shock forces coordination: confiscation risk, a US default or haircut, or capital controls.

## (d) US strategy response: stablecoins, Mar-a-Lago accord, tariffs, swap lines

### Takeaway
Washington's 2025–26 strategy has two parts. It *extends* dollar demand through regulated dollar stablecoins (the GENIUS Act requires T-bill/cash backing, so stablecoins are now a top-20 Treasury holder at ~$300bn+ market cap). It also *coerces*, through tariff threats on BRICS and Miran's "Mar-a-Lago accord" ideas to make foreigners pay for the reserve role. The two are in tension: one deepens the privilege, the other taxes it.

### Cited Findings
- [FACT] The GENIUS Act was signed July 2025. It requires 100% reserves in dollars, T-bills or short-duration equivalents, monthly disclosure, and AML/KYC compliance, and puts issuers above $10bn under Federal Reserve oversight. — [FinTelegram](https://fintelegram.com/stablecoins-became-a-top-20-us-debt-holder-is-treasury-risk-now-tokenized/); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/357872/us-stablecoin-market-could-exceed-2-trillion-projection-by-end-of-2028-thinks-treasury-secretary-bessent)

  - record ~**$322–323bn in May 2026**
  - ~$308–318bn in Aug 2026
  - BIS conservative estimate ~$270bn in Mar 2026
  — [Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/the-international-role-of-the-u-s-dollar-2025-edition-20250718.html); [Whale Alert](https://whale-alert.io/stories/a4b9010006e94f/Stablecoin-market-value-hits-record-322-billion-surpassing-FX-reserves-of-95-countries); [Spark tracker](https://www.spark.money/tools/stablecoin-market-cap-tracker); [Stablecoin Insider Jul 2026](https://stablecoininsider.org/stablecoin-market-report-july-2026/)

- [FACT] Trump's tariff threats against BRICS de-dollarization: 100% (Nov/Dec 2024) and an additional 10% (Jul 2025). — [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/indonesia-maintains-distance-from-brics-de-dollarisation-concept-amid-us-president-trumps-threats/)
- [MAINSTREAM] An IESE analysis frames Trump's financial strategy as "dollar diplomacy". — [IESE](https://www.iese.edu/insight/articles/trump-financial-strategy-dollar-diplomacy/?print=pdf) (not read in full)

### Inferences
- **The stablecoin channel is "digital dollarization".** Each $1 of GENIUS-compliant stablecoin creates ~$1 of demand for T-bills, cash or repo. At $2tn this would add structural *short-end* Treasury demand comparable to a large foreign central bank. But it tilts the duration mix toward bills, increasing rollover risk. It also exports dollar substitution into EMs, where retail and corporate users swap local deposits for USDT/USDC, eroding monetary sovereignty (e.g. Argentina, Turkey, Nigeria, and potentially Indonesia).
- **Gold revaluation** (marking the ~261m oz Treasury gold stock from the $42.22 statutory price to market) has been floated in policy circles [TRAINING-KNOWLEDGE, SPECULATIVE]. No action was verified this session.
- **Swap lines as a geopolitical tool** [MAINSTREAM, from training]. Access to Fed swap lines and FIMA is discretionary. Extending them to allies (and the 2025 US Treasury ESF $20bn swap with Argentina [TRAINING-KNOWLEDGE]) rewards alignment, while exclusion raises the cost of non-alignment. That reinforces a "dollar bloc".

## (e) Successor scenarios (2026–2040): probabilities, triggers, signposts

### Takeaway
No credible source found this session assigns numeric probabilities. The weightings below are the researcher's synthesis of mainstream analysis (Fed, Atlantic Council, IMF/OMFIF surveys, Pozsar, Chinn) and should be labelled as such. The modal path combines Scenario 1 and Scenario 2. The dollar stays dominant in FX, debt and payments, extended by stablecoins. Meanwhile reserves fragment toward gold and the euro, and sanctioned and China-centric corridors run on yuan/CIPS/mBridge.

### Cited Findings (anchors for the scenarios)
- [MAINSTREAM] The Fed expects the dollar to remain dominant "for the foreseeable future" absent large, lasting disruptions, with long-run risk higher. — [Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/the-international-role-of-the-u-s-dollar-2025-edition-20250718.html)
- [MAINSTREAM] Pozsar (2022) argued commodity-backed "outside money" and a multipolar order (Bretton Woods III) would follow the Russia reserve freeze. As of 2026 analysts call this "real but slow". — [Dubach 1/2](https://philippdubach.com/posts/pozsar-bretton-woods-framework/); [Dubach 2/2](https://philippdubach.com/posts/pozsars-bretton-woods-iii-three-years-later-2/2/)
- [MAINSTREAM] OMFIF 2026: 56% of reserve managers plan more diversification and 54% plan to expand reserves. — [Econbrowser](https://econbrowser.com/?p=61297)
- [FACT] Central banks keep buying gold for diversification. — [Econbrowser](https://econbrowser.com/?p=61297)
- [MAINSTREAM] Bessent and Standard Chartered see $2tn of stablecoins by 2028. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/357872/us-stablecoin-market-could-exceed-2-trillion-projection-by-end-of-2028-thinks-treasury-secretary-bessent)

### Inferences (researcher synthesis; qualitative weights, NOT sourced probabilities)

**Scenario 1 – Dollar dominance persists via "digital dollarization" (stablecoins + network effects).** Weight: high, roughly the base case for transactions and debt.
- *Mechanism:* GENIUS-regulated USD stablecoins become the default rails for EM retail, B2B and crypto settlement. Stablecoins replace foreign central banks as marginal T-bill buyers.
- *Signposts:*
  - stablecoin cap >$500bn by 2027 and >$1–2tn by 2028
  - stablecoin share of T-bill holdings
  - BIS FX share staying ≥85% in the 2028 Triennial
  - EM regulators (BI, RBI, BCB) restricting USD stablecoins
- *Timeline:*
  - 2026–28: scaling, with bank-issued tokens
  - 2028–32: stablecoins large enough to matter for T-bill pricing; EM deposit substitution provokes capital-flow-management responses
  - 2032–40: dollar transactional share stable, reserve share drifting toward ~50%

**Scenario 2 – Fragmented multipolar blocs (Bretton Woods III-lite).** Weight: high as a *partial* outcome running alongside Scenario 1.
- *Mechanism:* a dollar/ally bloc using swap lines, stablecoins and security ties; a yuan corridor (CIPS, mBridge, Hormuz-type yuan conditions, Russia, Iran, parts of Africa and ASEAN); the euro gaining reserve share; gold as neutral reserve.
- *Signposts:*
  - COFER USD <55%, EUR >22%
  - CNY in SWIFT >5%
  - CIPS direct participants and share not using SWIFT messaging
  - mBridge onboarding Gulf oil trades
  - EU joint-debt (Eurobond) issuance scaling
  - persistence of the Hormuz yuan toll after the war
- *Timeline:*
  - 2026–28: war-driven corridors institutionalize
  - 2028–2032: BRICS payment links operationalized bilaterally (no common currency)
  - 2032–40: three-pole reserve system (USD ~45–50%, EUR ~25%, gold large, CNY ~5%)

**Scenario 3 – Commodity/gold-anchored neutral settlement among the Global South.** Weight: low-moderate as a niche; low as the system core.
- *Mechanism:* gold held domestically, gold-backed settlement tokens, or the 2024 BRICS-floated "Unit" (40% gold) [TRAINING-KNOWLEDGE, SPECULATIVE]; commodity-for-commodity barter (Iran–China).
- *Signposts:*
  - central bank gold buying >1,000t/yr sustained
  - repatriation of gold from NY/London
  - any BRICS gold-settlement pilot
  - the Shanghai Gold Exchange international vaults
- *Timeline:* pilots 2027–30. Scale is limited by gold supply and the lack of a credit function.

**Scenario 4 – IMF SDR expansion.** Weight: low.
- *Mechanism:* large SDR allocations, a private SDR market and SDR-denominated bonds.
- *Constraints:* the US veto (16.5% vote share) [TRAINING-KNOWLEDGE]; no private SDR market.
- *Signposts:* a new general allocation after a crisis (as in 2009 and 2021), SDR bond issuance, and SDR invoicing.
- *Timeline:* only as a crisis response, more plausible in Scenario 5 than in normal times.

**Scenario 5 – Disorderly dollar crisis.** Weight: low but rising tail.
- *Triggers:*
  - fiscal dominance (debt ~$40tn, interest costs ballooning with 10y ~5.3%)
  - visible loss of Fed independence (forced cuts despite ~3.7% PCE)
  - a repeat of the April 2025 pattern (yields up and dollar down) at larger scale
  - taxes or haircuts on foreign Treasury holders (Mar-a-Lago-style coercion)
  - US capital controls
  - a technical default from a debt-ceiling crisis
- *Signposts:*
  - term premium/10y >6%
  - DXY and Treasuries falling together for weeks
  - failed or tailing auctions
  - foreign official Treasury holdings falling sharply
  - gold spiking
  - swap-line usage by allies
  - rating downgrades below AA
- *Timeline:* a crisis could occur any time; the most plausible window is 2027–2032. The aftermath would likely *accelerate* Scenarios 2–4 rather than install a single successor.

## (f) Effects on emerging markets: Indonesia

### Takeaway
Indonesia is the clearest example of *hedged* de-dollarization. It joined BRICS (Jan 2025) but explicitly rejects replacing the dollar. Meanwhile Bank Indonesia's LCT framework scaled sharply in 2026: about $22.6bn in Jan–Apr and $42.1bn in H1, already above all of 2025. Volume is overwhelmingly with China (~89%). For the rupiah this reduces transactional dollar dependence. It does not reduce exposure to the dollar funding cycle, Fed hikes, oil-price shocks or US tariffs.

### Inferences
- **LCT mechanics** [TRAINING-KNOWLEDGE]. BI's LCT (formerly LCS) frameworks with Malaysia, Thailand, Japan, China, Korea, the UAE and India allow direct IDR/partner-currency quotes through appointed cross-currency dealer (ACCD) banks. They reduce IDR→USD→CNY double conversion costs and the dollar liquidity needed for trade. The 2026 surge (with China at 89%) likely reflects the war-time oil and commodity shock, BI's policy push (the 2026 "diversification from USD" messaging), and Chinese supply-chain integration.
- **Rupiah implications.** LCT trims transactional dollar demand but:
  1. Indonesia's external debt and portfolio flows (SBN holdings) remain dollar-linked.
  2. The Fed's Sept 2026 hike, 10y UST at ~5.3% and oil above $80–100 pressure the rupiah through the oil-import bill and capital outflows.
  3. Concentration in CNY creates a new dependency on PBOC policy and Chinese capital controls.
  4. USD stablecoins (Scenario 1) could pull deposits away from the rupiah, a risk BI and OJK must regulate.
- **ASEAN payment connectivity.** QRIS cross-border links (Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Japan and others) and the ASEAN Regional Payment Connectivity MoU [TRAINING-KNOWLEDGE] are retail and tourist-scale. They help local-currency usage but are macro-insignificant next to commodity export invoicing (coal, palm oil and nickel are still largely dollar-invoiced).
- **Strategic positioning.** Indonesia's hedged approach (BRICS member, no de-dollarization pledge, expanding LCT, keeping US channels) maps onto Scenario 2. It is vulnerable to US coercion if tariffs or secondary sanctions are tied to BRICS payment initiatives.
