# Who Might Go to War with Europe/NATO, and When: Official and Expert Assessments (2025 to 2 Nov 2026)

Research date: 2 November 2026. Every assessment is dated. Most items come from press coverage of intelligence reports, not the primary documents. Where only a secondary source was available, that is noted.

## 1. Intelligence and military estimates: timelines and their basis

### Takeaway
Western services broadly agree that Russia is building the *capability* for a war with NATO, with a window usually given as **2028–2030** (Bundeswehr's "2029", Denmark's "within 5 years" from Feb 2025, Lithuania's "by 2030" strategy horizon). In **August–October 2026** the near-term warnings sharpened: US intelligence (Aug 2026), Denmark's DDIS (24 Sep 2026), Czech BIS, Poland's Tusk and Germany's BND (Oct 2026) all warned of a *limited* attack or a sharp escalation of hybrid attacks within "months". In contrast, the Baltic and Finnish services, normally the most hawkish, judged in their early-2026 annual reports that Russia has no intention of attacking NATO in 2026. Several of them stayed cautious in autumn 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Denmark (DDIS / Forsvarets Efterretningstjeneste)**
- **9 Feb 2025:** DDIS estimated how quickly Russia could be ready for each type of war, assuming the Ukraine war ends or freezes and Russia sees NATO as weak or divided:
  - a local war against a neighbouring country within about 6 months;
  - a regional war in the Baltic Sea region within about 2 years, posing a credible threat to one or more NATO countries;
  - a large-scale war on the European continent within about 5 years.

  The estimate also assumed Russian conventional forces keep growing faster than the West's. — [Il Foglio, 26 Sep 2026](https://www.ilfoglio.it/en/world/2026/09/26/news/the-timeline-of-a-russian-war-against-nato-according-to-danish-intelligence--408794); [SOFX](https://www.sofx.com/danish-intelligence-warns-russia-could-wage-war-on-nato-within-five-years/)
- **24 Sep 2026 update:** DDIS judges there is a "low but growing risk" that Russia could launch limited attacks on NATO members bordering Russia. These could take three forms:
  - isolated long-range strikes on infrastructure that supports Ukraine;
  - limited troop deployments, possibly without national insignia;
  - intensified hybrid attacks (destructive cyberattacks, sabotage with a high risk of casualties).

  These "could potentially take place in the coming months." A limited attack would be "a desperate move" meant to draw European resources away from Ukraine and split NATO. It would be most likely if the alliance failed to agree on a collective response. — [CNBC, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/25/russia-ukraine-war-nato-denmark-putin.html); [Euromaidan Press, 24 Sep 2026](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/09/24/russia-may-launch-limited-attacks-on-nato-denmark-warns/)

An invasion would need at least six months of visible preparation. DDIS said the three Feb 2025 timeframes "remain valid". It sees a Russian window of opportunity before about 2030, when Europe may build autonomous military capability. — [Euronews, 24 Sep 2026](https://euronews.com/2026/09/24/denmark-says-russian-invasion-of-nato-country-highly-unlikely-but-cannot-be-ruled-out); [Il Foglio](https://www.ilfoglio.it/en/world/2026/09/26/news/the-timeline-of-a-russian-war-against-nato-according-to-danish-intelligence--408794)

### Inferences
- **Two distinct timelines are being conflated in public debate.**
  - (a) *Capability for a large-scale war*: 2028–2030. This rests on the 2023 NATO assessment, DDIS Feb 2025, Bundeswehr "2029", Rutte's "within five years" and Lithuania's "by 2030". It is driven by Russian rearmament, force expansion, defence-industrial output, and the assumption that the Ukraine war ends or freezes and frees up forces.
  - (b) *A limited or hybrid attack*: "months", from autumn 2026 onward. This comes from US intelligence (Aug 2026), DDIS (Sep 2026), Czech BIS, Tusk and the BND. It is driven paradoxically by Russian *weakness* or desperation in Ukraine (energy strikes, stalled offensives), by a desire to split NATO, and by perceived NATO and US disunity.
- **The ordering has flipped.** In 2025 the main fear was that the end of the Ukraine war would free Russian forces for an attack on NATO. By autumn 2026, several services fear a limited attack *while* the war continues.
- **Frontline services softened their estimates in 2026.** The Baltic and Finnish services' early-2026 reports judged near-term intent as absent, and Lithuania lengthened its timeline. This suggests Russia's war losses and economic strain have pushed back large-scale capability. It is notable that the most exposed states were less alarmist than Washington and Berlin in autumn 2026.

- **US documents:** I found no ODNI Annual Threat Assessment 2026 text. A US 2026 National Defense Strategy reportedly describes Russia as a "persistent but manageable" threat to NATO's eastern members. I saw this only in a search-engine summary, without a verifiable URL, so it is unverified.

- **Probabilities:** No service published a numeric probability. DDIS's "low but growing" and "highly unlikely" are the closest to graded likelihoods.

## 2. Scenarios, triggers, and Russian intent and statements

### Takeaway
The most discussed scenarios are limited and deniable rather than a full invasion:
- hybrid escalation (sabotage, cyberattacks, false-flag drone strikes on Baltic infrastructure);
- an incursion by forces without insignia, or a strike on infrastructure that supports Ukraine;
- a grab of Baltic territory (Narva, or the Suwałki corridor) to test Article 5;
- nuclear signalling around Kaliningrad.

The Kremlin consistently denies any intent to attack Europe. Its statements still mix that denial with threats, including the 1 Oct 2026 nuclear warning over Kaliningrad and Putin's 30 Oct 2026 remark that Russia is "ready to fight a war with Europe, but we do not want it".

### Inferences
- **Triggers named across sources:**
  - Russian frustration in Ukraine, such as stalled offensives and Ukrainian strikes on its energy sector;
  - perceived NATO and US disunity or unwillingness to respond collectively;
  - Western support flows to Ukraine (rail, depots, Starlink);
  - Kaliningrad access and Baltic shipping;
  - the end or freeze of the Ukraine war, which would free forces (the 2025 framing).
- **Russia's likely aim:** The aim most often attributed to Russia is political (fracturing NATO, discrediting Article 5, cutting aid to Ukraine) rather than territorial conquest. That is why limited, deniable options dominate the scenarios.

## 3. Other threats to Europe: Iran war spillover, Belarus, China-Taiwan, terrorism

### Takeaway
The 2026 US–Israel war with Iran is the most important *indirect* threat multiplier found. It has drained US Patriot interceptor stocks in Europe to "beyond critical" levels, pulled European naval assets to the Gulf, and deepened NATO divisions. Belarus matters as a staging ground: Oreshnik missiles have been deployed there since Dec 2025, and the Suwałki axis runs along its border. I found no 2026 assessment framing China, Taiwan or terrorism as a direct war threat to Europe.

### Cited Findings
- **Interceptor shortage (AP, 27 Aug 2026):** US officials say the shortage of advanced missile interceptors in Europe is "beyond critical", largely because of the Iran war, as stocks in Europe were shifted to the Middle East.
  - CSIS estimates the US used about 1,500 Patriots, around 65% of its roughly 2,330 pre-war inventory.
  - Analysts warn this leaves few alternatives against Russian ballistic missiles. Officials still do not expect a Russian ballistic missile attack on NATO imminently, and say Russia could be in a position to attack by 2030.
  - The Pentagon and NATO pushed back against portrayals of Europe as defenceless.

## 4. Dissenting and sceptical views; "risk" vs "prediction"; is war imminent?

### Takeaway
No major official body predicts that a Russia–NATO war *will* happen. The consensus is that the *risk* is rising, especially of limited or hybrid attacks, while a large-scale invasion is judged "highly unlikely" in the near term (DDIS, BND, SACEUR). Sceptics argue three things:
- Russia is too depleted by Ukraine, with over 1 million casualties, an economy contracting in 2026 and forces stretched thin;
- NATO Europe out-spends Russia;
- warnings are partly instrumental, used to justify defence budgets or political agendas.

Strikingly, on 30 Oct 2026 the Pentagon itself asked European allies to tone down their rhetoric.

### Inferences
- **Risk vs prediction:** Every official estimate found is framed as capability, risk or possibility ("could", "low but growing", "cannot be ruled out"), not as a prediction. The date "2029" is a *readiness target* for NATO and Germany derived from Russian capability regeneration, not a forecast date of attack.
- **Is war imminent?** No official says a *large-scale* war is imminent. A minority of officials (US intelligence, DDIS, Czech BIS, Tusk) see a *limited* attack or a major hybrid escalation as possible within months. The frontline Baltic services, Lithuania's parliament and SACEUR are more restrained.
- **An unusual split:** The Pentagon's Oct 2026 request to "cool rhetoric", coming only months after US intelligence produced one of the starkest warnings, suggests a split within Washington between intelligence assessments and the policy line of the Colby-era Pentagon (troop review, engagement with Moscow). The real dispute among experts is less "Will Russia attack?" than whether NATO stays cohesive enough to deter a limited probe.
