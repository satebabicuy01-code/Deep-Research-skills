# Impacts of Europe's War-Preparation and Rearmament Drive (2025 to 2 November 2026)

Research date: 2 November 2026. Every item is dated where the source gives a date. Most figures come from news reports of primary sources (SIPRI, NATO, OECD, Eurobarometer). Check the primary documents before quoting exact numbers.

Important confounder: a 2026 Middle East/Iran conflict drove a global energy shock and moved defence stocks (an "Iran ceasefire deadline" was reported in April 2026). Do not attribute 2026 energy or oil-price effects (including in Indonesia) to European rearmament alone. See Q4.

## Q1. Economic impacts: spending trajectories, social budgets and austerity, debt and bonds, defence stocks, jobs, inflation

### Takeaway
European military spending is rising at its fastest pace since the early Cold War: SIPRI puts Europe at $864bn in 2025, up 14%, and NATO projects European allies plus Canada at 2.53% of GDP core and 3.93% including related spending in 2026. Germany is financing this with debt after its 2025 debt-brake reform, which pushed Bund yields up. In France and Germany, the push now coincides with austerity in education and social budgets, and that is fuelling a youth protest wave in autumn 2026. Estimates of the growth payoff vary widely (multipliers of about 0.5 to 1.9) and depend heavily on whether procurement stays in Europe.

### Cited Findings
**Spending trajectory**
- SIPRI (27 Apr 2026): European military spending in 2025 was about $864bn, up 14% on 2024 and the highest level SIPRI has ever recorded for Europe. European NATO spending grew faster than at any time since 1953. World spending reached $2.887tn (+2.9% real), the 11th consecutive annual rise, and 2.5% of global GDP, the highest share since 2009. — [Pravda.com.ua on SIPRI](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/04/27/8031979/); [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/world/article/military-spending-surges-in-europe-and-asia-pushing-world-to-levels-not-seen-in-16-years-report-says/); [StratPost](https://stratpost.com/sipri-global-military-spending-rises-in-2025/)
- SIPRI 2025: Ukraine ranked 7th globally at $84.1bn (+20%). Asia and Oceania rose 8.1% to $681bn, the largest increase since 2009. — [Aaj TV](https://english.aaj.tv/news/330457560)
- NATO estimates (7 Jul 2026, ahead of the Ankara summit): the 32 allies will spend more than $1.8tn in 2026, about 11% above an estimated $1.63tn in 2025. European allies and Canada will spend 2.53% of GDP on core defence plus 1.4% on related items, 3.93% in total. Five allies already exceed the 3.5% core target in 2026: Lithuania 5.33%, Estonia 5.11%, Latvia 4.92%, Poland 4.68% and Greece 3.65%. Denmark is at 3.49%. Slovenia is lowest at 1.61%. Germany's core spending rises from 2.22% (2025) to about 2.69% (2026). Spain stays around 2%. Czechia is reported at about 1.7% to below 2% (sources conflict). — [Reuters via Internazionale](https://www.internazionale.it/ultime-notizie-reuters/2026/07/07/five-nato-members-seen-to-spend-over-3-5-of-gdp-on-core-defence-this-year-alliance-estimates-show); [Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/world/nato-defense-spending-projected-to-top-18t-in-2026/3989627); [ICDS (9 Jul 2026)](https://icds.ee/en/defence-spending-who-is-doing-what-july-2026/)
- The Hague summit (June 2025) target is 3.5% of GDP for core defence plus 1.5% for defence-related spending by 2035. Progress on the 1.5% is not in official NATO figures. — [Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/world/nato-defense-spending-projected-to-top-18t-in-2026/3989627); [ICDS (14 Sep 2026)](https://icds.ee/en/natos-new-defence-spending-target-whos-planning-what-in-2026/)
- EU SAFE loan instrument (€150bn, first pillar of ReArm Europe, which aims to mobilise more than €800bn by 2030): it was fully subscribed by 19 member states. Poland has the largest allocation at €43.7bn and Romania the second largest at €16.7bn. Eight states, including Germany, did not apply. Disbursement was reported to begin in March 2026. — [Euronews (9 Sep 2025)](https://www.euronews.com/2025/09/09/big-step-forward-disbursement-of-eus-150-defence-loan-scheme-to-start-in-early-2026); [MNI](https://www.mnimarkets.com/articles/mni-brief-safe-loans-to-be-disbursed-from-march-2026-1760616717850); [Großwald tracker (secondary)](https://www.grosswald.org/safe-defence-loans-tracker/)

**Debt, bonds, fiscal rules**

The 10-year Bund yield jumped from about 2.5% to about 2.9% on the announcement (Mar 2025). — [InvestingLive (4 Mar 2025)](https://investinglive.com/news/germany-will-set-up-500-billion-special-defense-fund-20250304); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/markets/german-fiscal-bazooka-sends-euro-bund-yields-surging)
- German budget plans put defence outlays rising from €117.2bn in 2026 to €161.8bn in 2029. The 2026 budget was described as roughly tripling borrowing. — [Amwal Al Ghad](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=211978)
- Scope Ratings warns that repeatedly getting around fiscal rules through special funds could put Germany's debt ratio on a sustained upward path. The Bundesbank has proposed tapering defence borrowing by 2036. — [Scope Ratings](https://scoperatings.com/ratings-and-research/research/EN/178483); [Bundesbank](https://publikationen.bundesbank.de/content/972500)

**Growth, multipliers, jobs**
- Kiel Institute ("Guns and Growth"): moving from 2% to 3.5% of GDP and shifting to domestic production could raise GDP by 0.9 to 1.5% a year. The 3.5% path costs about €300bn a year. — [idw / Kiel](https://idw-online.de/en/news867360)
- IMF Working Paper 2026/053 (Mar 2026, Furceri et al.): the EU defence multiplier is about 1.3 on impact and 1.9 after two years. The multiplier in low-import-intensity countries is about three times that in high-import-intensity countries. — [IMF/RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:imf:imfwpa:2026/053); [SUERF](https://www.suerf.org/publications/suerf-policy-notes-and-briefs/macroeconomic-impacts-of-eu-defense-spending/)
- ABN AMRO puts the literature range at 0.6 to 1.0 and notes that up to 80% of recent procurement happened outside the EU, which limits domestic gains. Goldman Sachs is reported at about 0.5 (secondary report). BBVA Research finds +1.4% EU-27 output in year 1 per 1% of GDP. — [ABN AMRO](https://abnamro.com/research/en/our-research/macro-watch-can-defence-spending-revitalise-the-eurozone-economy); [Tiger Brokers (secondary)](https://www.itiger.com/news/1161337830); [BBVA Research](https://www.bbvaresearch.com/en/publicaciones/europe-buy-guns-or-buy-roses-eu-defence-spending-fiscal-multipliers/)
- Bank of Finland Bulletin (2025) also assesses whether defence spending will lift euro-area growth. — [BoF Bulletin](https://www.bofbulletin.fi/en/2025/5/will-higher-defence-spending-boost-euro-area-growth/)

**Inputs and materials (inflationary pressure in niche markets)**
- European antimony, used in ammunition primers, rose 327% year on year according to Argus because of a concentrate shortage and Chinese export controls. Prices later retreated from their July 2025 peak, to about $34/kg in Q1 2026. — [Argus Media](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2671800-eu-metal-sector-braces-for-higher-defence-spending); [Skillings](https://skillings.net/antimony-price-trend-2026-chinas-export-bans-and-the-supply-chain-siege)
- Tungsten: China controls more than 80% of supply and has applied export controls since Feb 2025. Prices hit record highs in 2026 (APT about $1,100+/MTU). Rovjok estimates the 3.5% NATO target adds about 1,500t a year of tungsten demand. — [Rembar](https://www.rembar.com/?p=3695); [Rovjok/Technologieland Hessen (Jun 2026)](https://www.technologieland-hessen.de/mm/\\mm001/260625_RohstoffRadar_Rovjok.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "guns vs. education" trade-off is now politically visible. In both France and Germany, youth protests in autumn 2026 explicitly tie education cuts and cost-of-living pressure to rising military budgets. Defence is largely shielded from fiscal rules (Germany's exemption, SAFE loans, the EU escape clause) while civilian budgets are not.
- The growth payoff depends on "buy European". With up to 80% of procurement going outside the EU, and much of it to US suppliers, the low-end multipliers of about 0.5 to 0.6 are more realistic in the short run than the Kiel and IMF upper bounds.
- Defence equities are trading on war-and-peace news. Valuations are sensitive to any Ukraine ceasefire even though backlogs are at records.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative, dated 10-year Bund or OAT yield level for Oct 2026, and no clear evidence that rearmament alone moved euro-area inflation. ECB analysis was not retrieved.
- I found no sourced estimate of defence-industry jobs created in 2025-26 (for example from ASD or Rheinmetall hiring figures).
- I could not confirm Rheinmetall's Q3 2026 results or share price as of Oct 2026.
- There is no consolidated data on how much specific European social budgets were cut "because of" defence. Most links are political claims by protesters, not budget accounting.

## Q2. Social and psychological impacts: fear surveys, preparedness and stockpiling, conscription debates, youth attitudes

### Takeaway
Large majorities of Europeans now feel threatened: 68% say their country is under threat (Special Eurobarometer, Jan 2026), 72% name nearby conflicts as a top concern, and about half in multi-country polls see a "high" risk of war with Russia. But only about a third back higher military spending, and willingness to fight, especially among the young, is low and falling. Governments are moving toward voluntary, survey-based service models (Germany, France, Belgium, Netherlands), and these are triggering youth resistance.

### Cited Findings
**Fear and threat perception**
- Special Eurobarometer on security and defence (fieldwork 3 to 28 Jan 2026, n=26,523, published 13 Feb 2026 before the Munich Security Conference): about two-thirds (68%) feel their country is under threat. One report says only 32% think military spending should be increased (verify against the factsheet). Cyprus is an outlier at 76%. — [EC Representation Cyprus (13 Feb 2026)](https://cyprus.representation.ec.europa.eu/news/europeans-perceive-growing-threat-their-security-and-trust-eu-do-more-field-according-latest-2026-02-13_en); [Yeni Şafak (14 Feb 2026)](https://en.yenisafak.com/world/eurobarometer-68-of-europeans-feel-their-country-is-under-threat-3714587); [Politis Cyprus](https://www.en.politis.com.cy/globe/globe-europe/986192/eurobarometer-76-of-cypriots-see-national-security-under-threat)
- European Parliament Eurobarometer (published 4 Feb 2026): 72% cite conflicts near the EU as a major concern and 66% cite natural disasters. Disinformation also ranks high. — [Euronews (4 Feb 2026)](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/02/04/europeans-see-wars-and-disinformation-as-top-concerns-eurobarometer-survey-finds)
- ECFR poll (fieldwork Nov 2025, 13 countries, n=16,393, published early 2026): most respondents fear a major European war, fear of a Russian attack on their own country has grown since the previous survey, and the modal expectation is that the Ukraine war will still be going on a year later. — [Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/1872499/most-europeans-see-high-risk-of-war-with-russia-according-to-major-poll); [Eurointegration (11 Feb 2026)](https://www.eurointegration.com.ua/rus/news/2026/02/11/7230930/)
- Le Grand Continent/Cluster17 poll (9 countries, Dec 2025): 51% see a "high" or "very high" risk that Russia goes to war with their country in the coming years, with majorities in Germany and France. — [Al Jazeera (4 Dec 2025)](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/12/4/most-germans-french-see-high-risk-of-war-with-russia-survey-shows); [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2766332/half-of-europeans-see-high-risk-of-war-with-russia-poll-finds)
- Note: news reports sometimes conflate the ECFR and Le Grand Continent polls. They are separate surveys.

**Preparedness and stockpiling**
- EU Preparedness Union Strategy (26 Mar 2025) sets a goal of at least 72 hours of household self-sufficiency (water, food, radio, torch, documents), plans an EU preparedness day and school lessons, and covers EU stockpiling of critical goods. Brussels framed it as practical, not a call for panic buying. — [Euronews (26 Mar 2025)](https://www.euronews.com/2025/03/26/brussels-ask-eu-citizens-to-put-together-a-72-hour-emergency-kit-to-face-crises); [Irish Times (26 Mar 2025)](https://irishtimes.com/world/europe/2025/03/26/stockpile-72-hours-of-supplies-in-case-of-disaster-or-attack-eu-tells-citizens); [Brussels Times](https://brusselstimes.com/1504722/prepare-72-hours-worth-of-emergency-supplies-in-case-of-war-eu-warns-citizens)

**Conscription and service models**
- Germany: a new military service law took effect at the start of 2026. All men turning 18 must fill in a questionnaire on fitness and interest. The law keeps open a return to compulsory service if volunteers fall short (conscription was suspended in 2011). — [Asia Economy (6 Mar 2026)](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026030608213195085); [Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/europe/german-students-walk-out-to-protest-new-military-service-law/3931736)
- German school strikes against the law: early Dec 2025, Mar 2026, 8 May 2026 (130+ cities), about 45,000 students in about 100 towns in Sept 2026, and the 31 Oct 2026 Gen Z protests (see Q1). Police counted about 3,000 in Berlin in March, while organisers claimed more. — [Eunews (2 Dec 2025)](https://www.eunews.it/en/2025/12/02/german-youth-in-the-streets-against-military-conscription-we-dont-want-to-be-cannon-fodder/); [Jacobin (Mar 2026)](https://jacobin.com/2026/03/germany-student-strikes-rearmament-conscription); [Tasnim (26 Sep 2026)](https://www.tasnimnews.ir/en/news/2026/09/26/3706347/tens-of-thousands-protest-german-conscription-plans/amp); [Rosa Luxemburg Stiftung](https://www.rosalux.de/en/news/id/54827/resisting-the-draft-in-germany)
- A poll cited by Jacobin (left-wing outlet) found 63% of 18 to 29-year-olds reject the new conscription law. — [Jacobin (Mar 2026)](https://jacobin.com/2026/03/germany-student-strikes-rearmament-conscription)
- France: Macron announced a 10-month voluntary military service on 27 Nov 2025, for ages 18 to 19 (some sources say up to 25), serving only on French territory and paid at least €800 a month. The scheme starts with 3,000 volunteers in 2026, aims for 10,000 by 2030 and 50,000 a year by 2035, and costs about €2bn. The recruitment campaign launched in Jan 2026, with the first intake in Sept 2026. About 2,200 applications were reported by Aug 2026 (single source). In a major crisis, parliament could make service mandatory. — [AJC/AP (Nov 2025)](https://www.ajc.com/news/2025/11/macron-unveils-new-french-voluntary-military-service); [The Star/Reuters (12 Jan 2026)](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/01/12/france-launches-recruitment-campaign-for-new-voluntary-military-service); [Il Sole 24 Ore](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/macron-2026-voluntary-military-service-ten-months-young-french-people-AHsCLRzD)
- Belgium: Defence Minister Theo Francken sent letters to all 17-year-olds, about 149,000 (90,800 in Dutch, 56,900 in French, 925 in German), in Nov 2025 for a voluntary service year aimed at about 500 recruits. Critics call it groundwork for conscription. Francken says Belgium is "not logistically ready" for compulsory service. Conscription is suspended, not abolished. — [La Libre (13 Nov 2025)](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/2025/11/13/149-000-lettres-envoyees-aux-jeunes-par-la-defense-a-quoi-peuvent-sattendre-les-volontaires-UFVXUR5X5FC5TLSFCWOE2YN2IA/); [Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/1835092/voluntary-military-service-letters-sent-to-all-17-year-olds-in-belgium); [Euronews (8 Apr 2025)](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/04/08/belgium-to-follow-dutch-example-and-introduce-voluntary-military-service)
- Netherlands: 17-year-olds receive a voluntary questionnaire that could later become mandatory, and the army runs a one-year "dienjaar" for 18 to 27-year-olds (2025 sources). — [Euronews (8 Apr 2025)](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/04/08/belgium-to-follow-dutch-example-and-introduce-voluntary-military-service)

**Willingness to fight**
- UK (YouGov/Public First for The Times, Feb 2025): only about 1 in 10 Gen Z adults (18 to 27) would fight for Britain, and 41% are proud to be British. — [The London Economic (Feb 2025)](https://thelondoneconomic.com/news/only-one-in-10-young-brits-would-fight-for-their-country-389702)
- Poland: willingness to take up arms has nearly halved since 2023, and only 18% of 18 to 29-year-olds are willing to fight (2026 report). Germany: 59% would not take up arms if the country were attacked. Both are secondary reports. — [Logos-Pres](https://logos-pres.md/?p=93084); [Fakti.bg](https://fakti.bg/en/world/887828-polls-young-people-in-europe-are-not-ready-to-risk-their-lives-in-the-event-of-a-russian-invasion)

### Inferences
- There is a "fear without consent" gap. Threat perception is high (about two-thirds) and majorities back a stronger EU defence role, but support for paying more (about 32%) and especially for serving personally is much lower. The gap is largest among youth, who carry both the fiscal cost (education cuts) and the potential service burden.
- Voluntary models paired with mandatory registration or questionnaires (DE, NL, BE) are the dominant approach. Protesters read them as a back door to conscription, which keeps the issue alive even though no full draft is in force.

### Gaps
- I found no hard data on survival-kit or emergency-supply sales or panic buying in 2025-26. Earlier reports were only about the EU guidance itself.
- No 2026 YouGov multi-country "would you fight" poll was found. The original ECFR country breakdowns were not retrieved.
- There are no official enrolment numbers for Germany's new service or France's first intake (Sept 2026).

## Q3. Political impacts: winners and losers, populist opposition, Russian narrative, fearmongering critiques

### Takeaway
Parties hostile to rearmament and Ukraine aid, above all Germany's AfD and BSW, are gaining. The AfD won its biggest state victory (Saxony-Anhalt, 6 Sep 2026) and jointly passed an "end Ukraine aid" initiative with BSW (14 Oct 2026). Youth anger is turning against governing leaders (Merz in Germany, Macron/Lecornu in France). The Kremlin consistently frames European rearmament as dangerous "militarisation" and Europe as a "war party", and critics such as BSW and Hungarian commentators call the preparedness rhetoric fearmongering or self-fulfilling.

### Cited Findings
- The AfD won its largest state-level victory in Saxony-Anhalt, falling just short of an absolute majority. BSW refuses a formal coalition. On 14 Oct 2026, AfD and BSW passed a "peace initiative" demanding an end to Ukraine aid. — [Reuters via Internazionale (14 Oct 2026)](https://www.internazionale.it/ultime-notizie-reuters/2026/10/14/germany-s-afd-and-leftist-bsw-call-for-end-to-help-for-ukraine); [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/?p=364363)
- AfD leader Alice Weidel has pledged to cut all military and financial aid to Ukraine if the AfD governs. One source puts the AfD first in federal polling at 29% (unverified). ## Q4. Global impacts: weapons demand, raw materials, energy and food, aid cuts, Global South and Indonesia; risk to Indonesia if war breaks out in Europe

### Takeaway
The clearest global spillover is the collapse in aid. Net ODA fell about 23% in 2025, the largest drop on record, and is projected to fall a further 6 to 7% in 2026. The OECD explicitly names defence pressure as a driver, and the UK is cutting to 0.3% of GNI by 2027 to fund defence. European demand is also tightening niche mineral markets (antimony, tungsten) and benefiting Asian arms makers, especially South Korean ones. For Indonesia, direct links run through French arms deals (Rafale, Scorpène), the newly signed IEU-CEPA, nickel trade and fiscal and oil-price vulnerability. The 2026 energy shock is mainly a Middle East story, not a European one.

### Inferences
- **Risk to Indonesia if war breaks out in Europe** (analytical inference drawn from the channels above, not from a single source):
  1. Energy and fiscal channel. Indonesia is a net oil importer with subsidised and frozen fuel prices, and each $1 rise in oil costs Rp6.7tn. A European war that spiked oil and gas prices would widen the deficit, while coal and gas exports would partly cushion it (the 2022 precedent).
  2. Food and fertiliser channel. Indonesia imports wheat and fertiliser from the Black Sea region (2022 precedent; not sourced here, see Gaps).
  3. Arms-supply channel. French production lines (Dassault, Naval Group, KNDS/Caesar) would prioritise European and Ukrainian needs, delaying Rafale and Scorpène deliveries and spares. Rupiah weakness would raise the cost of foreign-loan-funded procurement.
  4. Trade and finance channel. IEU-CEPA ratification and EU demand for nickel and EV inputs could stall in a recession. Risk-off capital flight would pressure the rupiah.
  5. Aid and diplomacy channel. European aid and Indo-Pacific security engagement (for example climate finance, JETP-type funds) would shrink further.
- Upside for Indonesia: higher commodity prices (coal, nickel, possibly tungsten and antimony) and a stronger bargaining position as Europe seeks non-Chinese critical-mineral supply.
- The aid shock already hits the Global South regardless of whether war breaks out. It is the most concrete global cost of rearmament to date.

- There are no figures on Indonesia's wheat and fertiliser import exposure to Russia and Ukraine in 2025-26.
- SIPRI arms-transfer data (Mar 2026) on European imports and exports, and its Southeast Asia share, was not retrieved.

## Q5. Is this a "security dilemma" that could raise the risk of war?

### Takeaway
Analysts are divided. Intelligence services (Estonia, Feb 2026) say Russia is not planning to attack NATO in 2026-27 but is rebuilding forces partly because it fears European rearmament, which is a textbook security-dilemma dynamic. EUISS and NATO commanders describe "years of maximum risk" around 2027-2030, before European capabilities mature (around 2035). Critics warn that fear-driven planning and misread hybrid incidents could make Europe "sleepwalk" into war. Moscow explicitly says it is factoring EU "militarisation" into its military planning.

### Cited Findings
- Estonian Foreign Intelligence (10 Feb 2026): Russia has no plan to attack a NATO state this year or next, but is rebuilding forces out of concern over European rearmament. The Kremlin worries Europe could act militarily without the US within 2 to 3 years. — [Euromaidan Press (10 Feb 2026)](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/02/10/estonian-intelligence-russia-wont-attack-nato-this-year-but-fears-european-rearmament); [Eurointegration](https://www.eurointegration.com.ua/eng/news/2026/02/10/7230858/)
- NATO generals warn of possible war by 2029, while Europe will not be ready until 2035 (Apr 2026). — [Euromaidan Press (7 Apr 2026)](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/04/07/natos-generals-warn-of-war-by-2029-europe-wont-be-ready-until-2035)
- EU Institute for Security Studies: "years of maximum risk" from Russia. A Ukraine ceasefire would not remove the risk. — [Informat.ro on EUISS](https://informat.ro/en/eu/eu-institute-for-security-studies-maximum-risk-russia-127283)
- Poland warns of "critical months ahead" (Jul 2026). — [ESD (Jul 2026)](https://euro-sd.com/2026/07/news/52472/poland-warns-of-critical-months-ahead/)
- Russian MFA (Jan 2026): it is "closely monitoring Europe's militarization" (NATO buildup, strike systems, arms to Ukraine) and will factor it into military planning. It promised a military response. — [Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/eurasia/russia-says-europe-escalating-tensions-through-militarization-will-take-additional-security-measures/3984253); [ROIC (15 Jan 2026)](https://www.roic.ai/news/russia-vows-military-response-to-european-militarization-citing-nato-buildup-as-security-threat-01-15-2026)

- A former German intelligence chief expects hybrid threats to escalate before 2029. — [Mezha](https://mezha.net/eng/news/former_german_intelligence-2/)

- Regional mirror: Andi Widjajanto applies the same security-dilemma logic to Indonesia's buildup in Southeast Asia. — [Jakarta Post (15 Aug 2025)](https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2025/08/15/how-prabowos-indonesia-can-avoid-a-regional-security-dilemma)

### Inferences
- The evidence fits a partial security dilemma: both sides cite the other's buildup to justify their own (Estonian intelligence and Russian MFA statements). The deterrence school argues that the risk comes from European weakness during the 2027-2035 capability gap, not from rearmament itself. The escalation risk analysts stress most is hybrid incidents and miscalculation, not a planned invasion.
- Fear messaging cuts both ways. It mobilises budgets, but it also fuels youth backlash and populist "warmonger" narratives (Q2, Q3). Some argue this weakens the domestic consent that deterrence depends on.

### Gaps
- No RAND, IISS or SIPRI peer-reviewed analysis explicitly testing the security-dilemma hypothesis for 2025-26 was retrieved.
- There are no quantitative war-risk estimates from reputable forecasters.
